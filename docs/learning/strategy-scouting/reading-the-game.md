## Overview

Highlight videos lie in a useful way: they show what is possible, not what
is legal, common, or worth points. Strategy starts with the current season’s
Game Manual, the official Q&A, and the field drawings. This lesson is how to
read those without drowning.

## Prerequisites

- None. This is the starting point for every scout.

## What you'll learn

- Which sections of the manual actually drive strategy.
- How Q&A changes what you thought you knew.
- How to take notes a designer can use on Monday.

## Unit 1 — Read for decisions, not completion

On Kickoff weekend, read with a highlighter for:

- **Scoring table** — every way points are awarded, and when.
- **Match play** — auto, teleop, endgame, and what robots may do in each.
- **Robot rules** — size, starting configuration, mechanisms that are
  illegal by design.
- **Violation guide** — which fouls are cheap and which erase a match.

You do not need to memorize every inspection appendix on day one. You do
need the scoring table cold.

## Unit 2 — Q&A is part of the manual

- The Q&A can legalize or kill a strategy after Kickoff. Assign someone to
  check it on a schedule.
- If two smart people disagree about a rule, look it up before you argue.
  “I saw a video” is not a citation.
- Field drawings and the arena section decide reach, height, and human-
  player geometry. CAD people need those numbers early.

## Unit 3 — Notes that travel

After the read, produce a one-page brief:

- How a match is won in one paragraph.
- The three actions that look high-value on paper.
- Open questions for Q&A or for a prototype.

Hand that brief to CAD, mechanical, and programming. Strategy that stays in
the strategy channel is a hobby.

## Steps & acceptance criteria

- [ ] Locate this season’s manual, Q&A, and field drawings from FIRST.
- [ ] Write the scoring table in your own words (or a clean copy with
      annotations).
- [ ] List three rules that would make a “cool” robot illegal.
- [ ] Produce the one-page brief and give it to another trade’s lead.
