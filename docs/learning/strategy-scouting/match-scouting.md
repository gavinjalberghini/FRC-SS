## Description

Match scouting is a measurement process. If two scouts mean different
things by "cycle," the spreadsheet is fiction. This ticket is
definitions, attention, and the habits that keep Friday's data usable on
Saturday morning.

You already have a schema and a paper sheet from
[Designing a Scouting System](../scouting-system-design/)
and capability claims from
[Pit Scouting](../pit-scouting/).
Today you fill the match sheet. The pit card said what the robot *can*
do. The match sheet says what it *did*. When they disagree, you keep
both and flag it — you do not silently "correct" the number to match the
story.

A **data dictionary** is one sentence per quantitative field: when it
increments, what does *not* count, and who resolves ties. Train on one
recorded match until two scouts produce the same numbers plus or minus
one. If you cannot get there, the field is too vague for Friday.

Watch **bumpers**, not the ball. Pick a robot before the match, not
"whoever has the piece." If you lose them, skip the cycle — do not
invent it. Mark disabled, no-show, and major fouls even when the rest of
the sheet is empty. Those rows matter more than a pretty cycle count.

TBA and the audience display are a sanity check on *final alliance
score*, not on per-robot cycles. Statbotics is not invited into the
stands. Both come back in
[Data Analysis](../data-analysis/).
Phones for the form are fine. Phones for chat during a match are how you
miss the climb.

This site does not store your sheets. Photograph them or enter them
where your team agreed in the system-design ticket.

## What you'll learn

- How to write a one-page data dictionary so "cycle" means one thing.
- How to watch one robot without chasing the game piece.
- How to catch a bad row before it hits the pick list.

## Tasks

1. **Write the data dictionary.** For every quantitative field on your
   paper sheet, write one sentence:

   - When it increments (game piece *scored and counted*, or "scoring
     burst longer than two seconds" — pick one and stay there).
   - What does *not* count (a drop, a foul-negated score, a climb that
     did not latch, fuel into an inactive hub in 2026).
   - Who resolves ties (scout lead, not an argument in the stands).

   Keep it to one page. This page is how you train. If a field needs
   three sentences, the field is wrong; go back to the schema.

2. **Scout one assigned robot on the shared match.** Open
   [2026 Einstein Finals 1](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=EjF9we707DA)
   and
   [the TBA page](https://www.thebluealliance.com/match/2026cmptx_f1m1).
   Assign yourself **one** bumper (write team number before you press
   play). Fill your paper sheet in real time — pause only if you would
   pause in the stands (you would not). Use your cycle definition, not
   TBA's fuel total. When the match ends, compare alliance score on TBA
   to your gut; do not "fix" your per-robot numbers to make the
   alliance total add up. They will not.

3. **Reconcile with a teammate.** A second scout (or a mentor) scouts
   the *same* robot on the same video with the same dictionary. Sit
   down and compare sheets. For every field that differs by more than
   one, write why (lost sight, different definition, invented a cycle).
   Update the dictionary if the definition was the problem. If the
   scout was the problem, that is training, not a quieter spreadsheet.

4. **Scout six robots across two matches without losing the bumper.**
   You need a second match. Pick one:

   - [Einstein Finals 2](https://www.thebluealliance.com/match/2026cmptx_f1m2)
     if video is posted on TBA or YouTube, or
   - any Week 1–3 2026 qualification match on
     [TBA events](https://www.thebluealliance.com/events/2026)
     that has a watch link, or
   - a mentor-chosen match from last season if 2026 video is thin.

   Watch *three* robots in match A (one at a time — rewind is legal at
   home; it is not legal in the stands, so do the first pass without
   rewind, then rewind only to check). Watch *three* different robots
   in match B. Six filled sheets. If you lose a bumper, the sheet gets
   a big "LOST SIGHT" and empty cycles — not a guess.

5. **Flag one row you do not trust.** Among the six, pick the sheet you
   would not show a captain. Write why: lost sight, robot in a pile,
   you were counting fuel and gave up, the climb was off-camera. That
   flag is quality control. In a real event the scout lead quarantines
   the row instead of averaging it into a first pick.

6. **Sanity-check against TBA, then stop.** For one match, write the
   TBA alliance totals next to your six-robot (or three-robot) notes.
   If your cycle counts *cannot* even roughly explain the winner, you
   either watched the wrong robots or your definition does not match
   the game. Fix the definition, not the official score. Skim
   [WPILib Driver Station](https://docs.wpilib.org/en/stable/docs/software/driverstation/index.html)
   so you know what "disabled" looks like from the stands (no enabled
   lights, robot dead, drive team waving).

7. **Compare one match sheet to the pit card.** If you pit-scouted 2910,
   2046, or 4414, put that card next to the Einstein sheet. One
   sentence: claim vs. field. You will do this for real teams all
   Saturday. The qualitative ticket next is how you write the sentence
   so a pick meeting can use it.

## Acceptance Criteria

- [ ] A one-page data dictionary exists for every quantitative field,
      including what does not count and who breaks ties.
- [ ] One Einstein Finals 1 sheet exists for a named robot, filled in
      something close to real time.
- [ ] A second scout's sheet on the same robot was reconciled; remaining
      differences are explained in writing.
- [ ] Six robot-sheets exist across two matches; lost-sight rows are
      marked empty, not guessed.
- [ ] One row is flagged as untrusted with a reason.
- [ ] A mentor can pick a random sheet and retell the dictionary
      definition of "cycle" from your page.

  group chat.
