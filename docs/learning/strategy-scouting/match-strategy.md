## Overview

A match plan that needs a paragraph in the alliance station will not be
heard. This lesson is a one-page format: autos, roles, endgame, and the
one adjustment you will make if you are winning or losing at 60 seconds.

## Prerequisites

- [Alliance Selection](../alliance-selection/)
- [Drive Team: In-Match Communication]({{ '/learning/drive-team/in-match-communication/' | relative_url }})

## What you'll learn

- What belongs on the page — and what does not.
- How to coordinate three autos without a 20-minute meeting.
- How the coach uses the plan during the match.

## Unit 1 — The one-pager

Every match sheet has:

- Alliance color, match number, opponent names.
- **Auto** — who goes where, who takes which piece, who stays out of
  whose path.
- **Teleop roles** — who cycles which side, who defends, who is not
  allowed to play hero ball.
- **Endgame** — who climbs / parks / stays in cycle, and the clock you
  start the endgame on.
- **Foul watch** — one rule this alliance is likely to break.

If it does not fit on one side of a sheet, it is not a plan.

## Unit 2 — Autos are a negotiation

- Three teams, three preferred autos, one field. Draw it.
- The highest-value legal combination wins, not the most impressive
  individual auto.
- Confirm in the queue. Do not discover a collision on the field.

## Unit 3 — The coach’s copy

The drive coach holds the sheet. The driver holds the robot. During the
match the coach calls **deviations** (“switch to defense,” “endgame now”),
not a second strategy lecture.

See the [Drive Team]({{ '/learning/drive-team/' | relative_url }}) track
for how those calls are phrased.

## Steps & acceptance criteria

- [ ] Fill a one-pager for a real or recorded alliance of three robots.
- [ ] Resolve an auto conflict on paper (two robots want the same piece).
- [ ] Give the sheet to a driver who was not in the meeting; they should
      be able to restate the roles in 20 seconds.
- [ ] Write the 60-second winning and losing adjustments in six words
      each.
