## Description

A match plan that needs a paragraph in the alliance station will not be
heard. This ticket is a one-page format: autos, roles, endgame, and the
one adjustment you will make if you are winning or losing at 60 seconds.

You just formed an alliance in
[Alliance Selection](../alliance-selection/)
— or you are practicing on a recorded three-team alliance. The season-
level version of this page was
[Robot Priorities](../robot-priorities/).
This is the match-level version. Same discipline: if it does not fit
on one side of a sheet, it is not a plan.

Every match sheet has alliance color, match number, opponent names,
auto (who goes where, who takes which piece, who stays out of whose
path), teleop roles (who cycles which side, who defends, who is not
allowed to play hero ball), endgame (who climbs / parks / stays in
cycle, and the clock you start the endgame on), and a foul watch (one
rule this alliance is likely to break). The drive coach holds the
sheet. The driver holds the robot. During the match the coach calls
**deviations** ("switch to defense," "endgame now"), not a second
strategy lecture. How those calls are phrased lives in
[In-Match Communication]({{ '/learning/drive-team/in-match-communication/' | relative_url }}).

Autos are a negotiation. Three teams, three preferred autos, one
field. Draw it. The highest-value *legal* combination wins, not the
most impressive individual auto. Confirm in the queue. Do not discover
a collision on the field.

This site does not store match plans. The coach's copy is paper or a
photo. You will iterate that page in
[In-Event Iteration](../in-event-iteration/)
after the match proves you wrong.

## Prerequisites

- [Alliance Selection](../alliance-selection/)
  (you know how the three robots got into the same color).
- [Drive Team: In-Match Communication]({{ '/learning/drive-team/in-match-communication/' | relative_url }})
  — at least the idea that the coach calls deviations, not essays.
- Your cycle times and foul list from
  [Scoring, Cycles & Penalties](../scoring-and-cycles/).

## What you'll learn

- What belongs on the page — and what does not.
- How to coordinate three autos without a 20-minute meeting.
- How the coach uses the plan during the match, including the 60-
  second winning and losing adjustments.

## Tasks

1. **Copy the one-pager template.** On paper, boxes for:

   - match number, color, your three teams, their three teams
   - auto diagram (even a bad sketch) plus three lines of who-goes-
     where
   - teleop roles (one line per robot)
   - endgame: who, and the clock ("start climb at 0:25")
   - foul watch: one rule (in 2026, zone-scoring and tower-contact
     rules from Section 7 are frequent)
   - 60-second adjustments: winning / losing, six words each

   If a box is empty, the plan is not done.

2. **Fill it for a real or recorded alliance of three.** Default
   alliance for this track: **Newton's Einstein Finals 1 robots** —
   868, 2910, 2046 — vs Daly 4065, 4414, 1323, match
   [2026cmptx_f1m1](https://www.thebluealliance.com/match/2026cmptx_f1m1),
   video
   [Einstein Final 1 on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=EjF9we707DA).
   You are writing the plan **as if you were Newton's coach before the
   match**, using only what a coach could have known: TBA records,
   your cards, pit claims. Then watch the match and mark where the
   real robots violated your page. That gap is normal; you will close
   it in the last ticket.

   If your mentor wants *your* team's next practice match instead, use
   that. Same boxes.

3. **Resolve an auto conflict on paper.** Invent (or use a real) clash:
   two robots want the same piece, the same start zone, or the same
   lane through the bump/trench. Draw both autos. Pick the combination
   that scores more *legal* points in 15 seconds of argument, not the
   ego win. Write the losing auto as "backup if they no-show." Confirm
   the rule in
   **Section 6.3 Setup** /
   **Section 6.4**
   so you are not drawing an illegal start.

4. **Write the 60-second adjustments in six words each.**

   - Winning: example "hold; no hero; climb on 25."
   - Losing: example "switch 2046 to defense now."

   Count the words. If you need a clause, it will not be heard. Align
   the vocabulary with
   [In-Match Communication]({{ '/learning/drive-team/in-match-communication/' | relative_url }})
   ("stick," "switch," "climb now").

5. **Hand the sheet to a driver who was not in the meeting.** They
   have 20 seconds to restate the three teleop roles and the endgame
   clock. If they cannot, the page is too dense. Cut until they can.
   This is the acceptance test that matters more than your handwriting.

6. **Foul watch from your old list.** Take one foul from the cycle
   ticket that would erase a good cycle. Put it on the page as the
   thing the coach says *before* the match ("bumpers in the zone
   before you shoot"). One rule. Not the whole of Section 7.

## Acceptance Criteria

- [ ] A one-page match plan exists for a named three-robot alliance
      (Einstein Newton vs Daly, or a mentor-approved match) with every
      box filled.
- [ ] An auto conflict is resolved on paper with a sketch and a winner.
- [ ] Winning and losing 60-second adjustments are six words each.
- [ ] A driver (or a teammate standing in) who missed the meeting
      restated roles in 20 seconds from the sheet alone.
- [ ] One foul-watch line cites a real rule, not "don't foul."
- [ ] A mentor has the page.

## Notes

- The coach's copy is the only copy that matters during the match.
  Extra strategy Slack messages in the last minute are how drivers
  miss the first auto.
- You are not allowed to add a second page "for the smart version."
  The smart version is the one they can run.
- Next — last ticket:
  [In-Event Iteration](../in-event-iteration/).
  Debrief the plan you just wrote. Change one ranking or one role
  because the field said so.
