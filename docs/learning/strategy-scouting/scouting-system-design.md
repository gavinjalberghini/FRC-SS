## Overview

A scouting system is a product used by tired people in a loud stands. If it
needs a 20-minute training or a perfect network, it will fail at the first
event. This lesson is how to choose *what* to collect and *how*, before you
fall in love with a form.

## Prerequisites

- [Robot Priorities](../robot-priorities/)

## What you'll learn

- How to pick metrics that change alliance decisions.
- Paper vs. app trade-offs.
- How to staff scouting so it does not eat the pit.

## Unit 1 — Collect only what you will use

For each proposed field, answer:

- **Which decision does this change?** First pick, second pick, defense
  assignment, auto coordination.
- **Can a trained scout record it in real time?** If not, it is a
  qualitative note, not a column.
- **Will we have it for every team?** A perfect metric on 12 teams is worse
  than a crude metric on 40.

Typical quantitative set: auto success, cycle count or scoring actions,
endgame result, major fouls, disabled. Everything else is optional.

## Unit 2 — Paper, app, or both

- **Paper** survives dead phones and venue Wi-Fi. It is slower to aggregate.
- **Apps** (Scout-a-lot, custom, TBA-backed tools) aggregate fast and fail
  when the tablet dies.
- A hybrid is common: app first, paper backup, one person entering.

Pick for the people you have, not the demo you saw on Chief Delphi.

## Unit 3 — Staffing

- Every match needs enough scouts to cover six robots without one person
  watching two.
- Pit scouting is a different shift from match scouting. Do not assign both
  to the same three people all day.
- A scout lead sits where they can see the field *and* the incoming data.

## Steps & acceptance criteria

- [ ] Write a metric list with the decision each metric supports.
- [ ] Choose paper, app, or hybrid and name the failure mode you are
      accepting.
- [ ] Draft a staffing plan for a 12-hour Friday.
- [ ] Time a teammate filling the form on a recorded match; cut fields
      that they miss.
