## Overview

Analysis is how scouting becomes a ranking. The failure mode is a 40-column
sheet nobody trusts. This lesson is a small set of views — your data, public
models, and the places they disagree — that a lead can explain in a meeting.

## Prerequisites

- [Match Scouting](../match-scouting/)
- [Qualitative & Super Scouting](../qualitative-scouting/)

## What you'll learn

- How to summarize a team in a few numbers.
- How to use TBA and Statbotics without outsourcing the pick list.
- How to handle small samples and schedule luck.

## Unit 1 — Your data first

For each team, compute a short card:

- Matches scouted (n).
- Auto success rate or auto points.
- Teleop scoring rate (cycles or points — pick one and stay consistent).
- Endgame success rate.
- Disable / no-show / card flags.

Averages hide boom-bust robots. Look at **consistency** (do they do it
every match) as well as the mean.

## Unit 2 — Public models

- **The Blue Alliance** — official results, OPRs, match video, rankings.
- **Statbotics** — EPA and related metrics that try to strip schedule
  luck.

Use them to *challenge* your list: “EPA loves this team; we have them
unscouted” is a to-do, not an automatic first pick. Public models do not
see a jammed intake in pit.

## Unit 3 — Small events and honesty

- At a 24-team event, everyone has a tiny sample. Do not overfit.
- A team that played three sandbag qual matches is not their Sunday self.
- When n is 2, qualitative and pit cards carry more weight. Say so out
  loud.

## Steps & acceptance criteria

- [ ] Build team cards for one recorded event (or a practice dataset) with
      the fields in Unit 1.
- [ ] Compare your top 8 to Statbotics or TBA OPR; explain two
      disagreements.
- [ ] Mark every team with fewer than four scouted matches so the pick
      meeting can see the uncertainty.
- [ ] Produce a one-screen view a coach can read in 30 seconds.
