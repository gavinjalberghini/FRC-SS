## Overview

Alliance selection is a live draft. Your list is a plan; the board is
reality. This lesson is the rules of the process, the social layer (who
will accept), and how to recover when the first three names are already
taken.

## Prerequisites

- [Building a Pick List](../pick-lists/)

## What you'll learn

- How the official selection process works this season.
- How to think about accept / decline.
- How to pick in 30 seconds when the plan dies.

## Unit 1 — The process

Read this season’s manual section on alliance selection. The shape is
stable:

- Captains pick in order. Teams accept or decline (rules for decline
  vary — know this year’s).
- A decline can burn a relationship and a ranking. Do not decline lightly.
- Third robots and backups matter more than pride. A functional third
  beats an impressive DNP.

Watch a recorded selection from last year as a rehearsal, not as
entertainment.

## Unit 2 — Who will accept

- Pit conversations Friday: “If we are in a position to pick you, do you
  want to play with a robot that does X?”
- Do not promise a pick you cannot make. Do not bluff a captain.
- Note teams that have already agreed with someone else. Surprises on the
  carpet are usually avoidable.

## Unit 3 — When the board changes

- Have a “if they are gone” next name for the first six picks.
- The scout lead or coach stands with the captain. One voice to the
  announcer.
- After you pick, immediately start the match-strategy conversation — do
  not celebrate so long you skip auto planning.

## Steps & acceptance criteria

- [ ] Explain this season’s accept/decline rules in your own words.
- [ ] Role-play a draft where your first two names are taken; make a pick
      in 30 seconds from the list.
- [ ] Script the one sentence the captain says at the mic.
- [ ] List three teams to talk to in the pits about a possible alliance
      *before* selection.
