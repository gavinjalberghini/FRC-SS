## Overview

The pick list you printed Friday is a hypothesis. Qualification matches are
the experiment. This capstone is the loop: debrief, update data, change the
plan, and tell the people who need to know — without relitigating the
season strategy in the pit.

## Prerequisites

- All prior Strategy & Scouting lessons.

## What you'll learn

- How to run a five-minute debrief.
- When to change a ranking or a match role.
- How to keep scouting staffed through Sunday.

## Unit 1 — Debrief while it is fresh

After each of *your* matches, before the next queue:

- What the plan was vs. what happened (auto, cycles, fouls, endgame).
- One robot or one rule that surprised you.
- One change for the next match (or “run it again”).

No speeches. The drive coach and scout lead talk; everyone else can add
one sentence.

## Unit 2 — Update the board

- Enter match scout data before you trust a “they’re so much better now”
  feeling.
- Move teams on the pick list when n grows or a mechanism appears/disappears.
- Publish a short “watch list” to the pit: teams whose story changed.

If the list never moves, you are not scouting — you are decorating.

## Unit 3 — Finish the event

- Saturday night: freeze the selection board, sleep, eat.
- After selection: new one-pagers for elim partners, new auto drawings.
- After the event: export the data and three lessons for next event. Do
  not wait for the offseason retrospective.

## Steps & acceptance criteria

- [ ] Run a five-minute debrief using the three bullets in Unit 1.
- [ ] Show one ranking change you made because of new data, with the
      rows attached.
- [ ] Hand a next-match one-pager to the drive team with at least one
      explicit change.
- [ ] Write three event-level lessons before the trailer is unpacked at
      home.
