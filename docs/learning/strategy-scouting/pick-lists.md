## Overview

A pick list is a decision document, not a popularity ranking. First picks
and second picks are different jobs. This lesson is how to produce a list
the alliance captain can read under time pressure and defend to the rest of
the leadership team.

## Prerequisites

- All Scout and Veteran Scout lessons.

## What you'll learn

- How to split first-pick and second-pick criteria.
- How to run a list meeting that ends.
- How to keep a “do not pick” list without being cruel in public.

## Unit 1 — Two lists, one sheet

- **First pick** — the partner who maximizes *your* robot. Usually a
  consistent scorer or the endgame you do not have.
- **Second pick** — the best remaining robot that fills the hole (defense,
  extra cycles, a climb, a second auto). Often a different ranking.
- **Avoid** — cards, repeated disable, or a robot that cannot play nice in
  an alliance station. Keep this short and evidence-based.

Sort by the role you need, not by a single composite score that hides the
role.

## Unit 2 — The meeting

- The scout lead presents; the room challenges with evidence, not volume.
- Time-box. A three-hour pick meeting is how you arrive at alliance
  selection exhausted and still arguing.
- Lock a draft Friday night, update Saturday, freeze a “board” version
  before you walk to the carpet.

Use the [decision-making process]({{ '/decision-making/' | relative_url }})
if the room splits. Then everyone supports the list.

## Unit 3 — Politics and courtesy

- Do not trash teams in the stands. The list is internal.
- Captain and coach agree on who speaks at the draft *before* they walk
  out.
- Be ready to explain “why not them” in one sentence of scouting, not
  “we just don’t like them.”

## Steps & acceptance criteria

- [ ] Produce a first-pick top 12 and a second-pick top 12 for a sample
      event, with a one-line reason each.
- [ ] Mark three teams avoid and cite the matches.
- [ ] Run a 30-minute mock list meeting that ends with a locked draft.
- [ ] Give the captain a one-page board they can hold in one hand.
