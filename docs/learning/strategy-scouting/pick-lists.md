## Description

A pick list is a decision document, not a popularity ranking. First picks
and second picks are different jobs. This ticket is how to produce a
list the alliance captain can read under time pressure and **defend** to
the rest of the leadership team — with a paragraph of justification, not
a vibe.

You have team cards, a coach view, and a complementarity rule from
[Data Analysis](../data-analysis/).
You have a role sentence from
[Robot Priorities](../robot-priorities/).
Sort by the role you need, not by a single composite score that hides
the role. EPA is allowed to *argue*. It is not allowed to *win
unopposed*.

**First pick** is the partner who maximizes *your* robot. Usually a
consistent scorer or the endgame you do not have. **Second pick** is
the best remaining robot that fills the hole (defense, extra cycles, a
climb, a second auto). Often a different ranking. **Avoid** is cards,
repeated disable, or a robot that cannot play nice in an alliance
station. Keep avoid short and evidence-based. Do not trash teams in the
stands. The list is internal.

The meeting: the scout lead presents; the room challenges with
evidence, not volume. Time-box. A three-hour pick meeting is how you
arrive at alliance selection exhausted and still arguing. Lock a draft
Friday night, update Saturday, freeze a "board" version before you walk
to the carpet. If the room splits, use the
[Decision-Making Guide]({{ '/decision-making/' | relative_url }}),
then everyone supports the list.

This site does not store pick lists. The captain holds paper. The next
ticket,
[Alliance Selection](../alliance-selection/),
is the live draft where this list meets the board.

## What you'll learn

- How to split first-pick and second-pick criteria so they are allowed
  to disagree.
- How to run a list meeting that ends.
- How to keep a "do not pick" list without being cruel in public — and
  how to defend "why not them" in one sentence of scouting.

## Tasks

1. **Write the two criteria, in sentences.** Before any ranking:

   - First pick: "We take the robot that \_\_\_\_ because our robot
     \_\_\_\_."
   - Second pick: "After that, we take the robot that \_\_\_\_."
   - Avoid: "We will not pick a robot that \_\_\_\_ (cite the field:
     cards, disable, station chaos)."

   If first and second criteria are the same sentence, you do not have
   a second-pick theory. Fix it.

2. **Produce two top-12 lists for a sample event.** Use the six Einstein
   teams plus enough TBA/Statbotics-backed teams from
   [Einstein 2026](https://www.thebluealliance.com/event/2026cmptx)
   or a full event your mentor names (a district event those teams
   played is better than inventing 24 robots). You will have **LOW n**
   on most of the Einstein-only set — that is fine if you mark it.

   For each name on first-pick top 12 and second-pick top 12, one
   line: team number, the role they play for *you*, and a pointer
   (match key or card field). The same team may sit in different
   places on the two lists. That is the point.

3. **Write the justification paragraph.** This is the artifact a
   mentor is looking for. One paragraph (8–12 sentences is too many;
   aim at 6–8) that a captain can read in the hallway:

   - whom you would first-pick if you are alliance 1, and why, citing
     *your* cards not only EPA
   - whom you would second-pick given that partner, and why (the hole)
   - one team you left off despite a shiny Statbotics number, and the
     match or flag that did it
   - what would make you change the first name Saturday morning

   A list without this paragraph is a spreadsheet. A paragraph without
   a list is a take. You need both.

4. **Mark three avoid teams and cite the matches.** Team number, the
   behavior (card, disable, unplayable defense, station problem),
   match key or pit note. If you do not have three real avoids in the
   sample, say so and write the *rule* you would use — do not invent
   crimes. Avoid is not "we don't like them."

5. **Run a 30-minute mock list meeting that ends.** You need two other
   humans (teammates or a mentor). Agenda:

   - 5 min: criteria from Task 1
   - 15 min: challenge the top 5 with cards and the two disagreement
     paragraphs from analysis
   - 5 min: lock a draft first-pick 8 and second-pick 8
   - 5 min: write down the dissent and close it

   Someone is timekeeper. When the timer ends, the list is the list.
   Use the decision guide if you split. Then everyone supports it —
   including the person who lost.

6. **Give the captain a one-page board.** One sheet, one hand: first-
   pick order, second-pick order, avoid, the justification paragraph
   in small type or on the back, and the "if they are gone" next name
   for the first six. This is what walks to the carpet in the next
   ticket. No laptop required.

## Acceptance Criteria

- [ ] First-pick and second-pick criteria are written as different
      sentences, plus an avoid rule.
- [ ] A first-pick top 12 and a second-pick top 12 exist, each name
      with a one-line reason.
- [ ] A ranked list is accompanied by a paragraph of justification a
      captain can read in one standing: first pick, second pick, one
      shiny-but-no, and the Saturday change condition.
- [ ] Three avoid entries exist with citations, or a written statement
      that the sample had fewer than three and the rule you would use.
- [ ] A 30-minute mock meeting happened and ended with a locked draft.
- [ ] A one-page captain board exists.
- [ ] A mentor can ask "why not team X?" and get one sentence of
      scouting, not "we just don't like them."

## Notes

- Captain and coach agree on who speaks at the draft *before* they
  walk out. That sentence is written in the next ticket; do not
  improvise it on the carpet.

  other team can hear you.

  emcee says "Alliance 1" is not a list.
