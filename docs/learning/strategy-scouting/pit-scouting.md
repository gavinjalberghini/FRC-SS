## Overview

Pit scouting answers “what *can* this robot do?” Match scouting answers
“what *did* it do?” You need both. This lesson is a respectful, fast pit
visit that produces photos and a capability card you will still believe on
Saturday.

## Prerequisites

- [Designing a Scouting System](../scouting-system-design/)

## What you'll learn

- What to look at when you have three minutes.
- How to ask questions students will answer honestly.
- How to record drivetrain, mechanisms, and autos without a novel.

## Unit 1 — Three minutes in the pit

- **Photo** of the whole robot, bumpers on, and one of the scoring
  mechanism. Ask first.
- **Drivetrain** — swerve, tank, other; relative speed.
- **Scoring** — which game pieces, which locations, intake reliability
  *as they describe it* (you will verify on the field).
- **Auto** — how many pieces / where, and whether they have more than one.
- **Endgame** — yes/no/maybe, and how long they say it takes.

Do not lecture. Do not recruit. Do not block their mechanic.

## Unit 2 — Questions that work

Prefer:

- “Where do you like to start auto?”
- “What still breaks?”
- “What do you want in a partner?”

Avoid:

- “So you’re a first pick, right?”
- Fishing for strategy they will not give a stranger.

Students usually know the robot. If only a mentor will talk, note that.

## Unit 3 — After the walk

- Enter the card the same hour. Memory merges robots.
- Flag contradictions with match data later (“said they climb; 0/8 in
  quals”).
- Thank them. You may need a spare part from this pit tomorrow.

## Steps & acceptance criteria

- [ ] Write a pit card with the fields in Unit 1, no extras that take a
      fourth minute.
- [ ] Pit-scout three robots (live or from event photos + TBA) and fill
      the cards.
- [ ] Compare one card to a match video; note one mismatch.
- [ ] Practice asking the three good questions without reading them.
