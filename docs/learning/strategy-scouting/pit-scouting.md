## Description

Pit scouting answers "what *can* this robot do?" Match scouting answers
"what *did* it do?" You need both. A robot that "has a climb" on a pit
card and 0-for-8 on the field is a fact, not a betrayal — but only if
you recorded both. This ticket is a respectful, fast pit visit that
produces photos and a capability card you will still believe on Saturday.

You designed a schema and a paper sheet in
[Designing a Scouting System](../scouting-system-design/).
The pit card is a *different* form. It is filled once per team, in three
minutes, while a mechanic is trying to work. It records drivetrain,
mechanisms, autos, and endgame *as they describe them*. You will verify
on the field in
[Match Scouting](../match-scouting/).

Do not lecture. Do not recruit. Do not block a queued team. Drive-team
event ops are covered in
[Event Operations]({{ '/learning/drive-team/event-operations/' | relative_url }});
if they are on the way to queue, you come back later. Ask before you
photograph. Students usually know the robot; if only a mentor will talk,
note that.

This website does not store pit databases. Your cards live on paper or
in a tool your team owns. For this ticket you will practice on **video
and TBA media**, not by ambushing a team at a local event unless a
mentor sets that up.

## Prerequisites

- [Designing a Scouting System](../scouting-system-design/)
  completed (schema and paper match sheet). You will make a *shorter*
  pit card from the same decisions.
- The alliance role from
  [Robot Priorities](../robot-priorities/)
  — you are looking for partners who fill your hole.

## What you'll learn

- What to look at when you have three minutes in someone else's pit.
- How to ask questions students will answer honestly, and which
  questions waste the visit.
- How to record drivetrain, mechanisms, and autos without writing a
  novel — then catch the lie against match video.

## Tasks

1. **Write the pit card (no fourth minute).** One side, fields only:

   - team number, nickname if they say it
   - photo boxes: whole robot bumpers-on, scoring mechanism (ask first)
   - drivetrain: swerve / tank / other, and a relative-speed check
     (slow / mid / fast — your eye, not their brochure)
   - scoring: which pieces, which locations, intake reliability *as
     they describe it*
   - auto: how many pieces or actions, where they like to start, more
     than one auto? yes/no
   - endgame: yes / no / maybe, and how long they *say* it takes
   - "what still breaks?"
   - "what do you want in a partner?"
   - scout name, time of visit

   No extras that take a fourth minute. If it is not on this list, it
   belongs on the match sheet or in qualitative notes.

2. **Learn the three questions that work.** Practice out loud, without
   reading:

   - "Where do you like to start auto?"
   - "What still breaks?"
   - "What do you want in a partner?"

   Avoid "So you're a first pick, right?" and fishing for a strategy
   they will not give a stranger. Record yourself once or say them to a
   teammate. The point is you can ask them while looking at the robot,
   not at a script.

3. **Pit-scout three robots from practice video and TBA media.** You
   are not walking a venue for this ticket unless a mentor arranges it.
   Fill three cards from public sources. Use this set unless your mentor
   substitutes a local event:

   - [Team 2910 (2026)](https://www.thebluealliance.com/team/2910/2026)
     — photos, events, and any robot media TBA has
   - [Team 2046 (2026)](https://www.thebluealliance.com/team/2046/2026)
   - [Team 4414 (2026)](https://www.thebluealliance.com/team/4414/2026)

   For "interview" practice, treat an Open Alliance or reveal video as
   the pit conversation. Search YouTube for that team's 2026 robot
   reveal or pit tour, or use a build-thread walkthrough such as
   [1710's Open Alliance thread](https://www.chiefdelphi.com/t/frc-team-1710-2026-build-thread-open-alliance/507939)
   for *one* of the three if a reveal is missing. Write what *they
   claim* in the same fields. If you cannot hear a claim, write
   "unknown — no interview source" instead of inventing a climb.

4. **Compare one card to match video.** Take one of the three cards and
   watch that team in
   [Einstein Finals 1](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=EjF9we707DA)
   (2910, 2046, and 4414 all play) or in a qualification match from
   their TBA event page. Note **one mismatch**: said they climb / did
   not; said two autos / ran one; said reliable intake / dumped a
   cycle. Write the match key (for example
   `2026cmptx_f1m1`) next to the mismatch. This is the habit you will
   live in on Saturday morning.

5. **Enter the cards the same hour.** Type or photograph the three
   cards into wherever your team will keep pit data (a sheet, a folder,
   a notebook). Memory merges robots. If your team has no store yet,
   a dated folder of photos plus a table is enough. This website is
   not that folder.

6. **Etiquette pass.** Write five lines you will not do in a real pit:
   blocking a mechanic, touching the robot without asking, filming a
   strategy whiteboard, staying through a queue call, arguing about
   their first-pick chances. Read the
   [drive-team event-ops lesson]({{ '/learning/drive-team/event-operations/' | relative_url }})
   far enough to know when a team is no longer available.

## Acceptance Criteria

- [ ] A pit card exists with the Task 1 fields and no extras that force
      a fourth minute.
- [ ] You can ask the three good questions without reading them.
- [ ] Three filled cards exist from TBA media and/or practice video,
      with sources cited (TBA URL, YouTube URL, or CD thread).
- [ ] One card is compared to a named match; one mismatch is written
      with the match key.
- [ ] Cards were entered into a store your team owns the same day they
      were filled.
- [ ] A mentor has looked at one card and the mismatch note.

## Notes

- Thank them. You may need a spare part from this pit tomorrow.
- "As they describe it" is not the same as "as it is." The mismatch
  note is the product, not the compliment.
- Do not promise a pick you cannot make. Pit scouting is not alliance
  selection;
  [Alliance Selection](../alliance-selection/)
  is where accept/decline gets real.
- Next:
  [Match Scouting](../match-scouting/).
  Same robots, different question: what did they *do* when the clock
  was running.
