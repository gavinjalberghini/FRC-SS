## Description

Everything the mechanical team does starts here. A bandsaw blade, a drill-press
chuck, and an angle-grinder wheel all move fast enough to cause permanent
injury in a fraction of a second. This ticket is the gate: **no one uses a
machine until a mentor has signed off shop-safety training.** Later tickets
will assume you have that sign-off. If you do not, stop and finish this one
first.

Personal protective equipment (PPE) is not a costume you put on for the photo.
**Safety glasses** exist because chips, broken bits, and cut ends leave the
work at high speed. FIRST and OSHA both treat flying particles as an eye
hazard, not an inconvenience. Everyone in the shop wears ANSI-rated glasses —
not just the person running the tool — because a chip does not care who
started the machine. Look for the **Z87** or **Z87+** mark on the lens or
temple. Fashion glasses and a phone camera are not a substitute.

**Gloves are banned on rotating tools.** That rule feels backwards the first
time you hear it, so here is why. A drill-press chuck, a bandsaw blade, a
lathe, or a spinning bit will grab fabric. A glove is a loop of strong cloth
wrapped around your fingers. Once it catches, the tool winds it in and your
hand follows. The motor does not decide to stop. FIRST's safety manual says
the same thing in writing: do not wear gloves at a band saw or drill press.
Tie back hair, tuck hoodie strings, take off rings and watches, and roll
sleeves. Anything that can wrap, will. Gloves *are* useful for carrying
sharp stock and for handling a part *after* the blade has stopped. Know the
difference.

Closed-toe shoes stay on. Hearing protection goes on for the chop saw and the
grinder — those tools are loud enough to damage hearing in a short session.
One operator owns each machine. Do not startle someone mid-cut. Clean chips
as you go so the next person does not slip on them. Know the off switch on
every tool and the shop's main cutoff *before* you need them. When something
goes wrong, **stop the tool first**, then look, then tell a mentor. A
near-miss counts. A broken guard counts.

This website does not track whether you finished. Shop sign-off lives with
the team — a paper card, a spreadsheet, or a GitHub issue the team exported
— not on this page. A mentor accepts the criteria below in person.

The next ticket, [Measurement & Layout](../measurement-layout/), is where you
learn to read a tape and mark a cut. You still do not run a saw.

## Prerequisites

- None. This is the first ticket for every mechanical team member.

## What you'll learn

- Which PPE the shop requires, and why glasses are for everyone in the room.
- Why gloves, hair, and hoodie strings are banned at rotating tools.
- How to walk a shop: off switches, first-aid kit, extinguisher, and the
  habit of stopping before you guess.

## Tasks

1. **Read the official safety baseline.** Open the
   [FIRST Safety Manual](https://www.firstinspires.org/hubfs/web/program/all/safety-manual.pdf?hsLang=en)
   and read the PPE section and the workspace / shop-equipment section. FIRST
   is not writing a suggestion. Write three notes you will show a mentor:
   what eyewear is acceptable, what footwear is required, and the sentence
   about gloves on shop equipment. Skim the
   [FIRST Safety page](https://www.firstinspires.org/resources/library/safety)
   so you know where the UL Safety Learning Portal lives if your team uses
   it.

2. **Learn why the glasses rule exists.** Read OSHA's
   [eye and face protection standard (1910.133)](https://www.osha.gov/laws-regs/regulations/standardnumber/1910/1910.133)
   far enough to see the flying-particles language, then skim the
   [Eye and Face Protection](https://www.osha.gov/eye-face-protection)
   topic page. Find a pair of shop glasses and point to the **Z87** mark.
   If you cannot find the mark, those glasses do not count.

3. **Walk the shop with a mentor or safety captain.** Do this in person, not
   from memory. On a scrap card or in a notebook, write the location of:

   - the PPE station (glasses, hearing protection, face shields)
   - the first-aid kit
   - the fire extinguisher
   - the shop's main power cutoff
   - the off switch on the bandsaw, drill press, chop/miter saw, and belt
     sander (skip any machine your shop does not have; do not invent it)

   Stand at each machine and find the off switch *before* anyone turns it
   on. You are not operating anything on this ticket.

4. **Read the machine-guarding idea.** Skim OSHA
   [1910.212](https://www.osha.gov/laws-regs/regulations/standardnumber/1910/1910.212)
   or the
   [Machine Guarding eTool](https://www.osha.gov/etools/machine-guarding).
   You do not need to quote the regulation. You need to be able to point at
   a guard on one shop machine and say what body part it is keeping out of
   the blade or chuck. If a guard is missing, tell a mentor. Do not run the
   machine.

5. **Demonstrate PPE at the door.** Put on the shop's required eye
   protection. Tie back long hair. Remove hoodie strings, rings, watches,
   and dangling jewelry, or show how you secure them. Show closed-toe
   shoes. Say out loud, to a mentor, when you *would* put gloves on (moving
   cut tube, handling a cooled part) and when you would take them off
   (anything that spins, including a cordless drill with a grabbing bit).

6. **Earn the team's shop-safety sign-off.** A mentor initials whatever
   record the team actually uses. If the team exported these tickets, paste
   a note on the issue that a mentor signed you off in the shop — the
   website still will not know.

## Acceptance Criteria

- [ ] You showed a mentor three notes from the FIRST Safety Manual: eyewear,
      footwear, and the glove rule for shop equipment.
- [ ] You pointed to a Z87 (or Z87+) mark on a real pair of shop glasses.
- [ ] A written shop-walk card lists the PPE station, first-aid kit, fire
      extinguisher, main cutoff, and the off switch on each machine the shop
      actually has. A mentor walked it with you.
- [ ] You pointed at one machine guard and stated what it keeps out of the
      danger zone.
- [ ] You demonstrated shop-ready PPE at the door and explained the glove
      rule in your own words, including *why* a rotating tool plus a glove
      is worse than bare skin.
- [ ] A mentor signed the team's shop-safety record. That record is not
      this website.

## Notes

- If you are unsure whether a tool is "rotating enough" for the glove
  rule, take the gloves off. Cordless drills and impact drivers count.
- Prescription glasses still need side protection that meets the shop
  rule. Ask a mentor before you assume yours qualify.
- A part can be remade. A finger cannot. Stop and ask.
- The next ticket is [Measurement & Layout](../measurement-layout/). You
  will mark stock. You will not cut it until later tickets, and only after
  this sign-off.
