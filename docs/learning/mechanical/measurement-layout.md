## Description

"Measure twice, cut once" is the oldest shop rule because aluminum tube is
expensive and a short part cannot be stretched. This ticket is where you
learn to *see* a length, not guess it. You already finished
[Shop Safety & PPE](../shop-safety/). You still do not run a saw. You mark
scrap, read numbers out loud, and hand the marked piece to a mentor.

A tape measure is a fraction tool. The big printed numbers are inches. The
lines between them get shorter as the fraction gets smaller:

- longest in-between mark = **1/2**
- next = **1/4** and **3/4**
- next = **1/8**, **3/8**, **5/8**, **7/8**
- shortest marks on a typical tape = **1/16**

**How to read 1/32 in.** Count the spaces between two inch marks. Sixteen
spaces means each tick is 1/16 in. Thirty-two spaces means the tape is
marked in 1/32 in — those extra ticks are usually only on the first foot,
and they are the shortest lines on the blade. On a 1/16 in tape, 1/32 in is
the *gap between* two ticks. Shop shorthand: 13/32 in is "7/16 minus," and
11/32 in is "5/16 plus." You do not need poetry. You need to land a mark
on the right side of a 1/16 in line when the drawing says 2-5/32 in.

The hook on the end of the tape **floats on purpose**. It moves by about
the thickness of the hook so a pull measurement (hooked on an outside
edge) and a push measurement (hook pressed against an inside corner) both
read zero at the work. Do not tape it tight. Do not "fix" it with a
hammer. If the hook is bent, retire the tape.

Drawings and vendor parts live in **decimal inches**. A 1/2 in hex shaft
is 0.500 in. A #10 clearance hole is about 0.196 in. That is a caliper
job, not a tape job. **Outside jaws** measure a tube or a shaft.
**Inside jaws** measure a bore or a slot. The **depth rod** measures how
deep a hole or pocket is. Close the jaws, wipe them, and zero the tool
before every session. Light, square contact — if you crank the thumbwheel
you flex the beam and lie to yourself.

Memorize the conversions you will use every week:

| Fraction | Decimal |
| --- | --- |
| 1/32 | 0.03125 |
| 1/16 | 0.0625 |
| 1/8 | 0.125 |
| 3/16 | 0.1875 |
| 1/4 | 0.250 |
| 3/8 | 0.375 |
| 1/2 | 0.500 |

Any other fraction is divide-the-top-by-the-bottom (5 ÷ 16 = 0.3125).

Layout is how a measurement becomes a part. A **combination square** puts
a line 90° to an edge and holds a repeatable distance from that edge. Mark
with a sharp pencil, a scribe, or soapstone — a fat marker line is already
wrong by 1/32 in. Cut later to the **waste side** of the line. When you
lay out more than one part from one stick, leave room for **kerf**, the
width of material the blade removes. Hole centers are two lines that
cross, then a **center punch** so the drill cannot wander. Punching and
drilling are later tickets. Marking them is this one.

This site does not store your marked tube. The mentor looks at the piece
in the shop.

## Prerequisites

- [Shop Safety & PPE](../shop-safety/) signed off. You are not using a
  machine on this ticket.

## What you'll learn

- How to read a tape to 1/16 in, and how to find 1/32 in on the blade.
- How to zero and read calipers on an outside, an inside, and a depth.
- How to convert the fractions an FRC drawing actually uses.
- How to square a line and mark hole centers on scrap tube.

## Tasks

1. **Watch someone read a tape out loud.** Watch
   [How to Read a Tape Measure — REALLY EASY](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=DqKIVmu6grM)
   (about eight minutes). Pause when they hit sixteenths. On a real tape,
   point to 1/2, 1/4, 1/8, and 1/16 between the 3 in and 4 in marks. Then
   find 1/32 in: either the extra-short ticks on the first foot, or the
   halfway point between two 1/16 in ticks. Skim
   [Bob Vila: How to Use a Tape Measure](https://www.bobvila.com/articles/how-to-use-a-tape-measure/)
   for the floating hook (the tang) if that still feels like a defect.

2. **Read lengths a mentor calls.** A mentor (or a veteran student)
   points at five places on a locked tape or a marked stick. You say the
   reading to the nearest 1/16 in, and you say whether the next 1/32 in
   is plus or minus of that mark. Write the five readings on a card.
   Disagreeing by a full 1/16 in means do the set again.

3. **Learn the caliper.** Watch
   [How to Use Digital Calipers to Measure Objects](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=oOZjbbe6YZk)
   or the Starrett overview
   [How to use a caliper](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=itH7nwjixNs).
   Then, on shop calipers:

   - wipe the jaws, close them, and zero
   - measure the **outside** of a piece of box tube or a shaft
   - measure the **inside** of a hole or a tube ID
   - measure a **depth** (a hole, a step, or the wall pocket of a tube)

   Write the three readings in decimal inches. Haas also has a short
   [measuring-tool basics](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=1tpyf5o9H20)
   if you want the "don't crank on it" lecture from a machinist.

4. **Convert without the chart.** Close this page. Convert 5/16, 7/8, and
   11/16 to decimals, and convert 0.375, 0.1875, and 0.0625 back to
   fractions. A mentor checks the six answers. Use divide-the-top if you
   stall — do not open a phone calculator for the first pass.

5. **Lay out a practice piece.** Take a scrap of 1×1 or 2×1 tube (or a
   flat bar if that is what the shop will spare). Using a combination
   square and a sharp pencil or scribe:

   - mark a square cut line at a length a mentor names (for example
     4-3/8 in), and write **WASTE** on the leftover side
   - mark a second cut line that leaves kerf room if two keepers will
     come from the same stick — ask what kerf your bandsaw actually
     removes (often about 1/16 in)
   - draw two crossing centerlines for a hole, 1/2 in from an edge and
     1 in from the end, and **center-punch** the intersection if a mentor
     is willing to watch a punch. If not, leave the cross and punch in
     [Drilling & Tapping](../drilling-tapping/)

   Write your name and the called length on the tube with a marker. This
   piece travels with you. You will cut it in
   [Cutting Materials](../cutting-materials/).

6. **Hand the piece to a mentor.** Show the tape-reading card, the three
   caliper readings, the six conversions, and the marked scrap. If the
   team exported tickets, note on the issue that the mentor saw the tube
   — do not upload a photo of the shop unless the team asks.

## Acceptance Criteria

- [ ] You pointed to 1/2, 1/4, 1/8, 1/16, and a 1/32 in location on a
      real tape, and you explained the floating hook without calling it
      broken.
- [ ] A card lists five mentor-called tape readings, each within 1/16 in
      of what the mentor read.
- [ ] Three caliper readings exist in decimal inches: outside, inside,
      and depth, taken after a zero.
- [ ] Six fraction ↔ decimal conversions were written without a chart
      and checked by a mentor.
- [ ] A named scrap of tube (or bar) has a square cut line, a waste-side
      label, kerf accounted for if two parts share the stick, and a
      crossed hole center. A mentor looked at it.
- [ ] You did not operate a saw or a drill press on this ticket.

## Notes

- A drawing that says `2.000 ± 0.005` is a caliper (or better) job. A
  tape is the wrong tool, and "about two inches" is not a tolerance.
- Do not drop calipers. Do not use them as a clamp or a scribe. A nicked
  jaw reads wrong forever.
- The next ticket, [Fasteners & Hardware](../fasteners-hardware/), is
  why those hole centers exist: a #10-32 and a 1/4-20 are different
  animals, and you need to tell them apart before you drill.
