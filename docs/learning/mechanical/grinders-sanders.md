## Description

You can cut and drill a part and still hand someone a knife edge. This
ticket is how you take material off *on purpose*: square an end, knock
a burr, open a slot a hair. Abrasive tools remove metal fast and throw
sparks and dust. That is why they come after
[Cutting Materials](../cutting-materials/) and after
[Shop Safety & PPE](../shop-safety/) is signed off. No sign-off, no
wheel.

**Gloves off** at anything that can grab (sander disc, rotary burr,
unprotected wheel). Face shield **over** glasses on the angle grinder.
Hearing protection on the grinder. Sparks are a fire problem around
lithium batteries, cardboard, and rags — clear the area and know the
extinguisher before you start. OSHA treats abrasive wheels as their
own machine family ([1910.215](https://www.osha.gov/laws-regs/regulations/standardnumber/1910/1910.215)):
guard on, wheel rated for the RPM of the tool, no cracked wheel.

The **belt/disc sander** is the shop's everyday finisher. Work on the
**down-running** side of the disc so the abrasive pushes the part into
the table, not up at your face. Use the table or fence. Light pressure
— leaning on aluminum loads the belt with metal and burns the part.
Fingers stay back from the belt tracking. This is the tool that makes
a bandsaw end look like you meant it.

The **angle grinder** is a handheld motor with a cutoff or grinding
wheel. Two hands, firm stance, work in a vise. Let the wheel reach
full speed before it touches. **Never grind on the side of a cutoff
wheel** — those wheels are not built for side load and they shatter.
Point the spark stream away from people, batteries, and the polycarb
rack. If the shop's rule is "students do not use the grinder until a
lead says so," that rule wins.

A **rotary tool** (Dremel-style) takes cutoff discs, sanding drums,
and burrs for a slot, a broken-bolt head, or a burr inside a tube.
Cutoff discs are glass-fragile. The bit will grab a loose part and
throw it. Clamp. Light touch. Glasses stay on — the debris is fine
and it finds eyeballs.

Pick the smallest tool that does the job:

- edge or hole burr → deburring tool, file, or a kiss on the sander
- square / smooth a cut → belt/disc sander
- bulk removal or a stuck rivet head → grinder, mentor present
- tight feature → rotary tool

You are not sculpting a gearbox plate down to a bearing bore on a
belt sander. That is how undersize holes happen. Measure twice after
you sand. A sander will also eat a punched #10 hole into an oval if
you "just touch up" the face — keep holes away from the belt.

Aluminum loads a belt that was meant for steel. Ask which sander is
for aluminum and stay on it. Mixing metals on one belt is how you
get a glazed, useless abrasive and a brown smear on the next part.

## Prerequisites

- [Shop Safety & PPE](../shop-safety/) signed off. No sign-off, no
  abrasive tool.
- [Cutting Materials](../cutting-materials/) completed — you need a
  cut end to finish.

## What you'll learn

- How to square a cut on the belt/disc sander using the table.
- The grinder rules that keep a wheel from coming apart at your
  knees.
- Which finishing tool to reach for, in this shop, for a given job.

## Tasks

1. **Read the abrasive-wheel rules.** Skim OSHA
   [1910.215](https://www.osha.gov/laws-regs/regulations/standardnumber/1910/1910.215)
   far enough to see guards, RPM, and the idea of inspecting a wheel
   before it spins. Then read the NIOSH
   [abrasive wheel checklist](https://www.cdc.gov/niosh/docs/2004-101/chklists/n49mac~1.htm)
   as a shop-walk list, not as homework. On this shop's grinder (or
   bench grinder), point to the guard and the unplug / power switch
   with a mentor. If the guard is off, you do not run it.

2. **Square a cut on the sander.** Take the keeper from
   [Cutting Materials](../cutting-materials/) or a new scrap end.
   Glasses on, gloves off, down-running side, table supporting the
   part. Kiss the end flat. Check with a combination square. Deburr
   the edges you just made sharp. Write "SANDER" and your name on
   the part.

3. **Say the grinder rules out loud.** To a mentor, without looking
   at this page:

   - two hands, work secured
   - full speed before contact
   - no side-loading a cutoff wheel
   - spark stream aimed away from people and batteries
   - face shield over glasses, hearing protection
   - stop and wait before you set the tool down

   If the shop allows a student grinder demo, a mentor may let you
   grind a *scrap* tab under those rules. If the shop does not, the
   spoken rules plus watching a mentor do one cut still count —
   write which path you took.

4. **Rotary tool on a tight feature.** Clamp scrap. Use a sanding
   drum or a small burr to break an inside edge or open a slot a
   mentor marks. Light pressure. Disc stays intact. Show the feature.

5. **Pick the tool.** A mentor names three jobs (burr on a hole,
   1/32 in off a tube end, cutoff a leftover bolt flush). You name
   the tool and the PPE for each, then do the one the mentor picks
   if it is safe on scrap.

## Acceptance Criteria

- [ ] You pointed to the guard and power on the shop's grinder or
      bench grinder after reading 1910.215 / the NIOSH list.
- [ ] A named part has a sander-squared end that a square accepts,
      marked SANDER.
- [ ] You recited the grinder rules to a mentor. If students may
      grind, you did one scrap pass under those rules; if not, you
      watched one and wrote that the shop holds the trigger.
- [ ] A clamped rotary-tool feature exists on scrap.
- [ ] You assigned the right finishing tool and PPE for three named
      jobs.

## Notes

- Aluminum dust on a steel-grinding belt is a loaded belt. Ask which
  sander is for aluminum. Do not mix.
- Never grind near a battery cart. Move the work or move the
  batteries. Sparks plus a charged pack is a fire you will not talk
  your way out of.
- A cutoff wheel that has been dropped gets inspected or thrown
  away. Hairline cracks fail at speed.
