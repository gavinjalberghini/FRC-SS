## Description

The cordless drill and the impact driver are the two tools you will pick
up more than any other. They look like siblings and they are not. Using
the impact on a #10-32 into aluminum is how you strip a head in one
trigger pull. Using the drill's clutch on a stuck bumper bolt is how you
stand there forever. This ticket is the difference, the controls, and
one clean hole plus one flush screw in scrap.

[Shop Safety & PPE](../shop-safety/) is required. Glasses on. Hair and
hoodie strings secured. **Gloves off.** A grabbing bit will wrap a glove
the same way a drill-press chuck will. Clamp the work. Do not hand-hold
a small piece of tube while you drill it — that is how a part becomes a
spinning blade. You already practiced that clamp habit in
[Hand Tools](../hand-tools/).

A **drill/driver** has a **keyless chuck** that closes on a round
(or hex) shank, and a numbered **clutch collar**. The clutch slips when
it hits a torque you chose, so a small screw stops before the head sinks
through polycarb. The drill icon on the collar locks the clutch out for
actual drilling. Speed gears: **low gear** is more torque (driving,
large bits); **high gear** is more speed (small bits in aluminum).
Forward drives and drills; reverse backs a screw out.

An **impact driver** has a **1/4 in hex collet**, not a chuck. It
hammers in rotation so a long lag screw or a stubborn bolt moves. That
hammer is why it feels small and hits hard. It is the right tool for
driving a lot of structural screws *once you have feel*, and the wrong
tool for plastic, tapped aluminum, or anything you care about leaving
pretty. If you are new, default to the drill and a low clutch.

Bits are not interchangeable by vibes. A twist bit sized for a **#10
clearance** (~0.196 in, often a #9 or 13/64) is not the **#21 tap
drill** you will use for #10-32 threads in
[Drilling & Tapping](../drilling-tapping/). Driver bits must match the
hex: 5/32 in in a #10-32 SHCS, fully seated. A worn rounded bit is
trash — throwing it away is cheaper than a ruined screw in a bellypan.

Technique: center-punch first so the bit does not walk. Start slow,
then bring speed up. Keep the bit perpendicular. In aluminum, a drop of
cutting fluid and a peck (back out to clear chips) keep the bit cutting
instead of polishing. When driving, start the clutch low and sneak up
until the head sits flush. If the clutch never slips and the screw is
still turning, you are about to strip.

A walking bit is almost always a skipped punch or a full-speed start.
Stop, punch, start slower. If the hole is already oval, do not "fix
it with a bigger bit" on a robot part — that is how a #10 becomes a
slot. Scrap is for learning; the bellypan is not.

You will drill a practice hole in scrap on this ticket. You will not
use the drill press yet. That machine is its own ticket.

## Prerequisites

- [Shop Safety & PPE](../shop-safety/) signed off. No shop-safety
  sign-off, no trigger.
- [Hand Tools](../hand-tools/) completed.

## What you'll learn

- When the drill/driver is the tool and when the impact is the tool.
- How chuck, clutch, speed gear, and direction actually change the job.
- How to drill one perpendicular hole and drive one screw flush without
  stripping.

## Tasks

1. **Learn the two tools from someone who uses both.** Watch
   [Drill vs Impact Driver… What's the Difference](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=GtU_Q7vGXX4)
   for the short version, then have a mentor put both tools in your
   hands **with the battery out**. Point to: chuck vs hex collet,
   clutch collar vs none, forward/reverse, and the speed selector.
   Say which tool you would use to (a) drill a #10 clearance in tube
   and (b) run twenty bumper bolts. Write those two choices down.

2. **Seat bits like you mean it.** With the battery still out or the
   tool on a bench:

   - open the drill chuck, insert a twist bit to the flats (not just
     the tip), center it, and tighten until a tug does not spin the
     bit in the chuck
   - click a hex driver bit fully into the impact collet (or the
     drill if you are driving with the drill)

   A bit that sticks out "because it is easier to see" is a bent bit
   waiting to happen.

3. **Set the controls for a real task.** Battery in. Clutch on a low
   number. Low gear. Forward. A mentor names a #10-32 button head into
   a nylock through scrap polycarb or thin plate. You set the tool
   *before* you pull the trigger. Then raise the clutch one click at a
   time until the head sits flush and stops. If you blow through the
   plastic, that piece is the lesson — do it again on a new hole.

4. **Drill one hole in clamped aluminum.** Center-punch a mark on
   scrap tube or plate (your measurement piece is fair game if a
   mentor agrees). Clamp it. Glasses on, gloves off. Drill a #10
   clearance hole, perpendicular, with pecking and a drop of fluid if
   the shop uses it. Deburr both sides with the tool you learned in
   [Hand Tools](../hand-tools/). The hole should pass a #10 screw
   freely and not pass a 1/4-20 by accident.

5. **Drive the matching screw.** Through that hole, into a nylock or
   a tapped block, drive a #10-32 so the head sits flush. No rounded
   hex. Show the joint to a mentor. Mark the scrap with your name —
   you will tap a *different* hole on the drill press later.

## Acceptance Criteria

- [ ] You pointed to chuck, clutch, gears, and collet on the real tools
      and wrote which tool drills a #10 hole and which is for a pile of
      bumper bolts.
- [ ] A twist bit and a hex bit were seated so they do not slip.
- [ ] You set clutch, gear, and direction *before* driving, and a
      #10-32 sits flush without a stripped head or a blown hole.
- [ ] A named scrap has one punched, clamped, deburred #10 clearance
      hole that a #10 screw passes and a 1/4-20 does not.
- [ ] Gloves were off and the work was clamped. A mentor was present
      for the first powered hole.

## Notes

- If the bit walks, you skipped the punch or you started at full
  speed. Stop. Punch. Start slower.
- Impact bits are harder and shorter. Do not use a cheap screwdriver
  bit in an impact — it shatters.
- A clutch number is not a torque spec. It is a relative click on
  *this* tool and *this* battery. Start low every time you change
  fastener size or material.
- The next ticket, [Cutting Materials](../cutting-materials/), is the
  first time you are allowed on the bandsaw and chop saw. Shop-safety
  sign-off is still required, and a mentor stands there for your first
  cuts.
