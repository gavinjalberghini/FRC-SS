## Description

Most FRC parts meet at a hole. Either the bolt **passes through**
(clearance) or it **screws into** the part (tapped). Getting those two
confused is how you chase a #10-32 into a hole that is already too
big, or snap a tap in a hole that was never drilled for it. This
ticket is the drill press and a clean #10-32 thread in scrap.

[Shop Safety & PPE](../shop-safety/) must be signed off. Glasses on.
**Gloves off.** A drill press that grabs an unclamped part will spin
that part — people call it a helicopter — and a glove makes it worse.
You already center-punched and drilled one handheld hole in
[Drills & Drivers](../drills-drivers/). The press is what you use when
the hole has to be straight. You already know #10-32 versus 1/4-20
from [Fasteners & Hardware](../fasteners-hardware/).

The drill press holds the bit perpendicular and lets you set depth.
Clamp the work to the table or in a drill vise. Line the punch mark
up with the bit *before* you lock the vise. Speed: **large bits and
metal run slower**; small bits can run faster. Ask a mentor what belt
or RPM this press should use for a ~3/16 in bit in aluminum. Peck
(back out to clear chips) on anything deeper than about one diameter.
Cutting fluid on metal. Ease off as the bit breaks through so it does
not grab.

A **clearance hole** is larger than the bolt so the bolt slides.
For a #10, that is about **0.196 in** (a #9 bit, or 13/64). For
1/4-20, something like **17/64** or letter F. A **tap drill** is
*smaller* than the bolt so there is material left for the tap to cut
into. Common 75% thread starting points:

- **#10-32** → tap drill **#21** (0.159 in)
- **1/4-20** → tap drill **#7** (0.201 in)

Use the chart on the wall or McMaster. Do not invent a bit because
it "looks close."

Tapping is a rhythm, not a workout. Haas's
[Tapping Essentials](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=bkrUzGooA9k)
is the video to watch: taper / plug / bottoming chamfers, and why a
taper tap is the one you start by hand. Then:

1. Drill the tap-drill hole. Deburr both sides.
2. Put the matching tap (#10-32) in a tap handle. Square to the hole.
   A drop of fluid.
3. Half turn in, quarter turn back to break the chip. Repeat.
4. Do not lean on it. Taps are hard and **brittle**. A snapped tap
   in a hole is a miserable extraction, often a scrapped part.

Test-fit: a clearance hole passes the bolt with fingers. A tapped
hole threads the bolt smoothly without a wrench for the first turns.
If you need a wrench to start, you are cross-threading or the hole
is wrong.

  drill press.
- [Measurement & Layout](../measurement-layout/),
  [Fasteners & Hardware](../fasteners-hardware/), and
  [Drills & Drivers](../drills-drivers/) completed.

## What you'll learn

- How to clamp, set speed, and drill on the press without a
  helicopter.
- How to pick a clearance bit versus a tap drill from a chart.
- How to hand-tap a #10-32 hole without breaking the tap.

## Tasks

1. **Watch the tapping video before you touch a tap.** Watch Haas
   [Tapping Essentials](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=bkrUzGooA9k)
   (Tip of the Day). Write: taper vs plug vs bottoming, and which
   one you will start with in a through-hole in 1/8 in wall tube.
   Optional second pass:
   [Thread Cutting with Mr Waite](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=oKnEnin9z6U)
   for the half-turn / quarter-back rhythm on camera.

2. **Look up the bits.** Using a shop tap chart or McMaster
   [thread charts](https://www.mcmaster.com/products/thread-charts/)
   / [drill charts](https://www.mcmaster.com/products/drill-charts/),
   write on a card:

   - #10-32 tap drill and #10 clearance
   - 1/4-20 tap drill and 1/4 clearance

   Pull those four bits (or the shop's equivalents) and show a
   mentor. Put them back in the labeled index.

3. **Drill-press setup, no spinning yet.** Glasses on, gloves off.
   Clamp scrap tube or plate in the vise. Point to the off switch,
   the chuck key (it does not stay in the chuck), and the speed
   control. Set a speed a mentor approves for a small bit in
   aluminum. Center-punch if the mark is not already punched.

4. **Drill two holes on the press.** On the same scrap (or the
   cut keeper from [Cutting Materials](../cutting-materials/) if a
   mentor agrees):

   - one **#10 clearance** hole
   - one **#10-32 tap-drill** hole

   Peck, fluid, ease through. Deburr both sides of both holes.
   A #10 screw must slide in the clearance hole and must **not**
   slide in the tap-drill hole.

5. **Tap the smaller hole.** Start square with a taper or plug
   #10-32 tap. Half in, quarter back, fluid, no heroics. Thread a
   #10-32 SHCS in with fingers for the first turns. If the tap
   binds, back it out and ask — do not force it. Mark the scrap
   with your name, "C" on the clearance hole, and "T" on the
   tapped hole.

## Acceptance Criteria

- [ ] A card lists tap-drill and clearance sizes for #10-32 and
      1/4-20, copied from a chart, and a mentor saw the matching
      bits.
- [ ] You set up the press (clamped work, speed, chuck key out,
      gloves off) before the spindle ran.
- [ ] A named scrap has a deburred #10 clearance hole that passes
      a #10 and a deburred tap-drill hole that does not.
- [ ] The tap-drill hole has clean #10-32 threads. A screw starts
      by hand. The tap is still in one piece.
- [ ] A mentor watched the first press holes and the tap.

## Notes

- A broken tap is a mentor problem immediately. Do not hide it in
  the hole and drill "around it."
- Through-holes in thin tube have very little thread. A nylock and
  a clearance hole is often stronger than a 1/16 in wall tap. Use
  what the drawing calls for.
- Next: [Grinders, Sanders & Rotary Tools](../grinders-sanders/)
  to clean up the edges you just made — still no gloves on a
