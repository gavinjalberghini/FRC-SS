## Description

The alliance station is loud. Long sentences die. Panic is contagious.
This ticket is a small vocabulary, a coach who calls deviations, and the
things you do not say when the clock is red.

You already have pieces of the script: the coach-to-driver arrow from
[Drive Team Roles](../drive-team-roles/), the three-word operator list
from [Operator Skills](../operator-skills/), and the human player's
"no more pieces" signal from [Human Player](../human-player/). This
ticket staples them onto one card you will actually use in a 150-second
match.

One voice to the driver. The coach watches the whole field because the
driver cannot. The coach calls **changes** ("you're on defense," "climb
now") and the clock ("60," "endgame," "last cycle"). The coach does not
narrate every successful pickup. Mentors do not shout over the glass. If
they have a safety call, it is "disable" or "e-stop," not a driving tip.
[G422](https://firstfrc.blob.core.windows.net/frc2026/Manual/2026GameManual.pdf)
is hands off the controls for coaches. The spirit of that rule is also
hands off the driver's ears.

This site does not track who is starting at coach. Practice matches
beat a confident speaking voice. If your team exported these tickets,
close this issue there once a mentor accepts the criteria below.

## Prerequisites

- [Drive Team Roles](../drive-team-roles/) — role map exists.
- [Operator Skills](../operator-skills/) — three-word vocabulary exists.
- [Human Player](../human-player/) — one "no more pieces" signal exists.
- [Strategy: Pre-Match Strategy](../../strategy-scouting/match-strategy/)
  if your team has started writing a one-pager; if not, you will make a
  thin one in this ticket.

## What you'll learn

- A default vocabulary that fits on one card.
- How the coach uses that card during teleop — deviations and the clock,
  not a stream of praise or blame.
- What never belongs on a headset or in someone's ear.
- How to debrief in three bullets without lecturing on the walk back.

## Tasks

1. **Listen to a match as a coach, not a fan.** Watch
   [Einstein Final Tiebreaker — 2026 FIRST Championship](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=j8wz5vw5XfE)
   once with the sound on. You will not hear the stations. That is the
   point. Write five calls you *would* have made as coach for one
   alliance (clock, defense, climb, jam you inferred, "stick" to the
   plan). Then cross out any call that is longer than three words or
   that blames a person. The leftover list is the tone of your script.

2. **Publish the communication script.** One page or one index card,
   same physical pile as the role map and the clock card. Use these
   headings and fill every line with *your* team's words:

   - **Clock** — "60," "endgame," "last cycle" (and this season's shift
     calls if you use them: "shift 2," "hub live").
   - **Field** — "left," "right," "defend," "clear."
   - **Robot** — "jam," "comms," "brownout," "you're clear" (from
     [The Driver Station](../driver-station/) and
     [Operator Skills](../operator-skills/)).
   - **Plan** — "stick," "switch," "climb now."
   - **Human player** — the one signal from
     [Human Player](../human-player/).
   - **Never** — blame during a live robot; strategy arguments; mentor
     driving tips through the glass.

   If a call needs a clause, it is too late or too early. Save it for
   the debrief.

3. **Write the last-60-seconds line.** The coach restates one sentence
   before the match and does not rewrite it at 0:20:
   auto + first teleop job + endgame cue. Example shape: *Auto hub,
   first cycle right, climb at 30.* You will reuse this sentence on the
   [pre-match card](../pre-match-checklist/). Time yourself saying it.
   It must fit in one breath.

4. **Coach a practice match using only the card.** Full cycle: 20-second
   hands-off auto, then teleop against a stopwatch using this season's
   times from
   [Match Flow & Field Rules](../match-flow-rules/). The coach may say
   only words that appear on the script, plus the clock. A backup
   tallies extra words. Driver and operator use the three-word list.
   Mentors stand where they cannot be heard unless they are calling
   "disable" for safety. Run at least one full 2:40 (auto + teleop) on
   a field or mock field.

5. **Practice the forbidden phrases by not saying them.** After the
   practice match, list three phrases you will not use in the station
   (examples: "that's on you," "try the lunch idea," "what are you
   doing"). Put them on the Never section if they are not already
   there. Then debrief with three bullets only — what was consistent,
   what broke, what the next match's one-sentence plan is. Not a
   lecture on the walk back to the pit. The
   [in-event iteration](../../strategy-scouting/in-event-iteration/)
   ticket uses the same three-bullet shape.

6. **Hand the script to a mentor on the station.** A mentor stands at
   the mock driver station, hears one full practice, and initials the
   card. If they add a word, it goes through the coach, not onto the
   driver's controller.

## Acceptance Criteria

- [ ] A one-page (or one-card) communication script exists with Clock,
      Field, Robot, Plan, Human player, and Never sections filled in
      with this team's words.
- [ ] You coached or played a full timed practice match using only that
      script. A tally of extra words exists. Mentor-checkable on a
      field or mock field.
- [ ] The last-60-seconds sentence is written and can be said in one
      breath.
- [ ] Three forbidden phrases are listed and were not used in the
      practice match.
- [ ] The debrief used three bullets, then stopped.

## Notes

- Headsets help and they also leak panic. If you use them, the script
  still applies. A headset is not permission to narrate.
- Alliance partners have coaches too. You are guests in a shared box.
  Your script does not include directing another team's driver.
- After the match, one breath, then the walk. The Question Box is for
  referees; the three-bullet debrief is for you.
- The next ticket ([Pre-Match Checklist](../pre-match-checklist/)) puts
  the last-60-seconds sentence on a card you run every match.
