## Overview

Most “the robot just didn’t work” stories start in the queue. A written
checklist, run the same way every match, is how you stop donating matches
to loose bumpers and tired batteries. This lesson is the list and the last
minute.

## Prerequisites

- [The Driver Station](../driver-station/)
- [Electrical Safety]({{ '/learning/electrical/electrical-safety/' | relative_url }})

## What you'll learn

- What belongs on the list.
- How to run it without blocking the alliance.
- What the last 60 seconds are for.

## Unit 1 — The list (every match)

At minimum:

- **Bumpers** — correct color, secure, legal.
- **Battery** — charged, latched, connector fully seated, voltage checked.
- **Mechanical** — obvious loose hardware on the mechanisms you just used.
- **Software** — correct alliance color if the code needs it; auto selected
  and confirmed out loud.
- **Controllers** — the ones you practiced with, in the ports you
  practiced with.
- **People** — glasses, e-stop knowledge, human player ready.

If it is not written, it will be skipped when you are late.

## Unit 2 — Who runs it

- One owner (often the technician or operator) reads; others confirm.
- Do not skip items because “we just checked.” The last match is why you
  check again.
- If something fails, you are not “probably fine.” You fix it or you tell
  the coach you are a no-go.

## Unit 3 — Last 60 seconds

- Hands still. Auto confirmed. Driver looking at the field, not the
  laptop.
- Coach restates one sentence: auto + first teleop job + endgame cue.
- No new strategy. No “try that thing we talked about at lunch.”

## Steps & acceptance criteria

- [ ] Write the checklist on one card and run it three times in the shop.
- [ ] Assign a reader and a confirmer for the next practice match.
- [ ] Demonstrate a battery swap and bumper swap on the clock.
- [ ] Practice the last-60-seconds script until it is under 15 seconds.
