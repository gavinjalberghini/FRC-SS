## Description

Most "the robot just didn't work" stories start in the queue. A written
checklist, run the same way every match, is how you stop donating
matches to loose bumpers and tired batteries. This ticket is the list
and the last minute.

You already walk a Driver Station
([The Driver Station](../driver-station/)), you already have a
communication script
([In-Match Communication](../in-match-communication/)), and you already
know [G301](https://firstfrc.blob.core.windows.net/frc2026/Manual/2026GameManual.pdf)
(be prompt) and
[G303](https://firstfrc.blob.core.windows.net/frc2026/Manual/2026GameManual.pdf)
(start your robot). The checklist is those rules turned into a card one
person reads and others confirm. If it is not written, it will be skipped
when you are late.

One owner reads — often the technician or operator. Others confirm. Do
not skip items because "we just checked." The last match is why you
check again. If something fails, you are not "probably fine." You fix it
or you tell the coach you are a no-go.

The last 60 seconds are not for new strategy. Hands still. Auto
confirmed out loud. Driver looking at the field, not the laptop. Coach
says the one sentence from the communication script. Mentors do not
reach in to plug Ethernet "so you don't mess it up." Students run the
card. Practice hours on this card beat a confident memory.

This site does not track completed checklists. If your team exported
these tickets, attach a photo of a filled card and close the issue once
a mentor accepts the criteria below.

## Prerequisites

- [The Driver Station](../driver-station/) — you can point to voltage,
  comms, USB, enable, and e-stop.
- [In-Match Communication](../in-match-communication/) — last-60-seconds
  sentence exists.
- [Electrical Safety](../../electrical/electrical-safety/) — battery
  handling is not a vibe.
- A practice robot, bumpers of both colors if you have them, and at
  least one charged battery.

## What you'll learn

- What belongs on the list, in an order you can run in a queue.
- How to run it without blocking the alliance.
- What the last 60 seconds are for — and what they are not for.

## Tasks

1. **Read the official "before each match" list, then write yours.**
   Skim
   [Driver Station Best Practices — Before Each Match](https://docs.wpilib.org/en/stable/docs/software/driverstation/driver-station-best-practices.html)
   and
   [Game Manual G303](https://firstfrc.blob.core.windows.net/frc2026/Manual/2026GameManual.pdf).
   Then write a **pre-match card** that fits on one side of an index
   card. At minimum it must include:

   - **Bumpers** — correct color, secure, legal
     ([inspection checklist](https://firstfrc.blob.core.windows.net/frc2026/Manual/2026FRCInspectionChecklist.pdf)
     bumper lines are the standard).
   - **Battery** — charged, latched, connector fully seated, voltage
     checked on the DS.
   - **Mechanical** — obvious loose hardware on the mechanisms you just
     used.
   - **Software** — correct alliance color if the code needs it; auto
     selected and confirmed out loud
     ([alliance color](https://docs.wpilib.org/en/stable/docs/software/basic-programming/alliancecolor.html)
     is FMS-set on the field).
   - **Controllers** — the ones you practiced with, in the ports you
     practiced with (USB tab).
   - **People** — glasses, e-stop knowledge, human player ready, coach
     has the one-sentence plan.
   - **Last 60** — the sentence from
     [In-Match Communication](../in-match-communication/).

   Number the lines. Leave a checkbox on each. This is the artifact
   later tickets will say "run the card."

2. **Assign a reader and a confirmer.** Write two names on the card
   (or **GAP** if you do not have them — this website will not invent
   them). Reader reads the line. Confirmer touches or points and says
   "yes" or "fail." The driver does not read their own USB ports while
   also watching the field.

3. **Run the card three times on a practice robot.** Full walk: bumpers,
   battery, DS setup, USB, auto named out loud, people, last-60
   sentence. A mentor watches one of the three. Time each run. If you
   skip a line because you are embarrassed, that run does not count.
   Photograph one completed card (checks and a time). That photo is
   evidence; this site does not store it.

4. **Time a battery swap and a bumper swap.** From "we need the other
   color / a fresh battery" to "card re-run through battery and
   bumpers." Write both times on the back of the card. You will need
   those numbers in
   [Pit Crew & Between-Match Repairs](../pit-and-repairs/). Disconnect
   the battery before hands go in
   ([Electrical Safety](../../electrical/electrical-safety/)).

5. **Practice the last-60-seconds script until it is under 15 seconds.**
   Hands still. Auto confirmed. Coach says the one sentence. Driver
   looks at the field. No new strategy. No "try that thing we talked
   about at lunch." Time it. Write the time on the card. Run it at the
   end of every checklist rehearsal, not only when you feel calm.

6. **Fail one item on purpose.** Have a partner leave a bumper strap
   loose, a stick in the wrong USB port, or a tired battery on the
   cart. Run the card. You must catch it and say "no-go" or fix it
   before you would have enabled. If the reader talks past the fail,
   run the card again.

## Acceptance Criteria

- [ ] A one-card pre-match checklist exists with numbered lines,
      checkboxes, a named reader and confirmer (or GAP), and the
      last-60-seconds sentence.
- [ ] The card has been run three times on a practice robot. A mentor
      watched at least one run on a field, mock field, or shop station.
      One completed card (photo or the physical card) exists.
- [ ] Battery-swap and bumper-swap times are written on the card.
- [ ] The last-60-seconds script is under 15 seconds and contains no
      new strategy.
- [ ] You caught a planted fail (loose bumper, wrong USB, tired
      battery) and treated it as a no-go or a fix.

## Notes

- On the field, FMS sets alliance color. Off the field, you set it on
  the DS Operation tab. Confirm auto *out loud* either way. A silent
  nod is how you run the wrong path.
- The technician may own the card and still not be a sixth stick. If
  they are in the event-designated technician area
  ([section 6.3.1](https://firstfrc.blob.core.windows.net/frc2026/Manual/2026GameManual.pdf)),
  the operator or driver finishes the last lines.
- A checklist that lives only in a group chat will not be in your hand
  in queue. Paper or a card on the cart.
- The next ticket ([Pit Crew & Between-Match Repairs](../pit-and-repairs/))
  starts when something on this card fails after a match, not before.
