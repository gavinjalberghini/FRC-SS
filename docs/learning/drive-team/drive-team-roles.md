## Description

A FIRST Robotics Competition match is a short, public exam. In
[REBUILT](https://www.firstinspires.org/resources/library/frc/season-materials)
the clock is 20 seconds of autonomous plus 2 minutes 20 seconds of teleop —
about two and a half minutes of live robot. If two people think they own the
same job, you lose seconds. If nobody owns a job, you lose the match. This
ticket names the seats so practice, queue, and events use the same words.

The [2026 Game Manual, section 6.2](https://firstfrc.blob.core.windows.net/frc2026/Manual/2026GameManual.pdf)
defines a **DRIVE TEAM** as up to five people from the same team, with four
official roles and at most one non-student:

- **DRIVE COACH** — one guide or advisor.
- **TECHNICIAN** — one resource for setup, troubleshooting, and removal.
- **DRIVER** — up to three student operators and controllers of the robot.
- **HUMAN PLAYER** — a scoring-element manager (shares the three-student
  DRIVER / HUMAN PLAYER allotment).

This playbook splits the official DRIVER job into two *jobs* you will
practice: **driver** (drivetrain) and **operator** (mechanisms). Some
robots use one student for both. The jobs still exist. Write them on the
role map even if one person holds both sticks.

The habit that decides matches is **one voice to the driver**. The coach
talks to the driver. The driver does not take advice from the stands, the
human player, or a mentor behind the glass. Mentors do not grab a
controller. If they do, the team is no longer student-driven in the only
moment that is public. [G422](https://firstfrc.blob.core.windows.net/frc2026/Manual/2026GameManual.pdf)
is the rule version of that habit: a robot is operated only by that
team's drivers and/or human players.

This site does not track who is starting. It does not store a roster, a
practice log, or a vote. Selection lives with the team — on a card you
write in this ticket, and later under
[Practice Hours & Selection](../practice-selection/). If your team
exported these tickets into its own GitHub, close this issue there once a
mentor accepts the criteria below.

## Prerequisites

- None. This is the starting point for every drive-team member.
- A way to watch a short official match video (phone or laptop with
  speakers or captions).

## What you'll learn

- The five jobs this playbook uses, and how they map onto FIRST's official
  DRIVE TEAM roles.
- Who speaks, who touches the robot, and who does not, during a live
  match.
- How backups work, and why a named gap is more honest than a pretend
  starter.
- How to watch a match as a crew member, not as a spectator.

## Tasks

1. **Watch one official match and name the jobs.** Watch
   [Einstein Final 1 — 2026 FIRST Championship](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=EjF9we707DA)
   (about 3.5 minutes, FIRST Robotics Competition channel) once all the
   way through without pausing for strategy debate. Then watch it again
   and write, for *one* alliance station you can actually see:

   - who is on the drivetrain sticks (driver)
   - who is on mechanisms, if a second student is visible (operator)
   - where the human player stands (outpost, not the driver shelf)
   - who is talking (coach) versus who is silent
   - whether anyone who is not a student appears to touch a controller

   You will not hear the headset. Infer jobs from bodies, buttons, and
   who looks at the field versus the laptop. Two sentences of notes are
   enough. Keep the note — Task 4 uses it.

2. **Read the official role table.** Open
   [2026 Game Manual section 6.2, Table 6-1](https://firstfrc.blob.core.windows.net/frc2026/Manual/2026GameManual.pdf)
   (or the
   [HTML manual](https://firstfrc.blob.core.windows.net/frc2026/Manual/HTML/2026GameManual.htm),
   section 6.2). Write four lines in your own words:

   - Drive coach is …
   - Technician is …
   - Driver (official) is …
   - Human player is …

   Add a fifth line the manual does not print: *Operator is the mechanism
   half of the official DRIVER job.* If you cannot explain the fifth line
   to a freshman, you do not understand it yet.

3. **Learn who talks and who does not.** On a scratch card, draw five
   boxes labeled driver, operator, human player, technician, and coach.
   Draw **one** arrow: coach → driver. That is the only strategy arrow
   that is allowed while the robot is enabled. Then write three "never"
   lines:

   - Mentors never grab a controller.
   - The human player never coaches the driver from the outpost.
   - The technician does not "just tighten that" after the robot is
     staged unless the coach and FTA process allow it.

   You will turn this card into a communication script in
   [In-Match Communication](../in-match-communication/). Do not write a
   novel. The point is the arrow.

4. **Write the team's role map.** On one side of an index card (or a
   single page in a shared drive the team already uses), list the five
   jobs. For each job write:

   - a one-sentence job description in *your* words
   - the current starter, or **GAP** if the team has not named one
   - the current backup, or **GAP**
   - whether that backup has practiced that seat this season (yes / no /
     unknown)

   Gaps are allowed. Inventing a name so the card looks full is how
   backups disappear at the event. This website will not fill the gaps
   for you. If your team exported these tickets, attach a photo of the
   card to the issue.

5. **Walk the five seats on a practice robot or mock station.** In the
   shop, stand where each person will stand for a practice match: driver
   and operator at the laptop, coach where the driver can hear them
   without turning around, human player at a taped "outpost," technician
   off the sticks. Say out loud, once each, the one-sentence job from
   Task 4. A mentor watches. Nobody picks up a controller in this task
   except to show which ports the driver and operator will use later.

6. **Hand the card to a mentor.** Give the role map to the drive coach or
   the mentor who owns drive team. Ask them to mark one thing that is
   wrong or missing. Fix that one thing. If the team has no named coach
   yet, write **GAP** on the coach line and still get a mentor signature
   that they saw the card.

## Acceptance Criteria

- [ ] You can name the five playbook jobs and FIRST's four official roles,
      and you can say in one sentence how operator maps onto DRIVER.
- [ ] A written role map exists with a starter or **GAP** and a backup or
      **GAP** for each of the five jobs. A mentor has seen it on a field,
      mock field, or shop station — not only in a group chat.
- [ ] You can point to the single allowed strategy arrow (coach → driver)
      and state that mentors do not grab controllers.
- [ ] You watched
      [Einstein Final 1](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=EjF9we707DA)
      and can identify at least three jobs from what is visible on
      camera.
- [ ] You can state, without looking at this page, who talks to a referee
      after a match (the coach, through the
      [Question Box](https://firstfrc.blob.core.windows.net/frc2026/Manual/2026GameManual.pdf)
      in section 6.7.1 — not the driver mid-match).

## Notes

- Buttons matter at events. FIRST issues Drive Coach, Drive Team, and
  Technician buttons. Drivers and human players wear Drive Team buttons.
  The technician is *not* a sixth stick.
- Playoff alliances sometimes "loan" a driver. The manual's intent is
  that a drive team is people who arrived affiliated with that team, not
  a strategic adoption. Read the blue box under section 6.2 before you
  invent a clever exception.
- Backups watch matches and run the checklist. They do not disappear
  until they are called. Switching seats mid-event is a coach decision,
  not a popularity vote after a bad match.
- The next ticket ([The Driver Station](../driver-station/)) is where you
  touch the laptop. Do not skip it because you "already know how to
  enable."
