## Overview

The Driver Station (DS) is how the robot is enabled, how you see brownouts,
and how you know you still have comms. Drivers who only watch the field
miss the warning that precedes a dead robot. This lesson is the DS you
must be fluent in before you care about fancy HUDs.

## Prerequisites

- [Drive Team Roles](../drive-team-roles/)

## What you'll learn

- The enable / disable / e-stop flow.
- What comms and brownout look like.
- Which DS screens matter in a match vs. in the shop.

## Unit 1 — Enable is a procedure

- Laptop charged, correct alliance station, Ethernet to the field when
  required, joysticks in the ports you practiced with.
- Wait for the FTA / field staff. Do not mash enable early.
- Know where **E-stop** is and that it is not a joke. Know how to disable
  if the robot is unsafe.

Practice the plug-in order until it is boring.

## Unit 2 — Comms and power

- **Comms** drop: the robot is a brick. Do not invent stick inputs. Tell
  the coach; the technician and FTA path starts.
- **Brownout**: voltage sags, brownout counter climbs, mechanisms die
  first. Back off current (usually drivetrain) instead of holding full
  stick into a stall.
- Battery choice is a drive-team decision as much as an electrical one.
  A tired battery is a strategy error.

## Unit 3 — What to glance at

In a match you have time for three glances:

1. Field.
2. Game clock / endgame cue from the coach.
3. DS: comms, battery, brownout, sticky faults.

Practice those glances in the shop with the robot on blocks. A first-time
DS user on the field is a risk.

## Steps & acceptance criteria

- [ ] Demonstrate plug-in, enable, disable, and e-stop location on a real
      DS.
- [ ] Explain comms loss vs. brownout and what the driver does in each.
- [ ] Set up the DS for a practice drive without a mentor touching the
      laptop.
- [ ] Point to battery voltage and the brownout indicator while the robot
      is enabled on blocks.
