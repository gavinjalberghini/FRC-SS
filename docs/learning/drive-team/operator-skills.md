## Overview

The operator’s job is to make mechanisms boring: same button, same timing,
same result. Fancy one-off sequences are how you miss in elims. This lesson
is timing, bindings, and how two people share one robot without a debate.

## Prerequisites

- [Driving Fundamentals](../driving-fundamentals/)
- [The Driver Station](../driver-station/)

## What you'll learn

- How to bind and practice mechanisms as drills, not vibes.
- When to act vs. when to wait for the driver.
- How to fail a cycle without failing the next one.

## Unit 1 — Bindings you can do in the dark

- One action per control when you can. Mode-shift only if you practice it
  weekly.
- The operator does not rematch bindings the morning of an event without
  a practice block after.
- Software should make illegal or self-destructive combos hard. Still,
  know what a stuck button does.

Walk the bindings out loud before every practice: “intake, score, stow,
climb.”

## Unit 2 — Timing is a conversation

- The driver delivers pose; the operator delivers the mechanism. If the
  pose is wrong, do not fire and blame the driver after.
- Short cues: “ready,” “scoring,” “jam,” “stowed.” Not a sentence.
- If you need a paragraph, the binding or the robot is wrong.

## Unit 3 — Recovery

- A miss is a reset: stow, clear the piece if the rules allow, get to the
  next start pose.
- Do not hold a failing command. Cancel, then retry.
- Tell the coach once. Then drive the next cycle.

## Steps & acceptance criteria

- [ ] Recite the binding map without looking at the controller.
- [ ] Run ten score attempts with a driver; log make/miss and cause.
- [ ] Demonstrate a jam recovery without the driver stopping the whole
      robot for more than a defined time.
- [ ] Agree on a three-word vocabulary with your driver and use only that
      in a practice match.
