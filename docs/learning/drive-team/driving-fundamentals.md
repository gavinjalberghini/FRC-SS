## Description

Driving is a sport skill. Talent helps; hours on a field — or a mock
field with real geometry — decide the event. This ticket is what to
practice and how to practice so you are not just "messing around with
the robot."

You already know the seats ([Drive Team Roles](../drive-team-roles/)),
you can enable without help ([The Driver Station](../driver-station/)),
and you can recite the clock
([Match Flow & Field Rules](../match-flow-rules/)). None of that scores
fuel. A **cycle** does: start pose, collect, score, reset. In REBUILT a
useful cycle also respects hub active/inactive windows. Practicing
"drive around until it looks cool" teaches crashes. Structured reps
teach matches.

The driver owns the drivetrain sticks. The operator owns mechanisms.
The coach owns the plan. One voice to the driver. Mentors do not grab a
controller to "show you the line." They can stand where you can hear
them and say "again from the tape." If the only way the robot scores is
with a mentor on the sticks, the team does not have a driver. It has a
demo.

Practice hours beat opinions. A fast afternoon in front of visitors is
not a log. Ten timed cycles with failures written down is a log. The
coach will use that log in
[Practice Hours & Selection](../practice-selection/). This website does
not store the log and does not pick starters. Selection lives with the
team.

This site does not track whether you finished the drill. If your team
exported these tickets, close this issue there once a mentor accepts the
criteria below.

## Prerequisites

- [The Driver Station](../driver-station/) — you can set up and enable
  without a mentor on the laptop.
- [Match Flow & Field Rules](../match-flow-rules/) — you know when auto
  is hands-off and when endgame starts.
- A practice robot or drive base, a stopwatch, game pieces (or close
  stand-ins), and a floor you can tape. Safety glasses. A coach or
  backup who is *not* on the sticks.

## What you'll learn

- The difference between free drive and a timed cycle drill.
- How to build field sense: where you are without staring at the
  bumpers.
- How to log hours that count toward selection — and how to end a
  session when reps fall apart.

## Tasks

1. **Watch cycles, not highlights.** Watch
   [Einstein Final Tiebreaker — 2026 FIRST Championship](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=j8wz5vw5XfE)
   (FIRST Robotics Competition channel, about 3.5 minutes). Pick *one*
   robot you can track and tally:

   - how many times it collected fuel
   - how many times it scored (or attempted) in an active hub
   - whether it left a cycle in time to climb

   Write the tally. You are not scouting for a pick list. You are
   learning what a cycle looks like at full speed so your shop drill
   has a picture.

2. **Write the drill before you enable.** On the same card style as the
   clock card, write one drill with all four parts filled in. Example
   shape (change the poses to *your* field tape):

   - **Start pose:** bumpers on a taped line, intake toward the pile.
   - **Goal:** collect at least one piece, drive to a taped hub stand-in,
     score or dump, return to the line.
   - **Clock:** stopwatch from first motion to bumpers back on the line.
   - **Abort:** if the intake jams, run the recovery you will write in
     [Operator Skills](../operator-skills/), then reset. Do not "keep
     going" through a broken intake and call it practice.

   If you cannot write the drill, you are about to free-drive. Stop.

3. **Walk the Driver Station, then run the drill.** Set up the DS the
   way [The Driver Station](../driver-station/) taught: sticks in the
   practiced ports, team number, comms, voltage. Enable teleop. Run
   **fifteen minutes** of that one drill. A partner runs the stopwatch
   and writes every cycle time plus make/miss/jam. You look at the
   field and the pieces, not the robot's bumpers. The coach says only
   the vocabulary you will lock in later ("ready," "jam," "reset") —
   not a stream of driving tips.

   Ten timed cycles is the minimum that counts. If you only get six
   because the robot died, write that. A dead robot is data.

4. **Drive from the other wall.** If your shop has room, tape a second
   driver station on the opposite side (red versus blue is a mirror).
   Run five more cycles from that side. If you do not have room, stand
   on the other side of the robot on blocks and *name* the landmarks
   you will have in a venue (truss, wall seam, alliance wall lights).
   Field sense is landmarks, not a sixth sense.

5. **Log the session.** In the team's existing log (paper, spreadsheet,
   or shared doc — not this website), add one row:

   - date
   - duration
   - which robot (comp / practice / kitbot)
   - which drill
   - cycle times and failure notes
   - who was on the sticks, who was coach, who was backup

   Tired drivers make sloppy hours. If the last three cycles fell
   apart, end the session. Write "stopped because reps fell apart."
   That line is more honest than another twenty minutes of crashing.

6. **Debrief in three bullets, then stop talking.** After the drill,
   the driver says three things: what was consistent, what broke, what
   the next session's drill is. The mentor may add one sentence. Nobody
   relitigates the Einstein match. Put the log where the coach can find
   it for selection.

## Acceptance Criteria

- [ ] A written drill exists with start pose, goal, clock, and abort.
- [ ] You ran a 15-minute structured session and recorded at least ten
      cycle times with make/miss/jam notes. A mentor saw the session on
      a field or mock field — a story about driving does not count.
- [ ] You drove at least one block of reps looking at the field and
      pieces, not the bumpers. The coach or mentor watched for crashes.
- [ ] The session is in the team's practice log with robot, drill,
      times, and who was in each seat.
- [ ] You can explain, without looking at this page, why ten timed
      cycles beat an hour of demo driving for visitors.

## Notes

- Reset the same way every time. Changing the start pose is a different
  drill. Mixing them and averaging the times is how you lie to yourself.
- Defense is a drill with a start pose and a clock too. Do not invent
  it as "go bother them" in the last five minutes.
- If a mentor takes the sticks to unstick a mechanism, they hand them
  back and you restart the rep. The recovery is yours.
- The next ticket ([Operator Skills](../operator-skills/)) is the other
  half of the cycle. Drivers who skip it still miss when the intake is
  late.
