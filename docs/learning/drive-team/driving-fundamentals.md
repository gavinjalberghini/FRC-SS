## Overview

Driving is a sport skill. Talent helps; hours on a field (or a mock field
with real geometry) decide the event. This lesson is what to practice and
how to practice so you are not just “messing around with the robot.”

## Prerequisites

- [The Driver Station](../driver-station/)
- [Match Flow & Field Rules](../match-flow-rules/)

## What you'll learn

- The difference between free drive and structured practice.
- How to build field sense (where you are without staring at the robot).
- How to log hours that count.

## Unit 1 — Structured practice

Free drive teaches crashes. Structured practice teaches cycles:

- A **rep** has a start pose, a goal, and a clock.
- Reset the same way every time. Changing the start is a different drill.
- Stop on a jam and run the recovery, then reset. Do not “keep going”
  through a broken intake and call it practice.

Ten timed cycles beat an hour of demo driving for visitors.

## Unit 2 — Field sense

- Look at the field and the game pieces, not the robot’s bumpers. The
  robot is in your hands; the field is the puzzle.
- Practice from both driver-station perspectives if your district uses
  both. Mirrors matter.
- Learn the landmarks (truss, wall seam, tape) you will actually have in
  the venue.

## Unit 3 — Log it

- Date, duration, which robot (comp vs. practice), which drill, what
  broke.
- The coach uses the log for [Practice Hours & Selection](../practice-selection/).
- Tired drivers make sloppy hours. End the session when reps fall apart.

## Steps & acceptance criteria

- [ ] Run a 15-minute structured session with a written drill and a
      stopwatch.
- [ ] Record ten cycles and the time of each; note the failures.
- [ ] Drive one session looking at the field, not the robot (coach
      watches for crashes).
- [ ] Add the session to the practice log.
