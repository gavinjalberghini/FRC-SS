## Overview

Drive-team seats are not seniority prizes and they are not a vote after
one good afternoon. They are earned in the log: structured hours, reliability
under a clock, and the ability to take a coach’s call. This lesson is how
to select and how to keep the people who were not selected still on the
crew.

## Prerequisites

- [Driving Fundamentals](../driving-fundamentals/)
- [In-Match Communication](../in-match-communication/)
- The [Decision-Making Guide]({{ '/decision-making/' | relative_url }})

## What you'll learn

- What “ready” means in writing.
- How to run a selection that people can respect.
- How backups stay match-ready.

## Unit 1 — Publish the standard before you pick

Write the standard in preseason:

- Minimum structured hours (and what counts).
- A skills check: cycles, defense, endgame, DS setup, vocabulary.
- Availability for the events you need them at.
- Coachability — a fast driver who cannot hear a call is not ready.

If the standard is secret, selection will look like favoritism even when
it is not.

## Unit 2 — The decision

- The drive coach and relevant leads recommend; mentors stay in the
  process as the [decision guide]({{ '/decision-making/' | relative_url }})
  requires.
- Use the log and a side-by-side practice, not a single scrimmage.
- Tell people *why*, privately and specifically. “We went another
  direction” is how you lose a backup.

## Unit 3 — After the announcement

- Backups practice the same drills on a defined schedule.
- Starters do not skip practice because they “already have the seat.”
- A mid-event swap is allowed when the log and the last matches say so —
  not because someone is loud.

## Steps & acceptance criteria

- [ ] Publish a one-page selection standard before tryouts.
- [ ] Run a timed skills check and record it the same way for everyone.
- [ ] Name starters and backups and a practice plan for the backups.
- [ ] Give one private, specific explanation to a person who was not
      selected (practice this even if you role-play it).
