## Description

Drive-team seats are not seniority prizes and they are not a vote after
one good afternoon. They are earned in the log: structured hours,
reliability under a clock, and the ability to take a coach's call. This
ticket is how to select and how to keep the people who were not selected
still on the crew.

You already have the evidence this decision needs:

- a **role map** with starters or gaps
  ([Drive Team Roles](../drive-team-roles/))
- a **practice log** of timed cycles
  ([Driving Fundamentals](../driving-fundamentals/))
- a **communication script** used in a practice match
  ([In-Match Communication](../in-match-communication/))
- a **completed pre-match card**
  ([Pre-Match Checklist](../pre-match-checklist/))
- an **inspection punch-list**
  ([Inspection & the Technician](../inspection-technician/))

This website does not track who is starting. It does not store hours, it
does not run a poll, and it does not close a seat. Selection lives with
the team — published in writing before tryouts, decided from the log and
a side-by-side practice, told privately and specifically. If the
standard is secret, selection will look like favoritism even when it is
not.

Mentors stay in the process the way the
[Decision-Making Guide]({{ '/decision-making/' | relative_url }}) requires. They do not
grab a controller to "break the tie." A fast driver who cannot hear a
call is not ready. Practice hours beat opinions.

If your team exported these tickets, attach the one-page standard and
the named map, then close the issue once a mentor accepts the criteria
below.

## What you'll learn

- What "ready" means in writing, before anyone is picked.
- How to run a selection people can respect.
- How backups stay match-ready instead of disappearing.
- Why this site will never be the roster.

## Tasks

1. **Watch a match as a selector, not a fan.** Watch
   [Einstein Final 1 — 2026 FIRST Championship](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=EjF9we707DA)
   and write four observable skills you would test in the shop (cycle
   consistency, defense without fouling, endgame leave-time, DS setup
   under a clock). You are not picking world champions. You are
   naming things you can time on *your* robot.

2. **Publish a one-page selection standard before tryouts.** One page,
   shared where the team already works, *before* anyone is named.
   Minimum sections:

   - **Hours that count** — structured drills with a written start
     pose, goal, and clock. Free drive and "I was in the room" do not
     count. Point at the
     [Driving Fundamentals](../driving-fundamentals/) log format.
   - **Skills check** — cycles, defense, endgame leave, DS setup from
     [The Driver Station](../driver-station/), vocabulary from the
     communication script, pre-match card from
     [Pre-Match Checklist](../pre-match-checklist/).
   - **Availability** — the events you need them at.
   - **Coachability** — can they take one voice? Mentors still do not
     grab the sticks to demonstrate "the right way" during the check.
   - **Backups** — every seat has a named backup who has practiced
     that seat, or the page says **GAP**.

   If you publish this after you already know who you want, you did
   not publish a standard. You published a justification.

3. **Run a timed skills check the same way for everyone.** Same drill
   card, same stopwatch, same logger. Record cycle times and failures
   the way Task 5 of Driving Fundamentals required. Include a DS
   cold-setup time and one full checklist run. A coach calls only
   words from the communication script. If two people want the same
   seat, they run the *same* drill, not two different "show us what
   you've got" sessions.

4. **Name starters and backups, or name the gaps.** Fill the role map
   from lesson 1 with real names or **GAP**. This website will not do
   it. Switching seats mid-event later is a coach decision from the
   log and the last matches — not because someone is loud. Starters do
   not skip practice because they "already have the seat." Backups
   practice the same drills on a defined schedule. Write that
   schedule (day, drill, who).

5. **Give one private, specific explanation.** Practice this even if
   you have to role-play it. Sit with a person who was not selected
   (or a teammate playing that person). Tell them *why*, using the
   standard and the log ("your cycles were 18 and 19 seconds with two
   jams; the seat needs sub-16 and a clean recovery"). "We went
   another direction" is how you lose a backup. Read
   [Feedback](../../leadership/feedback/) if you are tempted to be
   vague. Mentors may sit in; they do not give a second, conflicting
   reason after you leave.

6. **Put the decision where the team can find it — not here.** Post
   the standard, the named map, and the backup practice schedule on
   the team's board or shared drive. If you exported these tickets,
   attach those three artifacts to the issue. Do not edit this
   playbook with names.

## Acceptance Criteria

- [ ] A one-page selection standard was published *before* the skills
      check (date on the page).
- [ ] A timed skills check was recorded the same way for every person
      who wanted a seat. Mentor-checkable on a field or mock field.
- [ ] The role map names starters and backups or **GAP** for all five
      jobs, plus a backup practice schedule.
- [ ] One private, specific explanation was given (real or role-played)
      using the log, not a vibe.
- [ ] You can state that this website does not track who is starting
      and that selection lives with the team.

## Notes

- A mid-event swap is allowed when the log and the last matches say
  so. It is not a popularity vote in the stands.
- Coachability includes hearing "climb now" at 0:30
  ([Match Flow & Field Rules](../match-flow-rules/)) without arguing on
  the sticks.
- If every seat is a GAP, you still have a crew — you have a practice
  problem, not a website problem.
- The next ticket ([Event Operations](../event-operations/)) is the
  weekend: load-in, queue, and the coach's job between matches.
