## Description

Inspection is not a surprise if you ran the checklist in February. The
technician’s job is to bring a legal robot and a calm, factual
conversation to the Lead Robot Inspector (LRI) — and to keep it legal
after you pass, when the pit wants to "just add one more thing."

You already triage repairs in
[Pit Crew & Between-Match Repairs](../pit-and-repairs/). A repair that
changes size, weight, legality, or safety is not done until you ask
"does this need re-inspection?"
[I104](https://firstfrc.blob.core.windows.net/frc2026/Manual/2026GameManual.pdf)
is the rule. Fasteners, labels, code, identical COTS swaps, identical
mechanisms, and already-inspected subsets are the usual exceptions.
Everything else goes back in line.

[I102](https://firstfrc.blob.core.windows.net/frc2026/Manual/2026GameManual.pdf)
is get inspected before a qualification match.
[I103](https://firstfrc.blob.core.windows.net/frc2026/Manual/2026GameManual.pdf)
is bring it all — operator console and every mechanism you will use.
[I107](https://firstfrc.blob.core.windows.net/frc2026/Manual/2026GameManual.pdf)
is no student, no inspection. Students lead the conversation. Mentors
stand back unless asked. They do not grab a controller to "show the
inspector the climb."

The first event should not be the first time the robot sits on a scale.
Work the official punch-list at home. This website does not store your
open items. If your team exported these tickets, attach the punch-list
and close the issue once a mentor accepts the criteria below.

## Prerequisites

- [Pit Crew & Between-Match Repairs](../pit-and-repairs/).
- [Electrical: Wiring Craftsmanship](../../electrical/wiring-craftsmanship/)
  if you will argue a wiring item; the technician should have it either
  way.
- This season's robot rules and the official inspection checklist
  ([2026 FRC Inspection Checklist](https://firstfrc.blob.core.windows.net/frc2026/Manual/2026FRCInspectionChecklist.pdf)).
- A robot (or a representative configuration) you can weigh and walk.

## What you'll learn

- What inspectors actually look at first, using the official punch-list.
- How to talk to an LRI: answer the question that was asked.
- How to stay legal after you pass, including what I104 exempts.
- How the technician, the pre-match card, and the pit report fit
  together.

## Tasks

1. **Download the official punch-list and walk it.** Open
   [2026 FRC Inspection Checklist](https://firstfrc.blob.core.windows.net/frc2026/Manual/2026FRCInspectionChecklist.pdf)
   (also linked from
   [Season Materials](https://www.firstinspires.org/resources/library/frc/season-materials)).
   Print it or copy every line onto a team sheet. Walk the robot — in
   the shop, on a cart, on a mock field — and mark each line pass /
   fail / not applicable. At minimum you will touch:

   - size and weight
   - bumpers
   - electrical (battery secure, main breaker access, RSL)
   - pneumatics if used
   - software / radio / Driver Station versions this season
   - power-on check with the DS *tethered*
     ([WPILib Driver Station](https://docs.wpilib.org/en/stable/docs/software/driverstation/driver-station.html))

   Open items become the punch-list artifact. A blank "we looked fine"
   is not a walk.

2. **Weigh the robot and know the margin.** Weigh the configuration
   you would take to inspection (mechanisms you will use without
   re-inspect, per I103). Write robot weight, limit (this season's
   I103 number — read it, do not trust a memory of 125 or 150), and
   margin. Photograph the scale if you can. If you do not have a
   scale, use the school or a nearby team's, and write that you still
   need an event-day re-weigh. Playoff robots get re-weighed
   ([section 9](https://firstfrc.blob.core.windows.net/frc2026/Manual/2026GameManual.pdf)).

3. **Read I102–I107 and write the re-inspect rule.** In the
   [2026 Game Manual, section 9](https://firstfrc.blob.core.windows.net/frc2026/Manual/2026GameManual.pdf)
   (or the
   [HTML manual](https://firstfrc.blob.core.windows.net/frc2026/Manual/HTML/2026GameManual.htm)),
   read I102, I103, I104 (including exceptions A–F), I106 (robot off,
   battery out, stored energy down unless the inspector needs power),
   and I107. Write, in your own words, *what changes require a
   re-inspect this season* and *what does not*. Tape that paragraph
   to the pit wall. The technician owns the "is this still legal?"
   question before the pit owns the "is this cooler?" question.

4. **Role-play the LRI conversation.** A mentor or a student who is
   *not* the technician plays inspector. They ask three questions from
   the punch-list (battery vents, bumper corners, "show me the RSL").
   Students answer. Mentors stand back unless the inspector-in-role
   asks them. Answer the question that was asked. Do not volunteer a
   novel about a mechanism they have not reached. If something is
   wrong, say so and fix it. Arguing a failed bumper measurement is
   how you spend lunch in line again. Thank them.

5. **Tethered power-on with the real DS.** With the inspector
   punch-list's power-on section in hand, tether, enable only as the
   checklist requires, then disable and open the main breaker so LEDs
   go out. This is the same DS fluency as
   [The Driver Station](../driver-station/), now in an inspection
   frame. A mentor watches; they do not click.

6. **Document the passed configuration.** Take photos: full robot,
   bumpers, battery restraint, main breaker, RSL, operator console.
   Write the weight. This is what "we passed" means. After a pit
   repair, compare before you queue. If it no longer matches and I104
   does not exempt the change, you are not ready — you are in line.

## Acceptance Criteria

- [ ] A completed official inspection punch-list exists with pass /
      fail / N/A on each applicable line and a list of open items. A
      mentor walked at least part of it on the real robot.
- [ ] Robot weight (or a representative configuration) and margin are
      written down.
- [ ] A written I104 paragraph is posted: what needs re-inspect, what
      does not.
- [ ] Students answered three LRI-style questions without a mentor
      speaking first.
- [ ] Photos (or a written equivalent) of the would-be passed
      configuration exist.

## Notes

- Inspectors are why the field is safe. Treat the conversation like a
  design review, not a debate final.
- Adding a mechanism, changing bumpers, or a late-night "quick weld"
  can put you back in line. Re-inspection is not a punishment; it is
  the rule that keeps alliances from taking a red card for an unpassed
  partner (read I102's blue box).
- The technician is still not a sixth stick. Buttons: Technician
  button, designated area near the field during the match
  ([section 6.3.1](https://firstfrc.blob.core.windows.net/frc2026/Manual/2026GameManual.pdf)).
- The next ticket ([Practice Hours & Selection](../practice-selection/))
  is how people earn the seats you have been practicing. The website
  will not pick them.
