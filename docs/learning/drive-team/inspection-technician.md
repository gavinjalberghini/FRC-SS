## Overview

Inspection is not a surprise if you ran the checklist in February. The
technician’s job is to bring a legal robot and a calm, factual
conversation to the LRI — and to keep it legal after you pass, when the
pit wants to “just add one more thing.”

## Prerequisites

- [Pit Crew & Between-Match Repairs](../pit-and-repairs/)
- [Electrical: Wiring Craftsmanship]({{ '/learning/electrical/wiring-craftsmanship/' | relative_url }})
- This season’s Robot Rules / inspection checklist

## What you'll learn

- What inspectors actually look at first.
- How to talk to an LRI.
- How to stay legal after you pass.

## Unit 1 — Pass before you travel, then pass again

Work the official checklist at home:

- Weight, starting size, bumper rules, RSL, main breaker access.
- Battery secure, wiring workmanship, pneumatics if used.
- Software / radio as required this season.

The first event should not be the first time the robot sits on a scale.
See [Phase 5]({{ '/roadmap/' | relative_url }}).

## Unit 2 — The conversation

- Students lead. Mentors stand back unless asked.
- Answer the question that was asked. Do not volunteer a novel about a
  mechanism they have not reached.
- If something is wrong, fix it. Arguing a failed bumper measurement is
  how you spend your lunch in line again.
- Thank them. They are why the field is safe.

## Unit 3 — After you pass

- Re-inspection exists. Adding mechanism, changing bumpers, or a late-
  night “quick weld” can put you back in line.
- The technician owns the “is this still legal?” question before the
  pit owns the “is this cooler?” question.
- Keep the passed configuration documented (photos, weight).

## Steps & acceptance criteria

- [ ] Walk the current official inspection checklist against the robot
      and list open items.
- [ ] Weigh the robot (or a representative configuration) and know the
      margin.
- [ ] Role-play an LRI question the students answer without a mentor.
- [ ] Write the rule for what changes require a re-inspect this season.
