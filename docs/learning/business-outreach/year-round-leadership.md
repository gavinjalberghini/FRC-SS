## Description

Business work that starts at Kickoff is already late. Grants close in
the fall, sponsors budget in the spring, and the people who know the
contacts graduate in June. This capstone is the year-round calendar
and the handoff that keeps the trade alive.

You have now walked the whole loop: why this work is not extra credit,
a [pitch and paragraph](../team-story-brand/), a
[budget in the team drive](../season-budget/),
[what a sponsor buys](../sponsorship-fundamentals/), an
[ask and a stewardship note](../sponsor-outreach/), an
[event run-of-show](../outreach-events/), an
[evidence log](../measuring-impact/), a
[deliberate awards choice](../awards-landscape/), an
[Impact argument](../impact-award/) if you are submitting, a
[judge talk](../judge-interviews/), and a
[comms standard](../communications-media/). This ticket is how those
artifacts do not die when the robot is bagged — or when you are.

The front matter says “Ongoing” because the calendar is lived all
year. The **ticket** is still one sitting: publish the calendar, list
the off-season deadlines, walk a successor through one live
relationship, and move one login to team-owned access. Doing the year
is the job after the ticket.

Map work onto the year, not the six-week build. Align it with this
playbook’s [Season Roadmap](../../../roadmap/):

- **May–August** — retrospect, thank-yous, Impact notes while they
  are fresh, recruit, start fall grant drafts. The evidence log is
  cheapest the week the event happened, not in October.
- **September–December** — budget, sponsor renewals, grant
  submissions, training new advocates. Corporate budgets lock. A
  January ask is often a “next fiscal year.”
- **January–April** — execute events, collect evidence, interviews,
  in-season stewardship.

If the only business meeting is “when someone has time in January,”
the trade is not staffed. Lead Advocate is a co-captain on the
[org chart](../../../org-chart/), not a seasonal volunteer.

Money has a season too:

- Grants have published deadlines. Put them on the same board as
  robot milestones. FIRST keeps a running list on
  [Team Management Resources](https://www.firstinspires.org/resources/library/frc/team-management-resources)
  and the
  [Fundraising Toolkit](https://www.firstinspires.org/resources/library/fundraising-toolkit).
- Send the year-end report **before** you ask again. Sequence is
  respect. The stewardship email from Lesson 5 is that report, on a
  calendar.
- Registration invoices do not wait for inspiration. Current fees:
  [Cost & Registration](https://www.firstinspires.org/programs/cost-and-registration).

Handoff, before seniors leave:

- Shared contact list with last conversation date and promised
  benefits — in the **team’s drive**, the same place as the research
  cards. This website does not store it.
- Login ownership moved off personal accounts (social, email, FIRST
  Dashboard award submitter, the drive folder).
- A named successor already running a meeting, not “shadowing in
  May.”
- The impact file and brand kit in a place the next lead can find
  without a text.

Succession is a leadership skill. If the handoff feels like a speech
instead of a walk-through, do
[Mentorship & Succession](../../leadership/mentorship-succession/)
alongside this ticket.

This site does not track whether the calendar was followed. Mentors
accept the artifacts in the team’s drive. Next year’s funding is won
on that drive, this spring.

## Prerequisites

- All prior Business & Outreach lessons, or enough of their artifacts
  that you can point at a pitch, a budget, a research card, a
  run-of-show, an evidence log, and a comms standard.
- [Mentorship & Succession](../../leadership/mentorship-succession/)
  read, or scheduled.
- Edit access to the team drive and a successor (or a rising advocate)
  who can sit the walk-through.

## What you'll learn

- How to lay a 12-month business calendar against the
  [season roadmap](../../../roadmap/).
- How to time asks and reports so January is not the first time a
  sponsor hears from you.
- How to hand off contacts and unfinished grants without taking the
  relationships with you.

## Tasks

1. **Lay the year against the roadmap.** Open the
   [Season Roadmap](../../../roadmap/) and skim the
   [Fundraising Toolkit](https://www.firstinspires.org/resources/library/fundraising-toolkit)
   landing page. In the team drive, start a one-page 12-month
   calendar with three rows per season block (May–Aug, Sep–Dec,
   Jan–Apr). Each row is a named piece of work from this track
   (budget reopen, renewal ask, grant draft, event, evidence log,
   stewardship report, comms calendar, advocate training).

2. **List deadlines that fall outside build season.** Registration
   and extra-event dates from
   [Cost & Registration](https://www.firstinspires.org/programs/cost-and-registration),
   plus every grant or sponsor renewal the adult owner already
   knows about. If you do not know any, that is a finding — write
   “none documented; adult owner will list three by [date].” Put
   them on the calendar with owners.

3. **Assign owners for the next 90 days.** Not the whole year in
   detail. The next 90 days, every row has a student and an adult.
   If a row is “someone,” it is not staffed. New advocates from
   Lesson 1 can own a thank-you. They cannot own a grant deadline
   alone.

4. **Walk a successor through one live relationship.** Sit with a
   rising advocate. Open the contact record from
   [Sponsor Outreach](../sponsor-outreach/). Tell them: last
   conversation, promised benefits, what the sponsor is buying, the
   next date on the calendar. They repeat it back. If they cannot,
   the record is incomplete — fix the record, not the student.

5. **Move one critical login or folder to team-owned access.**
   Social admin, team email, the impact folder, the budget sheet, or
   the FIRST Dashboard award-submitter role. Personal accounts are
   how a graduating lead takes the team with them. Write what moved,
   from whom, to what. Do not write the password in the calendar.

6. **Schedule the monthly 15-minute budget review** you promised in
   [Season Budget](../season-budget/). A repeating event on the
   team’s real calendar, with the two owners named. A spreadsheet
   nobody opens is a wish list. Same for the evidence folder.

7. **Hand the calendar to a mentor.** Drive link, successor
   walk-through complete, one login moved. If your team exported
   these tickets, paste the link on the issue. This website does
   not get the contact list.

## Acceptance Criteria

- [ ] A one-page 12-month business calendar exists in the **team’s
      drive** with owners, mapped to the season roadmap.
- [ ] Grant, registration, and sponsor deadlines that fall outside
      build season are listed (or a dated plan to list them).
- [ ] The next 90 days have a student owner and an adult owner on
      every row.
- [ ] A successor walked through one live relationship and could
      repeat last contact, promised benefits, and the next date.
- [ ] One critical login or folder moved from a personal account to
      team-owned access.
- [ ] A monthly budget review is on the team’s real calendar.
- [ ] A mentor can find the pitch, budget, research cards, evidence
      log, and comms standard from the calendar without texting you.

## Notes

- “Ongoing” describes the job, not a license to leave the artifacts
  unfinished. Close the ticket when the calendar, the walk-through,
  and the login move exist. Live the calendar after.
- Do not export the sponsor list onto this website, a public GitHub
  issue, or a personal spreadsheet that leaves with you.
- A successor who has only shadowed is not a successor. They have
  run a meeting or owned a row.
- If you skip Impact next year, the evidence log still earns
  sponsors and recruits. The calendar does not disappear because the
  pursuit sheet said no.
- You started this track never having asked a sponsor for money.
  The year-round habit is how the team does not depend on you
  becoming the only person who ever has.
