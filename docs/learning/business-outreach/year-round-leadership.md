## Overview

Business work that starts at Kickoff is already late. Grants close in the
fall, sponsors budget in the spring, and the people who know the contacts
graduate in June. This capstone is the year-round calendar and the handoff
that keeps the trade alive.

## Prerequisites

- All prior Business & Outreach lessons.
- [Mentorship & Succession]({{ '/learning/leadership/mentorship-succession/' | relative_url }})

## What you'll learn

- How to lay a 12-month business calendar against the
  [season roadmap]({{ '/roadmap/' | relative_url }}).
- How to time asks and reports.
- How to hand off contacts and unfinished grants.

## Unit 1 — The calendar

Map work onto the year, not the six-week build:

- **May–August** — retrospect, thank-yous, Impact notes while they are
  fresh, recruit, start fall grant drafts.
- **September–December** — budget, sponsor renewals, grant submissions,
  training new advocates.
- **January–April** — execute events, collect evidence, interviews, in-
  season stewardship.

If the only business meeting is “when someone has time in January,” the
trade is not staffed.

## Unit 2 — Money has a season too

- Corporate budgets lock. A January ask is often a “next fiscal year.”
- Grants have published deadlines. Put them on the same board as robot
  milestones.
- Send the year-end report *before* you ask again. Sequence is respect.

## Unit 3 — Handoff

Before seniors leave:

- Shared contact list with last conversation date and promised benefits.
- Login ownership moved off personal accounts.
- A named successor already running a meeting, not “shadowing in May.”
- The impact file and brand kit in a place the next lead can find without
  a text.

## Steps & acceptance criteria

- [ ] Publish a one-page 12-month business calendar with owners.
- [ ] List grant and sponsor deadlines that fall outside build season.
- [ ] Walk a successor through the contact list and one live relationship.
- [ ] Move one critical login or folder to team-owned access.
