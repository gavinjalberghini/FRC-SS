## Overview

Impact is a researched argument: this team changed its community, in these
ways, over time, and students ran the work. The essay is the argument. The
supporting documents and the interview are the citations. This lesson is how
to build a submission that does not fall apart when a judge asks “who
actually did this?”

## Prerequisites

- All Advocate and Veteran Advocate lessons.
- A running impact file from [Measuring Community Impact](../measuring-impact/).

## What you'll learn

- How to structure an essay around evidence, not adjectives.
- What supporting documents are for.
- How to keep student voice without losing the facts.

## Unit 1 — The argument, not the scrapbook

Pick three pillars the team can prove (for example: rookie support, school
pipeline, industry partnership). For each pillar:

- **Need** — what was missing before you started.
- **Action** — what students did, with dates and partners.
- **Result** — a number or a named change you can defend.
- **Sustainability** — what continues if this year’s seniors graduate.

If a pillar is only “we visited a classroom once,” it is an anecdote. Cut it
or grow it before you write.

## Unit 2 — The essay

- Follow this season’s official word/page limits exactly. Over-length is
  unread.
- Students write. Mentors edit for clarity and rules, not for a corporate
  tone.
- Open with the claim. Do not bury the lede under a history of FIRST.
- Every paragraph should earn the next. If a sentence only restates the
  mission, delete it.

## Unit 3 — Supporting documents and the file

- Photos with captions, letters from partners, and the one-page budget
  snapshot judges always ask about.
- A short executive sheet the interview team can hold: pillars, numbers,
  who speaks to what.
- Version control. One “final” folder. No six copies named `impact_FINAL2`.

## Steps & acceptance criteria

- [ ] Write a one-page outline with three pillars, each with need / action /
      result / sustainability.
- [ ] Draft one pillar at full essay quality and have two students who did
      not write it fact-check it.
- [ ] Assemble a supporting-doc checklist against this season’s official
      packet.
- [ ] Name the interview team and a backup for each speaker.
