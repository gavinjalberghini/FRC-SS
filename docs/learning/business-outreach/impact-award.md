## Description

Impact is a researched argument: this team changed its community, in
these ways, over time, and students ran the work. The essay is the
argument. The supporting documents and the interview are the citations.
This ticket is how to build a submission that does not fall apart when
a judge asks “who actually did this?”

It is **multi-session**. The outline can happen in one sitting. A
pillar at full quality cannot. Fact-checking cannot. Assembling the
packet against this season’s official rules cannot. Do not try to
finish the essay in an evening and call the ticket done.

You only start this ticket if
[The FRC Awards Landscape](../awards-landscape/) said you are
submitting, and the
[evidence log](../measuring-impact/) is real. If the file is a folder
of vibes, go back. Judges will.

How Impact is judged, in official terms and in practice:

- FIRST describes it as the most prestigious award: the team that best
  represents a model for others to emulate and best embodies the
  mission of FIRST. Read the current purpose on
  [Submitted Awards](https://www.firstinspires.org/resources/library/frc/submitted-awards)
  and
  [Impact Award Resources](https://www.firstinspires.org/resources/library/frc/fia-resources).
  Do not paraphrase until you can quote.
- The submission (executive summaries plus an essay, optional video,
  optional documentation form) is **read before** the interview. As of
  the 2026 season the documentation form is submitted with the portal,
  not carried into the room. Confirm on the official pages; the
  process moves.
- Judges use written
  [judging guidelines](https://www.firstinspires.org/resources/library/frc/fia-resources)
  and a
  [definitions list](https://www.firstinspires.org/resources/library/frc/fia-resources)
  so words like “reach” and “partner” mean something. You are
  responsible for the words you chose.
- They compare teams at that event, not against a Hall of Fame essay
  you wish you had written. They will try to **verify** claims. A
  number without a source is a liability.
- Student voice matters. A mentor-polished corporate tone with
  students who cannot explain a paragraph is a tell.

Pick **three pillars** the team can prove (for example: rookie support,
school pipeline, industry partnership). For each pillar:

- **Need** — what was missing before you started.
- **Action** — what students did, with dates and partners.
- **Result** — a number or a named change you can defend from the
  evidence log.
- **Sustainability** — what continues if this year’s seniors graduate.
  That last one is succession. If you need the leadership version,
  read
  [Mentorship & Succession](../../leadership/mentorship-succession/).

If a pillar is only “we visited a classroom once,” it is an anecdote.
Cut it or grow it before you write.

The essay:

- Follow this season’s official character/page limits **exactly**.
  Over-length is unread. As of 2026 the essay cap is 10,000 characters
  including spaces, and each executive summary is 500 characters —
  confirm on
  [Impact Award Resources](https://www.firstinspires.org/resources/library/frc/fia-resources)
  before you draft.
- Students write. Mentors edit for clarity and rules, not for a
  corporate tone.
- Open with the claim. Do not bury the lede under a history of FIRST.
- Every paragraph should earn the next. If a sentence only restates
  the mission, delete it.

Supporting documents and the file:

- Photos with captions, letters from partners, and the one-page
  budget snapshot judges always ask about (from
  [Season Budget](../season-budget/)).
- A short executive sheet the interview team can hold: pillars,
  numbers, who speaks to what.
- Version control. One “final” folder in the **team’s drive**. No six
  copies named `impact_FINAL2`.

Read one Hall of Fame or regional winner so you know what “argument,
not scrapbook” looks like. Start at the
[Hall of Fame](https://www.firstinspires.org/resources/library/frc/hall-of-fame)
or
[Impact Award winners](https://www.firstinspires.org/resources/library/frc/fia-winners).
You are not copying their programs. You are noticing that every
paragraph has a date, a partner, and a result.

This website does not host essays, videos, or documentation forms.
Work stays in the team drive and the FIRST Dashboard. Mentors review
links. If your team exported these tickets, the issue gets the outline
link, not the essay pasted into a public tracker.

The next ticket, [Judge Interviews & Pit Talks](../judge-interviews/),
is how the paper survives contact with a human.

## Prerequisites

- All Advocate and Veteran Advocate lessons in this track.
- A running impact file from
  [Measuring Community Impact](../measuring-impact/).
- A pursuit sheet from
  [The FRC Awards Landscape](../awards-landscape/) that says you are
  submitting Impact this year, with named owners.
- Dashboard access for the student award submitter or a mentor who
  will submit. Submission how-to:
  [Awards Submission Tips](https://www.firstinspires.org/resources/library/frc/award-tips).

## What you'll learn

- How Impact is judged: paper first, verify second, interview third.
- How to structure an essay around evidence, not adjectives.
- What supporting documents are for.
- How to keep student voice without losing the facts.

## Tasks

1. **Read this season’s rules, not last year’s memory.** Open
   [Submitted Awards](https://www.firstinspires.org/resources/library/frc/submitted-awards),
   [Impact Award Resources](https://www.firstinspires.org/resources/library/frc/fia-resources),
   and
   [Awards Submission Tips](https://www.firstinspires.org/resources/library/frc/award-tips).
   Write down: character limits, required pieces (summaries, essay,
   optional video, optional documentation form), who is allowed to
   click Submit, and this year’s deadline. If a teammate “knows the
   limits,” ignore them until the page agrees.

2. **Read one winning submission.** Pick one essay from the
   [Hall of Fame](https://www.firstinspires.org/resources/library/frc/hall-of-fame)
   or a regional winner on
   [Impact Award winners](https://www.firstinspires.org/resources/library/frc/fia-winners).
   Write three notes: *a claim they proved*, *a number they sourced*,
   *a paragraph that would fall apart if a student could not explain
   it*. You will steal the discipline, not the programs.

3. **Write a one-page outline.** Three pillars. Each pillar has need,
   action, result, sustainability, and a pointer into the evidence
   folder (a file name or a row). If a pillar has no pointer, it is
   not a pillar. Put the outline in the team drive.

4. **Draft one pillar at full essay quality.** Not the whole essay.
   One pillar, at the density the character limit requires, in
   student voice. Then have **two students who did not write it**
   fact-check every number and every name against the evidence file.
   They write “confirmed” or “cut” on each claim. Mentors may fix
   grammar after the facts survive.

5. **Assemble the supporting-doc checklist.** Against this season’s
   official packet: documentation form (if you are using it), photo
   captions, partner letters, budget snapshot, interview one-pager
   (pillars, numbers, who speaks). Check boxes in the drive. Missing
   items get an owner and a date, not a hope.

6. **Name the interview team and a backup for each speaker.** Three
   pillars, one student each, plus a backup who can take any chair.
   Write who answers budget, who answers “what failed,” who answers
   “what happens when you graduate.” The next ticket rehearses them.
   This ticket names them.

7. **Mentor review, then stop or continue.** A mentor reads the
   outline, the one pillar, the fact-check notes, and the checklist.
   Together you decide whether to draft the remaining pillars in
   later sittings. Closing this ticket does **not** require a
   submitted essay. It requires a submission that could survive a
   judge if you finished the remaining pillars the same way.
   Submission itself happens on the FIRST Dashboard, by the people
   the official page allows, before the official deadline.

## Acceptance Criteria

- [ ] This season’s character limits, required pieces, submitter
      role, and deadline are written down from the official pages.
- [ ] A one-page outline exists with three pillars, each with need /
      action / result / sustainability and a pointer into the
      evidence file.
- [ ] One pillar is drafted at full essay quality and two students
      who did not write it have fact-checked every claim.
- [ ] A supporting-doc checklist exists against this season’s
      official packet, with owners for missing items.
- [ ] The interview team and a backup for each speaker are named,
      including who answers budget, failure, and succession.
- [ ] Work lives in the **team’s drive** (and, when it is time, the
      FIRST Dashboard). This website has no essay copy.
- [ ] A mentor can point at one claim in the drafted pillar and find
      the evidence in under two minutes.

## Notes

- Character limits include spaces and punctuation. Draft long, then
  cut. Do not draft to the cap and hope.
- Mentors who rewrite the essay in adult voice are doing the team a
  disservice. The interview will expose it.
- An optional video is optional. A video students cannot narrate is
  worse than no video.
- Do not upload partner letters or photos of minors to this website
  or to a public GitHub issue.
- The next ticket ([Judge Interviews & Pit Talks](../judge-interviews/))
  is the 6-minute talk. Bring the one-pager from Task 5.
