## Description

Sponsors are not ATMs. They are people buying a specific return: community
goodwill, student talent, a logo on a robot, or a story they can tell
internally. If you have never asked anyone for money, that sentence is the
whole lesson. You are not begging. You are offering a trade you can keep.

You already have a [one-sentence pitch](../team-story-brand/) and a
[season budget](../season-budget/) in the team’s drive. This ticket is how
those two artifacts become a package. The next ticket,
[Sponsor Outreach & Stewardship](../sponsor-outreach/), is how you send it.
Do not send anything until you can say what the other person is buying.

Use the word that matches the relationship:

- A **donation** is a gift. Thank them. Do not invent benefits you will
  forget. A $250 pizza gift is a donation. Calling it a “title
  sponsorship” trains people to ignore you.
- A **sponsorship** is a trade: money or in-kind goods for **named**
  benefits (logo on a named surface, a dated shop visit, a mid-season
  report). If you cannot deliver the benefit, it is not a tier. It is a
  hope.
- A **grant** is a proposal against a funder’s written criteria, often
  with a report at the end. The criteria are not a suggestion. Read them
  before you write.

What a sponsor actually buys is rarely “a robot.” It is one or more of:

- **Visibility** they can show a boss (logo, photo, a sentence in a
  newsletter).
- **Community credit** they can show a town (students taught, a shop
  visit, a named program).
- **Talent** (interns, future hires, a reason employees volunteer).
- **A story** they can tell internally without you in the room.

If you cannot name which of those you are selling, you are asking for
charity and calling it a partnership. Charity is fine when it is a
donation. Do not dress it up.

Research before the ask. For each prospect, write a **research card**
with four bullets:

- What they already fund (education, manufacturing, local youth).
- Who the right person is (not `info@`).
- What you can honestly offer that matches their goals.
- A specific ask tied to a **budget line** from
  [Season Budget](../season-budget/) (“travel to the district
  championship,” not “support robotics”).

Cold volume without research is spam. Ten researched asks beat a hundred
generic PDFs. FIRST’s
[Fundraising Toolkit](https://www.firstinspires.org/resources/library/fundraising-toolkit)
has a whole section on community analysis and sponsor documentation for
this reason. Read the landing page, then do the card. Do not wait for a
perfect packet.

A fair package is small and kept:

- **Logo on the robot / banner / website** — only if you will actually
  apply it and send a photo the week it goes on.
- **A shop visit or demo** — on a dated calendar, with a student host.
- **A mid-season and end-of-season update** — two emails, not a 20-page
  deck.

Never sell FIRST marks you cannot license. Read
[FIRST Brand & Assets](https://www.firstinspires.org/about/brand). Never
sell student time you cannot staff. Under-promise. Look at how
[Team 254 asks](https://www.team254.com/sponsorus/) and how they
[list current sponsors](https://www.team254.com/sponsors/) — a public
trade, not a mystery. Your tiers will be smaller. The honesty should
not be.

**This website does not store sponsor lists.** It does not store
research cards, contact names, or dollar amounts. The card lives in the
**team’s drive**, next to the budget. If you keep a personal list on
your phone, copy it to the shared drive before you graduate, or the
relationship leaves with you.

## Prerequisites

- [Team Story & Brand](../team-story-brand/) completed (pitch and
  paragraph you will reuse on the card).
- [Season Budget](../season-budget/) completed (a sheet with real lines
  you can point at).
- Access to the team’s drive. You need a place for the research card
  that is not this website.

## What you'll learn

- The difference between a donation, a sponsorship, and a grant.
- What a sponsor is actually buying when they say yes.
- How to research a company before you ask.
- How to build tiers you can actually fulfill, tied to the budget.

## Tasks

1. **Learn the official sequence.** Skim
   [FIRST Fundraising Toolkit](https://www.firstinspires.org/resources/library/fundraising-toolkit)
   (plan, community analysis, the ask, the thank-you) and the
   cost-and-fundraising block on
   [FRC Team Management Resources](https://www.firstinspires.org/resources/library/frc/team-management-resources).
   Write three labels in a note: *donation*, *sponsorship*, *grant*,
   each with one sentence in your own words.

2. **Classify three past supporters.** From the adult owner or last
   year’s notes, pick three real supporters (a company, a family, a
   school account, a grant). Mark each donation, sponsorship, or grant,
   and write one line about what they received. If they received
   nothing, write “nothing — we still owe a thank-you.” Put this in
   the team drive, not here.

3. **See a public package.** Read
   [Team 254 — Sponsor Us](https://www.team254.com/sponsorus/) and
   [Team 254 — Sponsors](https://www.team254.com/sponsors/). Write two
   sentences: *What a sponsor of that team is buying* and *What we can
   honestly sell this season*. Do not copy their tier names or dollar
   amounts. Copy the idea that the trade is visible.

4. **Write one research card.** Pick one **new** prospect (or a lapsed
   one you have not asked this year). In the team drive, create a
   one-page card with:

   - organization name and what they already fund
   - the right person (or “unknown — next step is to find them”)
   - the benefit you can deliver that matches their goals
   - a specific ask tied to a named budget line and a number from the
     sheet
   - the pitch sentence from [Team Story & Brand](../team-story-brand/)
     at the top

   Public sources only: their site, a “community” or “education” page,
   a news mention. Do not scrape employees’ personal social accounts.

5. **Draft a three-tier package.** One page. Three tiers. Each tier
   lists benefits you can deliver **this season** and the budget line
   it funds. Mark every benefit **already deliverable** or **not this
   year**. If “logo on the robot” is not already deliverable, it is
   not in the gold tier. Tie the middle tier to the line you used on
   the research card.

6. **Check FIRST marks.** Confirm you are not selling the FIRST logo
   as if it were yours. One sentence on the package page: *FIRST marks
   used only as allowed by* the
   [brand guidelines](https://www.firstinspires.org/about/brand).

7. **Hand the card and package to a mentor.** Share the drive links.
   A mentor should be able to read the card in two minutes and say
   whether the ask matches the budget line. If your team exported
   these tickets, paste the links on the issue.

## Acceptance Criteria

- [ ] You can explain, in your own words, donation vs. sponsorship vs.
      grant.
- [ ] Three past supporters are classified, with one line each about
      what they received (or that a thank-you is still owed).
- [ ] A one-page research card exists in the **team’s drive** with the
      four bullets, a budget-line ask, and the team pitch. This website
      does not have a copy.
- [ ] A three-tier package exists; every benefit is marked already
      deliverable or not this year; each tier points at a budget line.
- [ ] The package does not sell FIRST marks the team cannot license.
- [ ] A mentor read the card and can repeat the specific ask and the
      return the prospect is buying.

## Notes

- A research card is not permission to send the email. Sending is the
  next ticket. Mentors review before anything leaves the team.
- Do not promise a tax receipt unless the adult owner has confirmed
  the team or school can issue one. Wrong tax language is how you
  lose a sponsor and a school administrator in the same week.
- In-kind goods are real (stock, machine time, food). Put them on the
  budget as a line with an estimated value so the package stays
  honest.
- The next ticket ([Sponsor Outreach & Stewardship](../sponsor-outreach/))
  is the letter, the meeting, and the update after the check clears.
  Bring the card. Do not start a second card on your laptop.
