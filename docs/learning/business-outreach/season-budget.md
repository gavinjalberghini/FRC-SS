## Overview

A budget is a strategy document. It says what the team will and will not spend
money on this year. This lesson covers how to build one that a mentor and a
student lead can both defend, and how to track it so May is not a surprise.

## Prerequisites

- [Why Business Work Matters](../why-business-matters/)

## What you'll learn

- The major cost categories of an FRC season.
- How to separate committed costs from bets.
- How to track actuals without waiting for a year-end panic.

## Unit 1 — The cost of a season

Most teams spend in the same buckets. Name them explicitly:

- **FIRST and event costs** — team registration, event registration, FIRST
  Championship if you qualify.
- **Travel** — transport, lodging, food, chaperones.
- **Robot** — COTS, raw stock, electronics, printing, spare parts.
- **Shop & tools** — replacements, consumables, safety gear.
- **Outreach & awards** — printing, giveaways, Impact production.
- **Reserve** — the money you do not spend so a blown motor or a second hotel
  night does not end the season.

If a line is missing, the budget is fiction.

## Unit 2 — Committed vs. optional

- **Committed** costs happen if the team exists (registration, insurance,
  baseline travel). Fund these first.
- **Optional** costs are bets (an extra regional, a nicer CNC, a video
  shooter). Fund them only after committed costs and the reserve are real.
- **Do not hide** student-paid travel as “free.” If families pay, that is still
  a cost — and an equity issue.

## Unit 3 — Tracking

- One spreadsheet (or bookkeeping tool) with **budgeted**, **committed**, and
  **spent** columns.
- A student owner and an adult owner. Dual control is not distrust; it is how
  you catch mistakes.
- A monthly 15-minute review. A budget you never reopen is a wish list.

## Steps & acceptance criteria

- [ ] List every cost category above with at least one real line item.
- [ ] Mark each line committed, optional, or reserve.
- [ ] Enter three actual expenses (even small ones) into a tracking sheet.
- [ ] Explain who can approve a purchase over a stated amount.
