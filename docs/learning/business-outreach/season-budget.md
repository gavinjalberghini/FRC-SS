## Description

A budget is a strategy document. It says what the team will and will not
spend money on this year. After
[Team Story & Brand](../team-story-brand/) you have a sentence about who
you are. This ticket is the number that sentence has to fund.

You do not need to have asked a sponsor for money. You do need to know
what you would be asking *for*. A sponsor who hears “support robotics”
hears a bottomless ask. A sponsor who hears “district championship
travel, currently $X in the sheet” hears a line item. The next ticket,
[Sponsorship Fundamentals](../sponsorship-fundamentals/), is unusable
without this sheet.

Most teams spend in the same buckets. Name them explicitly:

- **FIRST and event costs** — team registration, event registration,
  FIRST Championship if you qualify. Read this season’s numbers on
  [Cost & Registration](https://www.firstinspires.org/programs/cost-and-registration).
  Do not copy a number from last year’s memory.
- **Travel** — transport, lodging, food, chaperones.
- **Robot** — COTS, raw stock, electronics, printing, spare parts.
- **Shop & tools** — replacements, consumables, safety gear.
- **Outreach & awards** — printing, giveaways, Impact production.
- **Reserve** — the money you do not spend so a blown motor or a second
  hotel night does not end the season.

If a line is missing, the budget is fiction. FIRST’s
[Fundraising Toolkit](https://www.firstinspires.org/resources/library/fundraising-toolkit)
starts with “creating a budget” for the same reason: you cannot raise
toward a vibe.

Separate **committed** costs from **optional** costs:

- **Committed** costs happen if the team exists (registration, insurance,
  baseline travel). Fund these first.
- **Optional** costs are bets (an extra regional, a nicer CNC, a video
  shooter). Fund them only after committed costs and the reserve are
  real.
- **Do not hide** student-paid travel as “free.” If families pay, that
  is still a cost — and an equity issue. Write it down. A team that
  pretends travel is free will lose students it never intended to lose.

Tracking is how May is not a surprise:

- One spreadsheet (or the bookkeeping tool the school already uses)
  with **budgeted**, **committed** (PO or verbal yes), and **spent**
  columns.
- A student owner and an adult owner. Dual control is not distrust; it
  is how you catch a missed decimal.
- A monthly 15-minute review. A budget you never reopen is a wish list.

Scope and money are the same skill. If that sentence is new, skim
[Managing Expectations](../../leadership/managing-expectations/) after
you build the sheet.

**This website does not host the spreadsheet.** It does not store
dollar amounts, vendor names, or student work. Build the sheet in the
**team’s drive** (or the school bookkeeping tool the mentors already
use). Mentors review a link, not an upload to this playbook.

If a sheet already exists, do not start a second one. Add the columns
this ticket asks for, or write a one-page cover that maps the existing
sheet onto the categories above. Two budgets is how money disappears.

## Prerequisites

- [Why Business Work Matters](../why-business-matters/) completed.
- [Team Story & Brand](../team-story-brand/) completed — you will tie
  later asks to this sheet, and the pitch should not invent a dollar
  amount the sheet cannot show.
- Edit access to the team’s drive, or a mentor willing to create a
  sheet you can both open. You do not need last year’s full books; you
  need enough to put a real number in three lines.

## What you'll learn

- The major cost categories of an FRC season.
- How to separate committed costs from bets.
- How to track actuals without waiting for a year-end panic — in the
  team’s system of record, not on this website.

## Tasks

1. **Read the official fees.** Open
   [FIRST Cost & Registration](https://www.firstinspires.org/programs/cost-and-registration)
   and write this season’s team registration fee and at least one
   additional-event or Championship fee into a scratch note. Skim the
   “Step 1 — Creating a Budget” framing on the
   [Fundraising Toolkit](https://www.firstinspires.org/resources/library/fundraising-toolkit)
   landing page. You are not filling FIRST’s templates unless a mentor
   asks. You are learning why a budget comes before an ask.

2. **Open or create the season sheet.** In the **team’s drive**, open
   the existing season budget. If there is none, create a spreadsheet
   named for this season (for example `2027-season-budget`). Columns
   at minimum:

   - category (the six buckets above)
   - line item
   - committed / optional / reserve
   - budgeted
   - committed (PO or promise)
   - spent
   - notes (who approved, where the receipt lives)

   Put your name and an adult owner in a header cell. Dual control
   starts as two names, not a speech.

3. **Fill every category with at least one real line.** Registration
   gets the number from Task 1. Travel, robot, shop, outreach, and
   reserve each get at least one line with a number you can defend
   (last year’s actual, a quote, or “unknown — need mentor”). An
   empty category is not conservative. It is a lie of omission.
   Mark each line **committed**, **optional**, or **reserve**.

4. **Enter three actual expenses.** Even small ones count: a hardware
   store run, a print job, a registration deposit. Put them in
   **spent** with a date and a note about where the receipt is. If
   the team has no receipts yet this month, enter three from the last
   season the adult owner can confirm. Tracking is a habit. Start it.

5. **Write the approval rule.** In the sheet header or a second tab,
   write who can approve a purchase over a stated amount (for example:
   “under $50: student lead + Slack note; $50–$250: adult owner; over
   $250: adult owner and lead mentor”). If the school already has a
   rule, copy it. Do not invent a looser one.

6. **Name the hidden family cost.** Add one line for student-paid
   travel or dues, even if the team does not call it a budget line.
   Write in the notes whether it is a barrier. You are not solving
   equity in this sitting. You are refusing to call it free.

7. **Hand the link to a mentor.** Share the drive link. Walk them
   through one committed line, one optional line, and the reserve.
   If your team exported these tickets, paste the link on the issue.
   Do not paste dollar amounts into this website.

## Acceptance Criteria

- [ ] A season budget spreadsheet exists in the **team’s drive** (not
      this website) with budgeted, committed, and spent columns.
- [ ] Every cost category above has at least one real line item.
- [ ] Each line is marked committed, optional, or reserve.
- [ ] Three actual expenses are entered with a date and a receipt
      note a mentor can find.
- [ ] The sheet names a student owner, an adult owner, and who can
      approve a purchase over a stated amount.
- [ ] Student-paid travel or dues appears as a line, not as “free.”
- [ ] A mentor opened the link and heard you defend one committed
      line, one optional line, and the reserve.

## Notes

- This playbook will never be the team’s books. Do not upload bank
  details, tax IDs, or family payment lists here. Those stay in the
  school or team system the adult owner already uses.
- A reserve you spend in January is not a reserve. Protect it in
  writing.
- Optional lines are allowed. Unlabeled optional lines that quietly
  eat the reserve are not.
- The next ticket ([Sponsorship Fundamentals](../sponsorship-fundamentals/))
  teaches what a sponsor actually buys. You will tie each package tier
  to a line on this sheet. If the sheet is fiction, the package is
  fiction.
