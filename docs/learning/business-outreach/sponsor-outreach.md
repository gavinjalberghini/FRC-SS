## Overview

The package is worthless if nobody sends it — and the check is wasted if you
disappear until next January. This lesson is the working loop: write, meet,
deliver, report.

## Prerequisites

- All Advocate lessons.

## What you'll learn

- How to write a short outreach letter that a human will finish.
- How to run a 20-minute sponsor meeting.
- How stewardship works after the money arrives.

## Unit 1 — The letter

Keep it to one page:

1. Who you are (the pitch from Lesson 2).
2. What you did last season that a stranger can picture.
3. The specific ask and what it funds.
4. The benefit you will deliver, and by when.
5. A clear next step (a date, a phone number, a visit).

Students sign it. Mentors may review; they should not ghostwrite. A letter
that sounds like a brochure gets filed with the brochures.

## Unit 2 — The meeting

- Two students, one adult in the background. Students lead.
- Bring the robot or a mechanism if you can; photos if you cannot.
- Ask what *they* care about before you pitch the gold tier.
- Write down every promise before you leave the parking lot.

If they say no, thank them and ask who else they would introduce you to. A
polite no is still a relationship.

## Unit 3 — Stewardship

The season after the check is how you earn the next one:

- Send the photo of their logo on the robot the week it goes on.
- Invite them to a practice or an event with a specific time, not “come
  anytime.”
- Close the year with a one-page report: what their money did, who it
  reached, what is next.
- Keep a contact log so a graduating lead does not take the relationship
  with them.

## Steps & acceptance criteria

- [ ] Write a one-page ask for a real prospect and have a lead mark it up
      once.
- [ ] Role-play a 20-minute meeting; students talk at least 80% of the
      minutes.
- [ ] Send (or draft and queue) a stewardship update to an existing
      supporter.
- [ ] Add or update one sponsor record with contact, amount, and promised
      benefits.
