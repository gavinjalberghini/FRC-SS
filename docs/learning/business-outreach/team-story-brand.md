## Description

Sponsors, judges, and new members all ask the same first question: *who are
you?* If the answer changes with whoever is holding the flyer, the team does
not have a story — it has opinions. This ticket produces the short, reusable
language and visual rules everything else in this track is built on.

You already know, from
[Why Business Work Matters](../why-business-matters/), that a story is a
requirement. Now you will write it. You do not need to have asked a sponsor
for money. You need a sentence a busy adult can remember after the handshake.

A **one-sentence pitch** is not a slogan and it is not the mission statement.
It is the answer you give when a parent in the hallway, a judge in the pit,
or a plant manager at a shop visit says “so what do you do?” It has three
jobs, in one breath:

- **Who** we are (students, school or community, FRC team number and name).
- **What** we do (design, build, and compete with a robot — and teach).
- **Why** it matters (STEM access, student leadership, a named community,
  not “we love STEM”).

Write it, then say it out loud. If you need a breath in the middle, it is
too long. Eight seconds is a useful ceiling. Empty superlatives
(“world-class,” “passionate,” “innovative”) make the sentence longer and
the memory worse. A number, a place, or a named program makes it shorter
and the memory better.

Nonprofit communicators teach the same shape. Skim
[The 3 Core Messages Every Nonprofit Needs](https://wwpr.org/the-three-core-messages-every-nonprofit-needs/)
for the “what we do in one sentence” test, and
[The Fundamentals of Storytelling for Nonprofits](https://www.marketingmission.org/blog/the-fundamentals-of-storytelling-for-nonprofits)
for how a one-liner sits inside a longer story. You are not a nonprofit
copywriter. You are stealing the discipline: who, what, why, no jargon.

The **one-paragraph story** expands the pitch into four beats, no more:

1. Who the team is and where it lives.
2. What students actually do (not “we love STEM” — name the shop, the
   classroom, the rookie team, the robot).
3. Who is helped besides the people on the drive team.
4. What you are asking for *or* what you want the reader to remember.

This paragraph is the body of a sponsor letter, a website hero, and the
first answer in a judge interview. Keep it factual. If you cannot point
at the evidence, cut the claim. The later tickets on
[Measuring Community Impact](../measuring-impact/) and
[The Impact Award Submission](../impact-award/) will punish adjectives
that the file cannot defend. Start honest now.

**Brand** is consistency, not decoration:

- **One name, one number, one logo lockup.** Do not invent a new wordmark
  per event.
- **A short color and type list** so posters, slides, and shirts look
  related.
- **A voice:** direct, specific, student-written.

FIRST marks are not yours to restyle. Read
[FIRST Brand & Assets](https://www.firstinspires.org/about/brand) and the
[Branding & Design Guidelines](https://info.firstinspires.org/hubfs/web/about/policy/first-brand-guidelines.pdf)
before you put a FIRST chevron on a flyer. You may use FIRST names and
artwork the way those documents allow. You may not stretch the logo,
recolor it, or sell a “FIRST sponsorship” you cannot license.

Look at how established teams put the same story on a public page. You
are not copying their voice. You are noticing that
[Team 254’s about page](https://www.team254.com/about/),
[Citrus Circuits’ about page](https://www.citruscircuits.org/about),
and [Mechanical Advantage’s home page](https://www.littletonrobotics.org/)
each answer who / what / where in a few sentences a stranger can finish.
Your paragraph should do the same for *this* team.

Paraphrase this playbook’s [Mission & Values](../../../mission/). Do not
replace it. The pitch and the paragraph have to be sentences a teammate
who is not on business can deliver without notes.

This site does not track whether you finished and does not store the
pitch. Keep the writing in the team’s drive. Mentors review it the same
way they review robot code: against written acceptance criteria.

The next ticket, [Season Budget](../season-budget/), is the number the
pitch will eventually have to fund.

## Prerequisites

- [Why Business Work Matters](../why-business-matters/) completed (you
  can say why this trade is not extra credit).
- The [Mission & Values](../../../mission/) page, read once.
- Access to the team’s existing logo file, colors, and one recent flyer
  or social post. Ask a lead if you do not have them.

## What you'll learn

- How to write a one-sentence pitch and a one-paragraph origin story.
- What a brand is when you are not a company (type, color, voice).
- How to keep every public artifact on the same page — including FIRST
  marks you did not design.

## Tasks

1. **Hear a pitch that works.** Read
   [The 3 Core Messages Every Nonprofit Needs](https://wwpr.org/the-three-core-messages-every-nonprofit-needs/)
   through the “What We Do (In One Sentence)” section. Then read one
   about page from an FRC team that is not yours:
   [Team 254](https://www.team254.com/about/),
   [Citrus Circuits](https://www.citruscircuits.org/about), or
   [Mechanical Advantage](https://www.littletonrobotics.org/). Write two
   notes: *What I can repeat after one read* and *What I still do not
   know*. The first note is the job of a pitch.

2. **Write the one-sentence pitch.** Draft a single sentence that names
   who you are, what you do, and why it matters. Say it out loud and
   time it. If it runs past eight seconds, cut a clause. If it uses
   “passionate,” “world-class,” or “innovative” and you cannot point at
   the evidence, cut the word. Put the sentence at the top of a doc in
   the team’s drive titled something like `team-pitch-yourname`.

3. **Write the one-paragraph story.** Expand the pitch into the four
   beats above. Cap it at about 120 words. A teammate who is not on
   business should be able to read it once and retell it. Hand it to
   that teammate. If they invent a fact you did not write, the paragraph
   is vague — rewrite.

4. **List the visual rules.** In the same doc, write the **name**,
   **number**, **logo file location** (in the team’s drive, not this
   site), and **two colors** (name plus a hex or swatch if you have
   them) that every public artifact should use. Add one line of voice:
   for example, “direct, specific, student-written; no empty
   superlatives.”

5. **Check FIRST marks.** Skim
   [FIRST Brand & Assets](https://www.firstinspires.org/about/brand)
   and the first pages of the
   [Branding & Design Guidelines PDF](https://info.firstinspires.org/hubfs/web/about/policy/first-brand-guidelines.pdf).
   Write one sentence: *We may …* and one sentence: *We may not …*
   about the FIRST logo on a team flyer.

6. **Mark one miss.** Find one existing flyer, slide, shirt, or post
   that breaks the rules from Task 4 or Task 5. Write how you would
   fix it (wrong logo, extra nickname, off-brand color, FIRST mark
   stretched). You do not have to reprint it today. You have to be able
   to see the break.

7. **Hand it to a mentor.** Send the doc URL (team drive, not this
   website) to the business mentor or lead. If your team exported these
   tickets, paste that URL on the issue and move it to In Review. Speak
   the pitch once in front of them.

## Acceptance Criteria

- [ ] A one-sentence pitch exists that a mentor timed under eight
      seconds spoken, and that names who, what, and why.
- [ ] A one-paragraph story exists (about 120 words, four beats) that a
      teammate who is not on business can deliver without notes.
- [ ] The same doc lists the team name, number, logo file location, two
      colors, and one voice rule.
- [ ] You wrote one “we may” and one “we may not” about FIRST marks
      after reading the brand guidelines.
- [ ] You marked one existing flyer or post that breaks those rules and
      said how to fix it.
- [ ] A mentor has the doc URL in the team’s drive.

## Notes

- Do not put a personal phone number, home address, or student last
  names in the public paragraph. Communications rules come later in
  [Communications & Media](../communications-media/); start clean.
- This website does not store the pitch, the brand kit, or sponsor
  language. If you typed a draft in a personal notes app, copy it to
  the team drive before you call the ticket done.
- The next ticket ([Season Budget](../season-budget/)) builds the
  spreadsheet that tells you what the paragraph is asking for. Do not
  invent a dollar amount in the story until that sheet exists.
