## Overview

Sponsors, judges, and new members all ask the same first question: *who are
you?* If the answer changes with whoever is holding the flyer, the team does
not have a story — it has opinions. This lesson produces the short, reusable
language and visual rules everything else is built on.

## Prerequisites

- [Why Business Work Matters](../why-business-matters/)
- The [mission]({{ '/mission/' | relative_url }}) page

## What you'll learn

- How to write a one-sentence pitch and a one-paragraph origin story.
- What a brand is when you are not a company (type, color, voice).
- How to keep every public artifact on the same page.

## Unit 1 — The one-sentence pitch

A pitch a busy adult can remember:

- **Who** we are (students, school or community, FRC).
- **What** we do (design, build, and compete with a robot — and teach).
- **Why** it matters (STEM access, student leadership, community).

Write it, then say it out loud. If you need a breath in the middle, it is too
long.

## Unit 2 — The one-paragraph story

Expand the pitch into four beats, no more:

1. Who the team is and where it lives.
2. What students actually do (not “we love STEM”).
3. Who is helped besides the people on the drive team.
4. What you are asking for *or* what you want the reader to remember.

This paragraph is the body of a sponsor letter, a website hero, and the first
answer in a judge interview. Keep it factual.

## Unit 3 — Visual and voice rules

Brand is consistency, not decoration:

- **One name, one number, one logo lockup.** Do not invent a new wordmark per
  event.
- **A short color and type list** so posters, slides, and shirts look related.
- **A voice:** direct, specific, student-written. No empty superlatives
  (“world-class,” “passionate”) unless you can point at the evidence.

## Steps & acceptance criteria

- [ ] Write a one-sentence pitch and time it under eight seconds spoken.
- [ ] Write a one-paragraph story that a teammate who is not on business can
      deliver without notes.
- [ ] List the name, number, logo file, and two colors that every public
      artifact should use.
- [ ] Mark one existing flyer or post that breaks those rules and say how to
      fix it.
