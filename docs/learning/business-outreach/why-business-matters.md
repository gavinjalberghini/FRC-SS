## Description

A robot that never ships, a team that cannot travel, and a shop that nobody
outside the school has heard of are the same problem: the non-technical work
did not get done. This ticket is the start of the business track. It exists
so that every later ticket — a pitch, a budget, a sponsor email, an Impact
essay — has a reason to exist.

You do not need to have asked anyone for money. Most people who join this
trade have not. The job is not “being good at talking.” The job is treating
funding, story, and community as requirements, the same way electrical treats
a breaker and programming treats a deploy.

The public product of an FRC team is bigger than the machine:

- **A funded season** — registration, travel, parts, and tools. FIRST
  publishes current fees on
  [Cost & Registration](https://www.firstinspires.org/programs/cost-and-registration).
  A robot that stays in the shop because the team could not pay an event
  invoice is not a technical failure.
- **A story** judges, sponsors, and new members can understand in one minute.
  If the answer to “who are you?” changes with whoever is holding the flyer,
  the team does not have a story. It has opinions.
- **A community** that would notice if the team disappeared. Mentoring a
  rookie team, running a classroom demo, or thanking a machine shop by name
  is how a team becomes something other than a club that meets in a trailer.

If any one of those fails, the robot does not get a full season. Treat them
as requirements, not extras. Business work is not extra credit. It is how
the season happens.

The [Mission & Values](../../../mission/) page says technical and
non-technical roles are equally valued. In practice that means:

- Business deadlines are as real as a mill slot. A late sponsor packet is a
  missed cycle, the same way a late bumper is a missed cycle.
- Mentors coach this trade the same way they coach CAD: questions first,
  then help. They do not ghostwrite your thank-you note any more than they
  CAD your tube for you.
- Veterans and leads exist here too. See the
  [org chart](../../../org-chart/). Lead Advocate is a co-captain, not a
  side quest.

Gracious Professionalism® shows up off the field. Read FIRST’s
[Vision & Mission](https://www.firstinspires.org/about/vision-and-mission)
for the official language, then practice the small version:

- Thank people by name, on time, for specific help.
- Represent the team at school, at events, and online as if a judge were in
  the room — because one might be.
- Share what you learn with nearby teams. Impact is not a zero-sum award.
  A team you helped this fall is evidence next spring, and it is also the
  point.

“Everyone contributes” is easy to say when the work is a mill. It is the
same sentence when the work is a spreadsheet, a thank-you, or a Saturday
at a middle school. If you would not ship a robot with a missing gearbox,
do not ship a season with a missing budget owner.

This site does not track whether you finished. It does not store your
thank-you notes, sponsor names, or homework. If your team exported these
tickets into its own GitHub, close this issue there once a mentor accepts
the criteria below. Keep the writing in the team’s drive or your own notes.

The next ticket, [Team Story & Brand](../team-story-brand/), is where you
write the one-sentence pitch a busy adult can remember.

## Prerequisites

- None. This is the starting point for every business-team member.
- A school or personal place to write (a doc in the team’s drive is
  enough). You do not need a sponsor list and you should not create one
  on this website.

## What you'll learn

- Why funding, story, and community are part of building a robot — not
  extras you do after the robot is done.
- How Gracious Professionalism® shows up off the field.
- What “everyone contributes” means when the work is a spreadsheet, not
  a mill.

## Tasks

1. **Read why FIRST talks about more than robots.** Skim
   [FIRST: Vision & Mission](https://www.firstinspires.org/about/vision-and-mission)
   and this playbook’s [Mission & Values](../../../mission/) page. Write
   three sentences in a scratch note you will keep: *A funded season is
   …*, *A story is …*, and *A community is …*. Use your own words. If you
   only restate the mission, rewrite until a teammate on mechanical would
   recognize the shop.

2. **Look at what a season actually costs.** Open
   [FIRST Cost & Registration](https://www.firstinspires.org/programs/cost-and-registration)
   and write down this season’s **team registration** fee and one
   **additional event** fee. You are not building the budget yet — that
   is [Season Budget](../season-budget/). You are proving to yourself that
   the robot is not free. Add one sentence: *Without business work, we
   cannot …* that names a real invoice, not a vibe.

3. **Name equal-weight work.** Ask a lead or look at this month’s board.
   Write one **technical** deliverable and one **non-technical**
   deliverable that have the same deadline weight (for example: bumper
   fabric due Friday, and a sponsor thank-you due Friday). If the team
   has been treating the second one as optional, say so. That is the
   habit this ticket is for.

4. **Write a two-sentence thank-you.** Pick a real person who helped the
   team — a mentor, a teacher, a shop teacher who cut stock, a parent who
   drove, a company that sent a box of parts. Write two sentences that
   name them, name the specific help, and say what it let students do.
   “Thanks for your support” is not a thank-you. “Thank you for loaning
   the mill last Tuesday so four students could cut the intake plates
   before Thursday’s practice” is. You do not have to send it yet. A
   mentor will tell you if the real person should receive it.

5. **Say it out loud.** In under a minute, tell a mentor or a teammate
   who is not on business: three things the team cannot do without
   business work, and why this trade is not extra credit. If you need
   notes, the sentences from Task 1 are the notes.

6. **Hand it to a mentor.** Put the scratch note (three sentences, the
   two fees, the equal-weight pair, and the thank-you) in the team’s
   drive or paste it on the exported issue. This website does not collect
   it. If your team exported these tickets, move the card to In Review.

## Acceptance Criteria

- [ ] You can explain, in your own words, three things the team cannot
      do without business work (funded season, story, community — or
      sharper local versions).
- [ ] You can name this season’s team registration fee from FIRST’s
      cost page and one additional event fee.
- [ ] You named one technical and one non-technical deliverable that
      have the same deadline weight this month.
- [ ] A two-sentence thank-you exists that names a real person, the
      specific help, and what it let students do. A mentor has read it.
- [ ] A mentor heard you say, in under a minute, why business work is
      not extra credit.

## Notes

- You are not asking a sponsor for money in this ticket. If someone
  offers you a contact, write the name in the **team’s** drive and stop.
  This website does not store sponsor lists.
- Do not invent a crisis to make the thank-you dramatic. Specific and
  small beats vague and grand.
- Impact is judged later in this track. The habit that wins it starts
  here: thank people, keep evidence, treat community work as real work.
- The next ticket ([Team Story & Brand](../team-story-brand/)) produces
  the one-sentence pitch and the one-paragraph story every letter and
  judge interview will reuse.
