## Overview

A robot that never ships, a team that cannot travel, and a shop that nobody
outside the school has heard of are the same problem: the non-technical work
did not get done. This lesson reframes business and outreach as a trade with
the same seriousness as wiring or code — because it is.

## Prerequisites

- None. This is the starting point for every business-team member.

## What you'll learn

- Why funding, story, and community are part of building a robot.
- How Gracious Professionalism® shows up off the field.
- What “everyone contributes” means when the work is a spreadsheet, not a mill.

## Unit 1 — The robot is not the whole product

The public product of an FRC team is bigger than the machine:

- **A funded season** — registration, travel, parts, and tools.
- **A story** judges, sponsors, and new members can understand in one minute.
- **A community** that would notice if the team disappeared.

If any one of those fails, the robot does not get a full season. Treat them as
requirements, not extras.

## Unit 2 — Equal work, different tools

The [mission]({{ '/mission/' | relative_url }}) says technical and non-technical
roles are equally valued. In practice that means:

- Business deadlines are as real as a mill slot. A late sponsor packet is a
  missed cycle.
- Mentors coach this trade the same way they coach CAD: questions first, then
  help.
- Veterans and leads exist here too. See the
  [org chart]({{ '/org-chart/' | relative_url }}).

## Unit 3 — Gracious Professionalism off the field

- Thank people by name, on time, for specific help.
- Represent the team at school, at events, and online as if a judge were in
  the room — because one might be.
- Share what you learn with nearby teams. Impact is not a zero-sum award.

## Steps & acceptance criteria

- [ ] Explain, in your own words, three things the team cannot do without
      business work.
- [ ] Name one technical and one non-technical deliverable that have the same
      deadline weight this month.
- [ ] Write a two-sentence thank-you to a real person who helped the team.
