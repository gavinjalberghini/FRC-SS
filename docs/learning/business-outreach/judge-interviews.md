## Description

Judges decide with their eyes and their questions. A perfect essay with
a confused pit talk loses to a clear pit and honest students. This
ticket covers the Impact interview and the everyday pit conversation
that wins Engineering Inspiration.

You have an outline and at least one fact-checked pillar from
[The Impact Award Submission](../impact-award/), and you know from
[The FRC Awards Landscape](../awards-landscape/) that EI is often won
in the pit. The
[one-sentence pitch](../team-story-brand/) is still the first sentence
they should remember. If you cannot say it in eight seconds, go back
and cut.

Judges are volunteers on a clock. The
[Judge Manual](https://info.firstinspires.org/hubfs/web/program/frc/awards/judge-manual.pdf)
is the document that tells them how little time they have. Respect it.
They have already read the paper if you submitted Impact. The talk
should **match the paper**. A new claim in the room that is not in the
essay is how you lose trust.

The timed talk:

- Open with who you are and the one sentence they should remember.
- Three pillars, one student each, then a close that points at the
  robot or the community — not “any questions?” as a shrug.
- Rehearse **standing up, with a clock**. Sitting around a laptop is
  not rehearsal.

Students speak. If a mentor answers the first question, the interview
is already going badly. FIRST’s own
[award-submission tips](https://www.firstinspires.org/resources/library/frc/award-tips)
and
[Impact resources](https://www.firstinspires.org/resources/library/frc/fia-resources)
assume students can stand behind the words. Write that into the
rehearsal, not the hope.

Hard questions. Judges will ask some version of:

- “What failed, and what did you change?”
- “Who owns this when you graduate?”
- “What did the *students* do versus the adults?”
- “Can you show me the number you just cited?”

Answer with a fact, then stop. Do not fill silence with bigger claims.
“I don’t know — I can get that from the file” is a strong answer. The
file is the evidence folder from
[Measuring Community Impact](../measuring-impact/). If you cannot walk
to it, do not cite it.

The pit as an exhibit:

- One visible story (banner or board) that matches the essay and the
  [brand rules](../team-story-brand/).
- A robot a judge can look at without climbing. Labels on subsystems.
- A student greeter whose job is “can I help you” — not a crowd of
  people on phones.
- Safety glasses and a clean floor. Judges notice sloppiness first.

The pit is shared space. Drive-team needs from
[Event Operations](../../drive-team/event-operations/) still apply.
Business students do not block a queue to deliver a monologue.

This ticket is one sitting of rehearsal plus a pit walk. It is not the
event. The event will be louder. Rehearse anyway.

This site does not track whether you rehearsed. A mentor watches the
talk or watches a recording the team stored in its own drive.

## Prerequisites

- [The FRC Awards Landscape](../awards-landscape/) completed.
- [The Impact Award Submission](../impact-award/) at least through the
  outline, one fact-checked pillar, and named speakers. If you are
  not submitting Impact, still do this ticket for EI and pit talks;
  use the three pillars you *would* defend from the evidence log.
- The [pitch](../team-story-brand/) you can say without notes.
- A space to stand and a phone timer. A pit, if you have one to walk.

## What you'll learn

- How to structure a 5–10 minute interview that matches the paper.
- How to answer a question you did not expect without inventing a
  better season.
- How to set a pit so a stranger understands the team without a
  lecture.

## Tasks

1. **Read how little time they have.** Skim the cultural-award /
   interview portions of the
   [Judge Manual](https://info.firstinspires.org/hubfs/web/program/frc/awards/judge-manual.pdf)
   and the interview notes on
   [Impact Award Resources](https://www.firstinspires.org/resources/library/frc/fia-resources).
   Write one sentence: *If we had five minutes, the one sentence they
   must leave with is …* Use the pitch, or a sharper version of it.

2. **Write the 6-minute card.** One page in the team drive: opening
   sentence, three pillars with a 90-second owner each, close, and
   who answers budget / failure / succession. The card matches the
   essay outline. If it does not, fix the card or fix the outline
   before you rehearse.

3. **Deliver the talk standing up.** Six minutes, clock visible, to
   teammates who were **not** on the writing team. Mentors do not
   speak unless a safety issue appears. Afterward, the audience
   writes every question you could not answer. Those questions
   become the next rehearsal list, not a reason to add adjectives.

4. **Practice one “what failed” answer.** Name a real miss from this
   season or last: a dead outreach event, a sponsor you ghosted, a
   number you could not defend. What you changed. Then stop. Time
   it under 45 seconds. If you cannot name a miss, you are not
   looking.

5. **Walk the pit as a stranger.** Thirty seconds, no briefing. List
   three things that are unclear (no team number visible, no greeter,
   a banner that contradicts the essay, a robot you cannot look at
   without climbing, phones). Assign a fix owner to each. If you do
   not have a competition pit yet, walk the shop the way a Saturday
   visitor would.

6. **Assign event roles.** Greeter, robot explainer, awards speaker,
   and a runner who can fetch the evidence folder or budget snapshot.
   Names, not “we’ll see who is there.” If the next event is a
   practice, still assign them.

7. **Hand the recording or the witness to a mentor.** A mentor
   watched the 6-minute talk live or watched a recording in the team
   drive. They heard the failure answer. They saw the pit list. If
   your team exported these tickets, paste the drive links on the
   issue — not the essay.

## Acceptance Criteria

- [ ] A 6-minute card exists that matches the essay outline (or the
      three pillars you would defend) and names who speaks.
- [ ] You delivered a 6-minute interview standing up, on a clock, to
      teammates who were not on the writing team.
- [ ] A written list exists of every question the audience asked that
      you could not answer.
- [ ] A “what failed” answer names a real miss and a change, and a
      mentor timed it under 45 seconds.
- [ ] A pit (or shop) walk produced three unclear things with fix
      owners.
- [ ] Greeter, robot explainer, and awards speaker are named for the
      next event.
- [ ] A mentor watched the talk or the recording. Mentors did not
      answer the first question.

## Notes

- If a judge asks a number and you do not have it, do not round up.
  Fetch or pass. Invented precision is worse than a clean unknown.
- Phones in the pit during a judge visit are a costume of not caring.
  Put them down.
- EI judges may never sit your formal interview. The greeter is the
  EI interview.
- The next ticket ([Communications & Media](../communications-media/))
  is how the same story leaves the pit without burning a student or
  a sponsor.
