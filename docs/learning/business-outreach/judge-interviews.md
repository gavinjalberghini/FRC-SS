## Overview

Judges decide with their eyes and their questions. A perfect essay with a
confused pit talk loses to a clear pit and honest students. This lesson
covers the Impact interview and the everyday pit conversation that wins
Engineering Inspiration.

## Prerequisites

- [The FRC Awards Landscape](../awards-landscape/)
- [The Impact Award Submission](../impact-award/)

## What you'll learn

- How to structure a 5–10 minute interview.
- How to answer a question you did not expect.
- How to set a pit so a stranger understands the team without a lecture.

## Unit 1 — The timed talk

- Open with who you are and the one sentence they should remember.
- Three pillars, one student each, then a close that points at the robot or
  the community — not “any questions?” as a shrug.
- Rehearse standing up, with a clock. Sitting around a laptop is not
  rehearsal.

Students speak. If a mentor answers the first question, the interview is
already going badly.

## Unit 2 — Hard questions

Judges will ask some version of:

- “What failed, and what did you change?”
- “Who owns this when you graduate?”
- “What did the *students* do versus the adults?”
- “Can you show me the number you just cited?”

Answer with a fact, then stop. Do not fill silence with bigger claims.
“I don’t know — I can get that from the file” is a strong answer.

## Unit 3 — The pit as an exhibit

- One visible story (banner or board) that matches the essay.
- A robot a judge can look at without climbing. Labels on subsystems.
- A student greeter whose job is “can I help you” — not a crowd of people
  on phones.
- Safety glasses and a clean floor. Judges notice sloppiness first.

## Steps & acceptance criteria

- [ ] Deliver a 6-minute interview to teammates who were not on the writing
      team; take notes on every question you could not answer.
- [ ] Practice one “what failed” answer that names a real miss.
- [ ] Walk the pit as a stranger and list three things that are unclear in
      30 seconds.
- [ ] Assign greeter, robot explainer, and awards speaker for the next
      event.
