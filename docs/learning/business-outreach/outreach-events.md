## Description

“We should do more outreach” is not a plan. An event is a plan: who it is
for, what they will do in 30 minutes, who on the team is running each
station, and how you will know it worked. This ticket is how to design
one that is worth the Saturday.

You already know why community work is not extra credit
([Why Business Work Matters](../why-business-matters/)) and you have a
[pitch and paragraph](../team-story-brand/) a host can hear. You may have
promised a shop visit in
[Sponsor Outreach & Stewardship](../sponsor-outreach/). This ticket is
the run-of-show that keeps that promise from becoming a scramble in the
parking lot.

Start from **one primary goal**. Examples:

- Recruit members from a specific school or grade.
- Give a sponsor a visible, well-run hour.
- Teach a concrete skill to a rookie team or a classroom.
- Collect stories and photos for the Impact file
  ([Measuring Community Impact](../measuring-impact/) is next).

If the goal is “be visible,” rewrite it until you can count something.
Visibility is a hope. Headcount, sign-ups, or a skill a guest can
demonstrate is a goal.

Design the hour around the people who will actually be there:

- **Audience and count.** 15 third-graders is a different event than
  four adult judges. Write the number you are planning for, not the
  number you wish would show up.
- **Stations.** One robot demo, one hands-on (drive a practice bot,
  build a simple mechanism), one story (students talking, not a mentor
  monologue). The
  [pitch](../team-story-brand/) is the story station, spoken.
- **Safety.** Glasses, battery rules, no unattended enabled robots.
  See [Electrical Safety](../../electrical/electrical-safety/). A demo
  that injures a guest is not outreach.
- **A run-of-show** with clock times and named student owners. “We’ll
  figure it out” is how you run late and look unserious. Sponsors and
  judges notice unserious.

Youth protection is part of the design, not a footnote. Read
[FIRST Youth Protection](https://www.firstinspires.org/programs/youth-protection-program)
before you put students in a classroom or a sponsor’s lobby. Two adults
who can see the room, no one-adult-one-student closed doors, and the
school’s own rules on top of FIRST’s.

Before you pack the trailer, assign follow-up:

- Who sends the thank-you the next day (host, sponsor, teachers).
- Who files photos, attendance, and a two-sentence “what we learned.”
- Who owns the next contact if someone asked how to join or help.

An event without a file is a memory. Memories do not win Impact and do
not recruit in August. The file lives in the **team’s drive**. This
website does not store attendance lists, photos of minors, or host
emails.

Look at how public-facing teams talk about programs that are not the
robot — [Team 254’s programs](https://www.team254.com/),
[Citrus Circuits’ workshops](https://citruscircuits.org/),
[Mechanical Advantage’s community programs](https://www.littletonrobotics.org/).
You are not copying their calendar. You are noticing that a named
program with a next step beats a one-off photo.

## What you'll learn

- How to pick an event that matches a real goal you can count.
- How to write a run-of-show and a staffing plan with named students.
- How to follow up so the event is not a one-off photo.

## Tasks

1. **Pick one goal you can count.** Write a single sentence: *This
   event is for [audience] so that [countable outcome].* If you cannot
   put a number on the outcome (sign-ins, “students who drove the bot,”
   “teachers who took a leave-behind”), rewrite the goal. Read
   [FIRST Youth Protection](https://www.firstinspires.org/programs/youth-protection-program)
   far enough to know who must be in the room.

2. **Write the one-page event brief.** In the team drive, one page:

   - goal (the sentence from Task 1)
   - audience and planned count
   - date, start/end, location
   - host contact (name and role — not a student’s personal cell)
   - what guests will do in 30–60 minutes
   - what you will count, and how (sign-in sheet, teacher headcount)

   This is not a poster. It is the page a lead can approve.

3. **Write the run-of-show.** A table or timed list covering load-in
   to load-out. Every block has a **clock time** and a **named student
   owner**. Include at least: load-in, safety briefing, robot demo,
   hands-on station, story/pitch station, Q&A, thank-you, load-out,
   follow-up owner. If two stations run at once, write both. “We’ll
   figure it out” is not a row.

4. **List PPE and robot-safe-state rules for that venue.** Glasses,
   when the robot is disabled, where the battery lives, who is allowed
   to enable, what happens if a guest grabs a mechanism. Point at
   [Electrical Safety](../../electrical/electrical-safety/) for the
   battery rules you are not rewriting. If the venue is a school,
   add the school’s visitor and photo rules.

5. **Name the three follow-up tasks.** Thank-you, file (photos,
   attendance, two-sentence debrief), next contact. Each has a person
   and a deadline inside 48 hours. Put those names on the brief.
   [Measuring Community Impact](../measuring-impact/) will use this
   file; do not wait until that ticket to decide who holds the camera.

6. **Walk the brief with a mentor.** Ten minutes. The mentor should
   be able to say who is in charge at 10:15 and what “success” means
   in a number. If your team exported these tickets, paste the drive
   link on the issue. Do not upload the host’s email list here.

## Acceptance Criteria

- [ ] A one-page event brief exists in the **team’s drive** with goal,
      audience, date, location, and planned count.
- [ ] The goal is countable; you can say how you will count it.
- [ ] A run-of-show exists with clock times and a named student owner
      for each station, including load-in and load-out.
- [ ] PPE and robot-safe-state rules for that venue are written, and
      youth-protection constraints are named.
- [ ] Three follow-up tasks have owners and a 48-hour deadline.
- [ ] A mentor can, without you in the room, tell you who owns 10:15
      and what number means the event worked.

## Notes

- You do not have to run the event in this sitting. You have to design
  one well enough that a lead would let you put it on the calendar.
- Photos of minors need the consent process the school and FIRST
  require. If you do not know the process, the photo owner on the
  follow-up list is “do not post until we know.”
- Do not enable a robot in a crowd to look impressive. Impressive is
  optional. Safe is not.
- The next ticket ([Measuring Community Impact](../measuring-impact/))
  is the evidence log this run-of-show is about to fill. Design the
  count now or you will invent it in April.
