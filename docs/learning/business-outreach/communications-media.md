## Overview

The team’s public voice is part of the robot. A careless post can burn a
sponsor, a student, or a school administrator in an afternoon. This lesson
is the standard for what gets published, who publishes it, and what never
leaves the private chat.

## Prerequisites

- [Team Story & Brand](../team-story-brand/)
- The [Team Contract]({{ '/contract/' | relative_url }}) technology section

## What you'll learn

- How to plan a simple content calendar.
- Photo, name, and location rules.
- How to talk to press without freelancing policy.

## Unit 1 — One voice, many hands

- A small set of official channels (site, one social, email). Personal
  accounts can cheer; they should not negotiate or announce.
- A student comms owner and an adult who can take something down.
- The brand rules from Lesson 2 apply to posts. A new filter is not a new
  identity.

## Unit 2 — What never gets posted

- Student last names, locations of homes, or travel plans that make a group
  findable in real time.
- Photos of minors without the consent process the school and FIRST
  require.
- Internal conflict, other teams’ failures, or unreleased strategy.
- Sponsor logos in a context they did not approve (memes, political
  captions, complaints).

When in doubt, do not post. Ask. The story will still be there tomorrow.

## Unit 3 — Press and school

- A one-page fact sheet (name, number, season goal, student contact,
  mentor contact) so nobody invents a quote.
- Students may speak to their own work. Policy, safety incidents, and
  official results go through the adult owner.
- Credit FIRST, the school, and partners accurately.

## Steps & acceptance criteria

- [ ] Draft a two-week content calendar with owners and a purpose for each
      post.
- [ ] List the consent and takedown rules you will follow this season.
- [ ] Write a five-line fact sheet a reporter could copy without calling
      twice.
- [ ] Review three recent posts against the brand and safety rules; fix
      any miss.
