## Description

Judges and sponsors hear “we inspired hundreds of students” every weekend.
The teams they remember are the ones who can say *how many, where, doing
what, measured how*. This ticket is the evidence habit that makes later
award work possible.

You designed an event in
[Planning Outreach Events](../outreach-events/). If that event already
happened, log it. If it has not, log a past event the team can still
defend, or log the upcoming one with a plan for the count. Either way,
you are building an **evidence log** in the team’s drive — not a vibe,
and not a page on this website.

Impact, when it is judged, is a researched argument. Official language
lives on
[FIRST Impact Award Resources](https://www.firstinspires.org/resources/library/frc/fia-resources)
and
[Submitted Awards](https://www.firstinspires.org/resources/library/frc/submitted-awards).
Judges are told to look for claims they can verify. Your job in this
ticket is not to write the essay. Your job is to stop inventing April
from memory. The essay ticket
([The Impact Award Submission](../impact-award/)) will fail if this file
is empty.

Useful metrics are specific and repeatable:

- **People** — unique students, teachers, families, other teams. Say
  how you counted (sign-in, teacher headcount, not “the gym looked
  full”).
- **Hours** — student hours and mentor hours, separately.
- **Depth** — a 5-minute hallway demo is not the same as an 8-week
  mentor relationship. Tag the type.
- **Repeat** — first-time vs. returning partners.

Vanity metrics (likes, “reach”) are optional garnish. Do not lead with
them. A judge who asks “how do you know?” is doing their job. “The gym
looked full” is not an answer.

A good **story** has a person, a change, and a quote they approved:

- Get permission to use names and photos, especially of minors. Read
  [FIRST Youth Protection](https://www.firstinspires.org/programs/youth-protection-program)
  and follow the school’s consent process on top of it.
- Write the story the same week. Memory invents adjectives.
- Prefer “what they can now do” over “they were so excited.”

Ethical storytelling is a real discipline, not a mood.
[Meyer Partners on respectful nonprofit storytelling](https://www.meyerpartners.com/fundraising-blog/nonprofit-storytelling)
and
[DonorPerfect on impact stories](https://www.donorperfect.com/nonprofit-technology-blog/fundraising-software/impact-stories-nonprofit-storytelling/)
are useful because they treat consent and specificity as the work.
People are not props for an award.

Keep one shared folder in the **team’s drive** with:

- Event briefs and attendance (from the last ticket).
- Photos with captions (who, where, date) and a consent note.
- Thank-you emails and partner contacts.
- A one-line log after every event: goal, count, what we would change.

This file is the raw material for Impact. If you start it in March, you
are already late. If you start it on this website, you are in the wrong
place. This playbook does not store evidence, photos, or sponsor names.

## Prerequisites

- [Planning Outreach Events](../outreach-events/) completed (a brief
  and a run-of-show exist, even if the event has not run yet).
- Access to the team’s drive. You need a folder a successor can find
  without texting you.

## What you'll learn

- Which numbers are worth collecting, and how to say how you counted.
- How to capture stories without turning people into props.
- How to keep a running file instead of reconstructing April from
  memory.

## Tasks

1. **Read how Impact talks about evidence.** Skim
   [FIRST Impact Award Resources](https://www.firstinspires.org/resources/library/frc/fia-resources)
   far enough to find the current documentation form and the reminder
   that judges may verify claims. Write one sentence: *A claim we
   cannot verify is …* Finish it with a real example from your team
   (“we inspired the community”) and what evidence would replace it.

2. **Create or open the evidence folder.** In the team drive, a folder
   named for the season (for example `impact-evidence-2027`) with a
   simple naming rule: `YYYY-MM-DD-event-shortname`. Put the event
   brief from the last ticket in it. If a folder already exists, use
   it. Two folders is how evidence disappears.

3. **Log one event.** Past or upcoming. One row or one page with:

   - name, date, location
   - goal (from the brief)
   - people: number, who they were, **source of the count**
   - hours: student and mentor, separately
   - type/depth (hallway demo, classroom hour, season-long mentorship)
   - first-time vs. returning partner
   - what you would change

   If the event has not happened, fill the plan and leave actuals
   blank with an owner who will fill them within 48 hours after.

4. **Write a 6–8 sentence story.** A person, a change, a quote they
   approved — or a note that you do not have consent yet and will not
   use a name. No “hundreds of inspired students.” One person, one
   thing they can now do. Read
   [How to Write Nonprofit Impact Stories](https://www.donorperfect.com/nonprofit-technology-blog/fundraising-software/impact-stories-nonprofit-storytelling/)
   if the draft is all adjectives.

5. **Caption one photo or explain why you have none.** Who, where,
   date, consent status. If you cannot name the consent status, the
   photo does not go in the file and does not go on social. Follow
   [FIRST Youth Protection](https://www.firstinspires.org/programs/youth-protection-program).

6. **Explain the count out loud.** To a mentor, in two minutes: the
   difference between a **headcount** (someone counted) and an
   **estimate** (someone guessed). Use the event in Task 3. If your
   number is an estimate, say so in the log.

7. **Hand the folder link to a mentor.** This website does not get
   the files. If your team exported these tickets, paste the drive
   link on the issue.

## Acceptance Criteria

- [ ] An evidence folder exists in the **team’s drive** with a
      consistent file name rule.
- [ ] One past or upcoming event is logged with people, hours, type,
      and the source of the count.
- [ ] You can explain the difference between a headcount and an
      estimate, using that event, and a mentor heard it.
- [ ] A 6–8 sentence story exists with a consented quote, or a written
      note why you cannot use one yet.
- [ ] One photo has a caption and a consent status, or you wrote why
      the file has no photo yet.
- [ ] No attendance list, minor photo, or partner email was uploaded
      to this website.

## Notes

- “Reach” on a social post is not a people count. If you include it,
  label it as garnish.
- Do not back-fill last year with numbers nobody counted. Write
  “unknown” and do better this year. Judges can hear a clean unknown.
  They can also hear a rounded fantasy.
- The next ticket ([The FRC Awards Landscape](../awards-landscape/))
  is the map of which award this file is for — and which awards it is
  not for. Do not start the Impact essay until you know you are
  submitting on purpose.
