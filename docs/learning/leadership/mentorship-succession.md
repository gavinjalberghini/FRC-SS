## Description

The last test of a student leader is not this year's robot. It is what
the team can do in September after you have a college ID. School teams
lose captains on a calendar. If the wiring map, the standup habit, and
the "how we treat freshmen" live only in your head, you did not lead a
team. You rented one.

Hunter's servant-leadership loop ends in other people's growth. Liz
Wiseman's *Multipliers* names leaders who make the people around them
smarter instead of becoming the bottleneck genius. The Leadership
Pipeline idea is simpler for FRC: you move from **doing**, to
**delegating** (ticket 6), to **developing** — teaching judgment so
someone else can decide, run the 15-minute standup, and eventually
develop the next person. Senior leadership lives in that third stage.
Your highlight reel is the wrong scoreboard.

You already have a RACI that should not have you as A on every line, a
stack of Leadership notes, a used meeting agenda, a feedback
conversation, a conflict write-up, and a culture conversation with a
mentor. This capstone asks you to **mentor one teammate on purpose**,
**name successors while you can still coach them**, and **write down
the things only you know**. Spreading knowledge is Gracious
Professionalism toward next year's unpaid students. Hoarding it is a
quiet kind of authority-without-service.

Mentoring is mostly questions. "What have you tried? What do you think
we should do?" builds a decision-maker (ticket 10). Answering every
time builds a dependent. Let them struggle up to the edge of costly or
unsafe failure, then catch them. Share the *why* behind your calls so
they inherit reasoning, not a rulebook they cannot update.

Hand off gradually. A successor who "takes over" the week you leave
has not taken over. They have been dropped. Identify people early —
including people without titles, because ticket 1 is still true.

This site does not track whether you finished. The mentoring log, the
succession plan, and the "only I know this" document live in your
notes and in the team's working docs. A mentor signs off in a
conversation, not in a web form.

## What you'll learn

- The doing → delegating → developing ladder, and where you actually
  sit on it.
- How to mentor with questions, stretch work, and a safety net.
- How to plan succession on a team that graduates its leaders every
  year.
- How to move "only I know this" knowledge into a place the team can
  find in August.

## Tasks

1. **Read developing-not-doing.** Read a short overview of Liz
   Wiseman's multiplier vs diminisher idea —
   [Multipliers](https://www.multipliersbooks.com/)
   or a mentor-owned copy of the opening chapters. Skim the idea of a
   leadership pipeline (Charan, Drotter, and Noel) enough to say how
   the *work* of leadership changes when you stop being the best pair
   of hands. Reread your ticket 1 notes on servant leadership. Write
   one paragraph: where you still do, where you delegate, where you
   already develop.

2. **Mentor one person for two real sessions.** Pick a teammate you
   named on the RACI as a stretch (or the person from ticket 11's
   autonomy/mastery/purpose). In two separate work sessions:

   - they own a real task
   - you ask before you tell
   - you share why you would decide X, then let them take a
     reversible call (ticket 10)
   - you catch safety and one-way doors

   Log each session in the notes: questions you asked, the moment you
   wanted to grab the tool, what they decided, what you would do
   differently. This is the feedback conversation of ticket 4 aimed at
   growth, not correction only.

3. **Name successors and a season plan.** Write, with a mentor, one
   or two people who could run your group next year — including a
   titleless person if that is who the team already follows. For each:

   - skills they have
   - skills they need (standup, RACI, a crucial conversation, a
     one-way-door decision)
   - a stretch they will own *this season* while you can still coach
   - when they will run a meeting you only attend

   Do not announce a coronation in the group chat. Grow them. The
   team will notice.

4. **Write down 'only I know this.'** Pick one piece of knowledge that
   currently lives in your head: a vendor contact, a pit routine, a
   software deploy step, how to talk to that one inspector, the real
   reason last year's bumper rule bite happened. Put it in the team's
   working docs or playbook — not only in your private Leadership
   notes — so it survives your account permissions. In the notes,
   link or photograph the location and name a second person who has
   now read it.

5. **Capstone review of the whole notes document.** Sit with a mentor
   for twenty to thirty minutes with the Leadership notes from
   tickets 1–11 open. Walk them through:

   - the unused-title vs influence examples from ticket 1
   - the used standup agenda from ticket 5
   - the RACI and one delegation brief
   - the feedback and conflict debriefs
   - the culture norms and the burnout relief

   Ask: what is still only theater? What would next year's lead need
   next? Write a final dated entry: three things you will keep doing
   after this ticket is "done," because this ticket's time estimate is
   *ongoing*.

6. **Leave one system better.** Besides the document in Task 4, change
   one system: a training checklist, a board column, a meeting
   cadence, a safety or GP norm stated at standup. Small and real.
   Write before-and-after in the notes. Trajectory beats a personal
   highlight reel.

## Acceptance Criteria

- [ ] Notes include an honest doing / delegating / developing
      self-assessment after the Multipliers / pipeline reading.
- [ ] You mentored one teammate across two real sessions, asked before
      telling, and logged both sessions.
- [ ] A succession plan names at least one successor, the stretches
      they will own this season, and a meeting they will run while you
      still coach. A mentor helped write it.
- [ ] One "only I know this" item now lives in a team-accessible doc,
      and a second person has read it.
- [ ] You completed a capstone mentor conversation over the full
      Leadership notes (agendas, RACI, reflections) and wrote three
      ongoing practices.
- [ ] You changed one team system, not only one personal habit. None
      of this work is stored on this website.

## Notes

- Identifying a successor in your last month is how knowledge walks
  out the door in a cardboard box. Start when it feels too early.
- Pair and cross-train. A single "next captain" is still a single
  point of failure.
- Mentoring is not owning their grade or their college essay. Keep
  adult issues with adults.
- You will not finish becoming a leader on this ticket. You will
  finish a body of evidence a mentor can see: notes, a used agenda, a
  RACI, conversations you actually had. That was the point of all
  twelve.
- If you are not graduating yet, run this ticket anyway. Succession
  is a habit, not a senior-year event.
