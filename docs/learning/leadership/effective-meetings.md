## Description

On a volunteer team, time is the one resource nobody can donate twice. A
student who stays until 9 p.m. after sports and homework will not keep
doing that if Monday's "quick sync" ate 50 minutes and decided nothing.
Meetings are where influence either respects people or spends them. A
title can call a meeting. Only a trusted leader can end it on time with
owners and dates.

You already have a subsystem, a RACI (ticket 3), and a short retro under
your belt (ticket 4). This ticket is the operating rhythm: **do not hold a
meeting you cannot purpose**, write an agenda with times, run a standup
that stays at status, and leave with decisions and action items that have
one owner each.

A **standup** is a 5–15 minute sync, often standing up so it cannot grow
roots. Each person answers three questions:

1. What did I get done?
2. What am I doing next?
3. What is **blocking** me?

Rules that keep it from becoming a design review: status, not
problem-solving; everyone speaks briefly; blockers get an owner who will
work them *after* the circle. Two people who need to argue about a belt
path take it "offline" — they stay in the room at the table, the other
eight get their night back.

Before any longer meeting, write the purpose as a decision or an outcome
("Pick bumper fabric and an owner by 7:20," not "discuss bumpers"). Invite
the people on the RACI, not the whole team "so they feel included."
Inclusion is a job on the robot and a note they can read. During the
meeting, timebox, park tangents on a parking-lot list, pull in the quiet
person, and drive to a call. Afterward, publish **decisions** and
**action items** (one owner, one date). A decision with no owner is a
wish. Ticket 6 is how you hand those actions off without taking them
back.

This site does not track whether you finished. You will keep the **agenda
you actually used** — marked up, timed, honest — in your Leadership notes
and show it to a mentor.

## What you'll learn

- When to send a message instead of calling a meeting.
- How to write a timed agenda and run a 15-minute standup that surfaces
  blockers.
- How to timebox, use a parking lot, and capture owned action items.

## Tasks

1. **Read two short meeting guides.** Read Atlassian's
   [daily standup](https://www.atlassian.com/agile/scrum/standups)
   and skim the
   [Team Playbook](https://www.atlassian.com/team-playbook)
   enough to steal one facilitation move (parking lot, DACI/decision,
   or a retro format you have not used). Optional: the programming
   [Kanban & Agile](../../programming/kanban-agile/) ticket's standup
   definition if your software group already runs one.

2. **Write the agenda you will actually use.** In your Leadership notes,
   under `Ticket 5 — Meetings`, draft a one-page agenda for a real
   upcoming session with your group. Include:

   - purpose in one sentence (decision or outcome)
   - start and end time (standup: 15 minutes max; if this is a longer
     planning meeting, still timebox each topic)
   - attendee list taken from the RACI, not "anyone who wants to come"
   - topics with rough minutes
   - a parking-lot heading, empty, ready for tangents
   - a blank **Decisions** and **Action items** section you will fill
     *during* the meeting, not from memory the next day

   Share the agenda with the group before you meet — chat, email, or a
   photo of the whiteboard. If you cannot share it ahead, you still
   start the meeting by reading it out loud.

3. **Facilitate a real standup (or the first 15 minutes of a work
   session).** Run it. Phone or timer visible. Three questions each.
   When two people start solving, park it: "You two stay after; we need
   the rest of the blockers first." Draw out anyone who says "same" or
   "nothing." End at or before 15 minutes. You are not allowed to
   "just finish this one design thing" in the circle.

4. **Fill the agenda after, in ink.** On the same document you used
   (print, photo of the whiteboard, or the digital agenda), write:

   - actual start and end times
   - decisions made, in full sentences
   - action items as `owner — verb — date`
   - parking-lot items and who will pick each one up
   - one facilitation note: who you pulled in, who you had to rein in,
     where you broke your own timebox

   This marked-up agenda is the artifact. A clean rewrite that hides
   the overruns is less useful than the messy one you used.

5. **Ticket 5 reflection.** In the notes, answer:

   - Did the RACI names match who actually spoke? Who was missing?
   - Which blocker was the most expensive, and who is clearing it?
   - What would you cut from the agenda next time?
   - How did Start with Heart (ticket 2) show up when someone was
     defensive about being blocked?

6. **Mentor conversation.** Show the mentor the agenda you *used*, not
   a theoretical template. Ask them whether the action items are
   actually owned and whether the standup stayed at status. Add their
   comments under the reflection.

## Acceptance Criteria

- [ ] You wrote an agenda with a purpose, times, a tight invite list,
      and blank decision / action sections before the meeting.
- [ ] You shared that agenda with attendees (or read it at the start).
- [ ] You facilitated a standup or opening sync that stayed at or under
      15 minutes and used the three questions.
- [ ] The same agenda (or a photo of it) is filled in with real times,
      decisions, owned action items, and parking-lot items.
- [ ] Notes include a short reflection on RACI vs. who spoke, the
      costliest blocker, and one cut for next time.
- [ ] A mentor has seen the used agenda. It lives in your notes, not on
      this website.

## Notes

- If the whole team "needs to hear it," write a three-bullet note
  instead of growing the invite. Volunteers punish bloated meetings by
  not coming back.
- Starting late teaches people to arrive later. End on the clock even
  if it feels rude the first time. That is respect, not rigidity.
- Do not turn standup into public feedback. Corrective SBI stays
  private (ticket 4). Blockers stay factual.
- Keep the used agenda. Tickets 6, 7, and 9 will ask you to point at
  owners, slipped dates, and what was actually important.
- The next ticket, [Delegation & Accountability](../delegation-accountability/),
  is how those action items leave your hands without leaving your
  follow-up.
