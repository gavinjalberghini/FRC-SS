## Description

Culture is not the poster in the hallway. Culture is the **worst
behavior you tolerate** and the **best behavior you celebrate**. On a
volunteer FRC team that sentence is the whole engine. Nobody can be
ordered to care. A star who belittles freshmen will become the
standard, no matter what the captain said at kickoff. A quiet student
who stays to sweep, who admits a bad dimension, who thanks someone for
raising a problem early — if nobody names that, it dies.

You have spent ten tickets on influence, listening, safety, feedback,
meetings, delegation, expectations, conflict, time, and decisions.
Those are culture, delivered daily. This ticket makes the pattern
visible: explicit norms, volunteer motivation that is not pizza-as-a-
personality, specific public recognition, and burnout as a leadership
problem rather than a badge.

Daniel Pink's *Drive* (and the RSA talk built from it) names three
intrinsic motivators that actually move people who are not on a
payroll: **autonomy** (real ownership — your RACI's R with room on the
*how*), **mastery** (they can see themselves getting better), and
**purpose** (the daily tap-and-die connects to FIRST's mission and to
this team's reason for existing). Extrinsic "prizes for points" fade.
A freshman who owns a sensor mount and is coached when it fails will
still be here in February.

Recognition has to be specific. "Good job everyone" trains nothing.
"Maya's strain relief made inspection boring" trains strain relief.
Catch people doing it right. Spread credit. Include effort and growth,
not only the student who already had a title. That is Radical Candor
on the praise side (ticket 4) and servant leadership on the credit
side (ticket 1).

Sustaining energy is a senior-leader job. The most committed people
burn out first. Celebrate milestones, not only the banner. Food and
inside jokes are not unprofessional; they are how unpaid people
remember they belong. If you grind 24/7, you have announced the norm.
Ticket 9's important-not-urgent quadrant includes rest.

Gracious Professionalism is the FIRST-facing name for the same
culture: high-quality work that still values the person. Coopertition
extends it off your own alliance. If your "standards" require
humiliating another team or your own freshman, they are not standards.

This site does not track whether you finished. The norms list, the
recognition you actually gave, and the burnout plan live in your
Leadership notes.

## Prerequisites

## What you'll learn

- How leaders set culture by what they model and what they allow.
- Autonomy, mastery, and purpose as the real volunteer motivators.
- How to give specific public recognition and why generic praise
  fails.
- How to spot burnout risk and relieve it without making rest a
  secret.

## Tasks

1. **Read Drive and FIRST's values, short.** Watch Daniel Pink's RSA
   animate
   [Drive: The surprising truth about what motivates us](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=u6XAPnuFjJc)
   (about 10 minutes) or read the opening of *Drive* if you have it.
   Then read
   [About FIRST](https://www.firstinspires.org/about)
   and
   [FIRST Core Values](https://help.firstinspires.org/s/article/What-are-the-FIRST-Core-Values)
   with a leader's eyes: which value is a poster here, and which is a
   behavior? Optional: your team's own mission page if you have one.

2. **Name the real norms.** In a dated `Ticket 11 — Culture` section,
   write three norms your team *actually* lives (punctuality, safety,
   who cleans, who may interrupt, whether late work is joked about).
   For each: how it is reinforced, and one time a high performer was
   or was not held to it. Then write one norm you will make explicit
   this week out loud — not a speech, a sentence at standup: "We don't
   mock questions; we answer them."

3. **Give someone autonomy, mastery, and purpose.** Pick one teammate
   (ideally someone on your RACI who is not already a lead). Write:

   - **Autonomy:** a decision they will own this week (ticket 6 brief)
   - **Mastery:** a skill they will visibly get better at, and how
     they will see it
   - **Purpose:** one sentence connecting their task to the robot or
     to FIRST's mission

   Then do it. Do not only write it.

4. **Give specific public recognition.** This week, in front of the
   group, name one person and one behavior. Write the sentence in the
   notes before you say it so it stays specific. Separate this from
   the corrective SBI you already practiced — praise is public. If
   you are uncomfortable, that is data; do it anyway once.

5. **Spot a burnout risk and relieve it.** Name a person (including
   you) who is carrying too much — the student who is R on too many
   RACI lines, the mentor who never leaves, the captain who took back
   every monkey. Propose a concrete relief: a paused project (ticket
   9), a delegated line (ticket 6), a mandatory leave-at-time, a
   second person trained. Run that proposal past a mentor *before*
   you announce someone is "burned out" in public. Then implement the
   piece you have authority to implement.

6. **Reflection and mentor conversation.** Write:

   - the worst behavior currently tolerated, and what you will do
     the next time it happens (private SBI, not a pile-on)
   - whether your own hours are teaching martyrdom
   - one tradition or belonging habit worth protecting (food,
     thank-you notes, a clean shop song — whatever is *yours*)

   Sit with a mentor and ask what culture they wish last year's
   seniors had left. Add their answer. You will need it for ticket 12.

## Acceptance Criteria

- [ ] Notes include three lived norms with how they are reinforced,
      plus one norm you stated out loud this week.
- [ ] You can explain autonomy, mastery, and purpose in your own
      words and you applied all three to one real teammate.
- [ ] You gave specific public recognition naming a person and a
      behavior, and the sentence is in the notes.
- [ ] You identified a burnout risk, checked a mentor before making
      it public, and implemented one relief action.
- [ ] A mentor discussed last year's leftover culture with you. Work
      stays in your Leadership notes, not on this website.

## Notes

- Holding a star to the same safety and respect rules as a freshman
  is how you prove the norms are real. Looking away is a decision.
- Pizza is hospitality, not a motivation strategy. Use it. Do not
  confuse it with autonomy.
- Public recognition of only the already-famous students is how
  everyone else learns they are furniture.
- The last ticket, [Mentorship & Succession](../mentorship-succession/),
  is culture that outlives your graduation. Bring the whole notes
  document. This is the capstone, not a new personality.
