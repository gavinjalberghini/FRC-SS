## Description

Most people walk into an FRC shop thinking leadership is a title. Captain.
Mechanical lead. Safety officer. The name on a shirt or a slide at kickoff.
That is **authority**: a position someone handed you. Authority is useful when
it is backed by trust. Alone, it is an empty chair.

Leadership on a volunteer team is **influence** — the ability to help a group
move toward a shared goal because people choose to follow you. Nobody here is
paid. A sophomore who is tired, overcommitted, or quietly angry can stop
showing up on Thursday and there is no HR conversation. Titles are weak.
Influence is everything.

James C. Hunter, in *The Servant*, defines leadership as the skill of
influencing people to work enthusiastically toward goals identified as being
for the common good. He draws a line that is easy to mix up with this ticket's
vocabulary. Hunter calls coercion **power** and earned trust **authority**.
This curriculum uses everyday shop language instead: **authority** means the
title; **influence** means the trust. Same idea, different labels. Write your
notes in *this* curriculum's words so later tickets stay consistent.

**Servant leadership** flips the org chart. Your job is not to look like a
lead. Your job is to make the team successful: remove roadblocks, get people
the parts and the context they need, give credit away, and take responsibility
when something slips. That is also how FIRST asks you to carry yourself.
[Gracious Professionalism](https://www.firstinspires.org/about) is not a
poster. It is high-quality work that still treats people as people — fierce
about the robot, kind about the human.

Picture a 20-person volunteer team in week four of build season. The captain
has the title and the loudest voice at kickoff. The person teammates actually
ask before they cut tube is the junior who stays late to label drawers, who
admits when a CAD dimension is wrong, and who never mocks a freshman for a
"dumb" question. That junior has no paycheck and no officer role. They have
influence. You are going to learn to see that difference, then practice one
small act of leading by example this week.

This is ticket 1 of 12. You will keep a **Leadership notes** document you own
— a Google Doc, a paper notebook, or a private markdown file. Later tickets
add dated entries to the same document: a RACI, a standup agenda you actually
used, a feedback conversation you practiced, a conflict write-up. This website
does not store that work. Mentors read it with you, not through a login.

This site does not track whether you finished. If your team exported these
tickets into its own GitHub, close this issue there once a mentor accepts the
criteria below.

## Prerequisites

- None. This is the starting point for the leadership track. You do not need a
  title. You need a team you actually work with, even as a new member.

## What you'll learn

- The difference between authority (a title) and influence (trust), in shop
  language you can say out loud.
- What servant leadership and leading by example look like on a volunteer FRC
  team where nobody can be ordered to care.
- How FIRST's vision and Gracious Professionalism set the standard for *how*
  you lead, not only *what* you ship.
- How to start a Leadership notes document that the rest of this track will
  keep using.

## Tasks

1. **Start your Leadership notes.** Create a document you own and will still
   have in May. Title it something like `Leadership notes — Your Name`. Put
   it where a mentor can sit next to you and read it (shared doc, printed
   pages, or a laptop file). Do **not** paste it into this website. At the
   top, write:

   - your name and the name teammates should use
   - your graduation year
   - the group you actually work with (intake, electrical, outreach, drive
     team, a build table — pick one real group)
   - one sentence: what you think "leadership" means today, before you do
     the reading

   You will reopen this file in every later ticket.

2. **Read a short piece of servant leadership.** If you have access to
   James C. Hunter's *The Servant* (team bookshelf, library, or a borrowed
   copy), read the opening chapters through the first full discussion of
   leadership as influence and service — typically the first sitting, not
   the whole book. If you do not have the book, read Robert K. Greenleaf's
   overview of the idea at the
   [Greenleaf Center: What is Servant Leadership?](https://www.greenleaf.org/what-is-servant-leadership/)
   **and** a short take on Hunter's parable, such as
   [The Servant — five lessons and takeaways](https://www.15minutebusinessbooks.com/blog/2017/03/24/the-servant-by-james-c-hunter-my-five-lessons-and-takeaways/).
   You are not writing a book report. You need the argument: leaders serve,
   influence is earned, character is a set of chosen behaviors.

3. **Read FIRST's own words on how we treat people.** Read
   [About FIRST — purpose, vision, mission, and Gracious Professionalism](https://www.firstinspires.org/about)
   and the
   [FIRST help article on purpose, mission, and vision](https://help.firstinspires.org/s/article/What-are-the-Purpose-Mission-and-Vision-of-FIRST).
   Optionally skim the
   [Gracious Professionalism explainer](https://ftc-docs.firstinspires.org/en/latest/gracious_professionalism/gp.html)
   (the ethos is the same across FIRST programs). Copy, in your notes, one
   sentence from FIRST that you could use when a teammate is "winning" an
   argument by making someone else small.

4. **Write the ticket 1 reflection** in your Leadership notes (about a page,
   dated). Answer all of the following in your own words — no quoting this
   page back:

   - **Authority vs. influence.** Give one example from *your* team of a
     title that did not move people, and one example of someone who leads
     without a title. Name the behaviors, not the vibe.
   - **Servant leadership.** Name one roadblock you could remove this week
     for the group you wrote at the top (a missing fastener bin, an unclear
     owner, a freshman who does not know where to stand). Say what you
     will actually do, not "be more helpful."
   - **Leading by example.** Name two concrete behaviors you will practice
     in the next seven days (on time, cleanup, admitting a mistake, staying
     calm when a match goes badly). They must be visible to someone else.
   - **Four traits.** Competence, consistency, care, composure. Which is
     currently strongest? Which will you grow? Give one shop example for
     each answer.
   - **Hunter / Greenleaf in one paragraph.** What did the reading change
     about your sentence from Task 1?

5. **Do one unglamorous thing on purpose.** Before you talk to a mentor,
   actually do the roadblock-removal or example behavior from Task 4 at a
   real meeting or work session. It can be small. It cannot be theoretical.
   In your notes, write four sentences: what you did, who saw it, what
   happened, what you would do differently.

6. **Mentor conversation.** Sit down with a mentor or a veteran student
   leader for ten minutes. Bring the notes, not a speech. Ask them:

   - Who on this team leads without a title, and how can they tell?
   - Where has a title without trust hurt this team?
   - What does "serve the team" look like *here* in the next month?

   Afterward, add a short paragraph to the notes: what you heard, and one
   thing you will try because of that conversation. If your team exported
   these tickets, paste a one-line status ("notes written, mentor chat
   done") on the issue and move it to In Review. Do not upload the notes
   to this website.

## Acceptance Criteria

- [ ] A Leadership notes document exists that you own. A mentor has seen
      it in person or on your screen. This website does not have a copy.
- [ ] The notes include your name, graduation year, the group you work
      with, and a before-and-after sentence on what leadership is.
- [ ] You read either Hunter's opening of *The Servant* or the Greenleaf
      overview plus a Hunter summary, and you can say in two sentences
      what servant leadership is without reading this page.
- [ ] You read FIRST's vision/mission and Gracious Professionalism pages
      and copied one usable sentence into the notes.
- [ ] The dated ticket 1 reflection covers authority vs. influence with
      real team examples, one servant-leadership action, two lead-by-example
      behaviors, and a strongest / grow trait.
- [ ] You performed one unglamorous or roadblock-removing action at a
      real work session and wrote what happened.
- [ ] You had a mentor (or veteran leader) conversation about titleless
      leadership on this team and logged what you heard.

## Notes

- A title is not a personality transplant. If you were just named a lead,
  this ticket is still the starting point. People will watch what you do
  in the next two weeks more than what was announced.
- Do not put other students' private criticism, phone numbers, or a
  "who is a bad leader" dossier in the notes. Describe behaviors. Mentors
  may read this with you.
- Hunter's power/authority wording and this ticket's authority/influence
  wording are easy to mash together in a reflection. Pick this ticket's
  words and stick to them.
- Leading by example is not "I will have a better attitude." It is "I will
  be in the shop at 6:00 with the cleanup list in my hand."
- The next ticket, [Communication Fundamentals](../communication-fundamentals/),
  is where influence becomes a conversation: listening first, then saying
  the point clearly. Bring the same notes document.
